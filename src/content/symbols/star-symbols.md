## Text stars vs. star emojis

There are two kinds of star on this page, and they behave differently once you paste them.

Text stars such as ★ ☆ ✦ ✧ ✩ are ordinary characters, the same as a letter or a comma. They pick up the font, size and color of the text around them. In a document or on a website you can make ★ gold, gray or red, and it will line up neatly with the words next to it. That is why rating widgets, résumés and clean bio designs usually use text stars.

Star emojis such as ⭐ 🌟 ✨ are pictures. Each phone and computer maker draws them in its own style, and they keep their own colors no matter how the surrounding text is styled. They stand out more in a chat or caption, and they read as friendlier and more casual.

A few characters sit in between. ✳ ✴ and ❇ exist as plain text, but Apple devices and some apps show them as colored emojis by default. If you need the plain version, test it on the device where it will be seen.

Here is what the most common stars are usually used for:

| Symbol | Kind | Usually used for |
|---|---|---|
| ★ | Text, filled | Ratings, highlights, bullet points, bios |
| ☆ | Text, outline | Empty rating slots, softer decoration, cute combos like ☆彡 |
| ⭐ | Emoji | Praise ("you're a star"), favorites, good work. See [the star emoji](/emojis/star/) |
| 🌟 | Emoji | Something extra special or a standout moment. See [glowing star](/emojis/glowing-star/) |
| ✨ | Emoji | Sparkle, excitement, "new," and sarcastic emphasis around words. See [sparkles](/emojis/sparkles/) |
| 💫 | Emoji | Dizziness or feeling starstruck, and dreamy decoration. See [dizzy](/emojis/dizzy/) |
| 🌠 | Emoji | Wishes, night skies, a rare moment. See [shooting star](/emojis/shooting-star/) |

The ✨ sparkles emoji has a second life as tone. Wrapping words in it, as in ✨self care✨, can be sincere or ironic depending on context. For more on what individual emojis signal, see [what does this emoji mean](/what-does-this-emoji-mean/).

Two star emojis carry religious meaning. ✡️ is the [Star of David](/emojis/star-of-david/), a symbol of Judaism and the Jewish people. ☪️ is the [star and crescent](/emojis/star-and-crescent/), widely associated with Islam. They are not general decoration, so use them when you mean them.

## Writing a star rating in plain text

A five-star rating is the most practical use of star symbols. The pattern is filled stars for the score and outline stars for the rest:

- ★★★★★ for five out of five
- ★★★★☆ for four out of five
- ★★★☆☆ for three out of five

Keep both stars from the same family. ★ and ☆ are designed as a pair, so they are the same width in most fonts and the row stays even. Mixing ★ with ✩ or ⭐ with ☆ tends to look uneven.

Half stars are the awkward part. There is no half-star character that shows up reliably on phones and computers today, so plain text cannot draw 4.5 stars cleanly. The usual workarounds are to round to the nearest whole star or to add the number after the row: ★★★★☆ 4.5/5. Review sites that show half stars use images or styling rather than a character.

Emoji ratings like ⭐⭐⭐⭐ also work in chats and comments, but there is no empty emoji star to pair with ⭐, so an emoji rating only shows the score, not the scale. Writing "4/5" next to it removes any doubt.

## Stars in bios, usernames and captions

Small stars and sparkles are one of the easiest ways to decorate a profile without making it hard to read. A few patterns that work well:

- Frame a name: ✦ Maya ✦ or ☆ Leo ☆
- Separate facts in a bio: designer ⋆ runner ⋆ dog person
- Mark a line break or section: ──── ✦ ────
- Add a night-sky mood: ☾ ⋆*･ﾟ or 🌙✨

Use one or two styles per profile. A bio with ★, ✧, ⊹ and ✨ all at once looks busy, while a single repeated symbol looks deliberate. The [cute symbols](/cute-symbols/) page has matching flowers, bows and brackets, and [heart symbols](/heart-symbols/) pairs well with stars for soft aesthetic bios.

Platforms differ in where they accept symbols. Display names on most social apps take them, but the @handle or account name is often restricted to letters, numbers, periods and underscores. Online games commonly filter special characters from player names, and some school or work systems strip them out. If a field rejects a star, that field does not allow it; trying a different star will usually not help.

## How to type a star without copy and paste

Copying from this page is the quickest route, but you can also type stars directly.

**Windows.** Press the Windows key and the period key together to open the emoji panel. Type "star" to find ⭐ 🌟 and the other star emojis. The panel also has a symbols section for text characters, where you can browse for ★ and ☆.

**Mac.** Press Control + Command + Space to open the Character Viewer, then type "star" in the search field. It lists both text stars and emoji stars. If you only see emojis, expand the viewer to the full window with the button in its top-right corner, which shows the complete symbol categories. The [Mac emoji guide](/guides/how-to-get-emojis-on-mac/) covers the viewer in more detail.

**iPhone.** The standard keyboard has no ★ key, but you can make your own shortcut. Go to Settings, General, Keyboard, Text Replacement, tap +, paste ★ as the phrase, and set a shortcut such as "bstar". Typing the shortcut then offers the star. Star emojis are in the emoji keyboard; search for "star".

**Android.** Gboard and most other keyboards let you search the emoji panel for "star." For text stars, Gboard's personal dictionary can store a shortcut that expands to ★ or ☆, the same way text replacement works on iPhone.

For emoji shortcuts on every platform, including Chromebook and Linux, see [how to type emojis on any device](/guides/how-to-type-emojis-on-any-device/).

## When stars do not display

Every symbol needs a font on the reader's device that includes it. ★ and ☆ are in nearly every system font, so they are the safest choice. Less common variants such as ⚝, ⭑ and ⭒ are more likely to appear as an empty box or a question mark on older phones, in some apps, and in certain games. Small characters like ⊹ and ‧ can also look different from one font to the next, sometimes larger or lower than expected.

If something you pasted shows up as a box, the character did not travel with a font that can draw it. Swap it for ★, ☆, ✦ or ✧. The guide on [why emojis show as boxes](/guides/why-emojis-show-as-boxes/) explains the same problem for emojis.

The complete list of Unicode characters, including every star, is published in the [Unicode code charts](https://www.unicode.org/charts/).

## Accessibility: how stars sound to screen readers

Screen readers announce symbols by name. ★ is typically read as "black star" and ☆ as "white star," because those are the official Unicode names for the filled and outline versions. Emojis are read by their emoji names, such as "glowing star" or "sparkles."

That makes decoration and ratings sound very different from how they look:

- ★★★★☆ is read as four "black star" announcements followed by "white star," which a listener has to count.
- ✦ Maya ✦ becomes something like "black four pointed star Maya black four pointed star."
- A long divider like ⋆⁺₊⋆ ☾⋆⁺₊⋆ turns into a string of names that has nothing to do with the content.

A few habits keep pages and posts usable:

- Always write a rating in words as well: ★★★★☆ 4 out of 5.
- Keep decorative stars at the start or end of a name, not between every letter.
- Use a short divider, or none, in places where people need to read the text, such as captions with important information.
