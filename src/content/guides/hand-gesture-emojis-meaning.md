Hand gesture emojis mostly mean what the physical gesture means: 👍 is approval, 🙏 is prayer or thanks, 🤞 is hoping for luck, 🤟 is "I love you," 🤙 is "call me" or "hang loose," 🫶 is love and support, and 🤌 is the Italian "what do you want?" The complications come from look-alike pairs, from second meanings that grew up online, and from gestures that are friendly in one country and offensive in another.

This guide decodes the common ones, shows how to separate the similar ones, and flags the gestures to handle with care.

## Approval and thanks: 👍 🙏 👏 🙌 🤝

### 👍 Thumbs up

The [thumbs up](/emojis/thumbs-up/) is the fastest way to say yes. It answers logistics perfectly: "Meet at 6?" "👍." It is also a standard reaction in messaging apps and workplace tools, where it means "seen and agreed."

It causes friction in two situations. Alone, in reply to a long or emotional message, it can feel like the conversation is being closed without a real answer; that reading is more common among younger users and is explored in [passive-aggressive emojis](/guides/passive-aggressive-emojis/). And in a negotiation, it may count for more than you intended: in 2023 a Canadian court in Saskatchewan held that a farmer's 👍 in reply to a photographed contract amounted to accepting it. That was one case decided on its particular facts, not a general rule, but it is a good reminder that a thumbs up says "I agree."

Its opposite, thumbs down 👎, is blunt. It is fine for voting on options and harsh as a reply to someone's idea.

### 🙏 Folded hands

[Folded hands](/emojis/folded-hands/) shows two palms pressed together. It originated in Japanese emoji sets, where the gesture goes with please, thank you, and apology. English speakers adopted it mainly for prayer and gratitude, and for earnest requests:

- "Thank you so much 🙏"
- "Praying for your dad 🙏"
- "Can you cover my shift Friday? 🙏"

A persistent myth says it is two people high-fiving. It is not: the thumbs sit on the same side, the sleeves on some designs match, and the Unicode name is plain. If you want a high five, 🙌 is the usual stand-in, and the newer leftwards and rightwards pushing hands 🫸🫷 can be placed together to make one.

### 👏 🙌 🤝

[Clapping hands](/emojis/clapping-hands/) 👏 is applause: "Great presentation 👏👏." Placed between every word, as in "read 👏 the 👏 instructions," it becomes an emphatic, scolding beat, a style that started on social media and can read as aggressive.

[Raising hands](/emojis/raising-hands/) 🙌 shows two hands in the air: celebration, relief, "yes!" and in religious contexts praise. It is one of the safest positive emojis.

The [handshake](/emojis/handshake/) 🤝 means agreement or a deal. Online it also labels two unlikely parties sharing something, in the meme format "Me 🤝 my cat: refusing to get up before nine."

## Greetings and attention: 👋 ✋ and the pointing hands

The [waving hand](/emojis/waving-hand/) 👋 is hello or goodbye. Tone depends on placement. "Hey 👋" is friendly. "Okay, bye 👋" after an argument is a door closing. Users of Chinese messaging apps often describe the smiling wave as a cold or sarcastic farewell, so take care with it there.

The raised hand ✋ is "stop," "wait," or, in class and meetings, "I have a question." For volunteering, the person raising hand 🙋 is clearer.

The pointing hands direct attention:

- 👉 [Backhand index pointing right](/emojis/backhand-index-pointing-right/) and 👈 pointing left highlight a link or a name. Together, as 👉👈, the two fingertips touching, they mean shyness or nervously asking for something: "Would you maybe want to get coffee 👉👈."
- 👆 points to the message above, meaning "this" or "what they said." 👇 points down to a link or thread below.
- ☝️ Index pointing up is "one thing," "wait," or a reference to God.
- 🫵 Index pointing at the viewer is "you," as in recruiting posters, and can feel accusatory.

## Luck, peace, and OK: 🤞 ✌️ 👌 🤏

[Crossed fingers](/emojis/crossed-fingers/) 🤞 means hoping for a good outcome: "Interview at 10 🤞." It can also evoke the childhood idea of crossing your fingers behind your back to cancel a promise, so "I'll be on time 🤞" is ambiguous between "I hope so" and "don't count on it."

The [victory hand](/emojis/victory-hand/) ✌️ is the peace sign. It works as a breezy sign-off ("Later ✌️"), as the number two, and as a pose in photos. The emoji shows the palm facing outward, which matters: the same two fingers with the back of the hand toward the viewer are an insult in the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

The [OK hand](/emojis/ok-hand/) 👌 means "fine," "perfect," or "delicious." It also needs care. In Brazil and Turkey the gesture is obscene. In the United States, it was appropriated by some white supremacists in the late 2010s, and in 2019 the Anti-Defamation League added it to its database of hate symbols while stressing that the overwhelming majority of uses are the ordinary, innocent one. In normal conversation, 👌 after "dinner was amazing" will be read exactly as intended.

The [pinching hand](/emojis/pinching-hand/) 🤏, thumb and index finger nearly touching, means "a little bit" or "this close": "I was 🤏 this close to missing the flight." It is also used for jokes about size, including crude ones, so think about the sentence you attach it to.

## Love and friendship: 🤟 🫶 🫰 🤲

The [love-you gesture](/emojis/love-you-gesture/) 🤟 extends the thumb, index finger, and pinky. It is the American Sign Language sign for "I love you," which combines the handshapes for the letters I, L, and Y. It was added to the emoji set in 2017. Use it as an affectionate sign-off to family and friends.

[Heart hands](/emojis/heart-hands/) 🫶, added in 2022, shows two hands forming a heart. It is the gesture performers make toward a crowd, and it carries that feeling: appreciation, support, "love you guys." Among friends it is warm without being romantic. "Thank you for last night 🫶" to a friend who helped you move is exactly right.

The hand with index finger and thumb crossed 🫰 is the finger heart popularized by South Korean pop culture, where the crossed tips form a tiny heart. The same motion is the "money" rub in many countries, and some people use it for a finger snap.

Palms up together 🤲 shows cupped, open hands. It is associated with prayer, particularly in Islam, and is also used for offering, receiving, or asking gently.

For how these compare with the hearts themselves, see [heart emoji colors and their meanings](/guides/heart-emoji-colors-meaning/).

## Attitude and energy: 🤘 🤙 🤌 👊 ✊ 💪 💅

[Sign of the horns](/emojis/sign-of-the-horns/) 🤘 has the index finger and pinky up with the thumb holding down the two middle fingers. It is the rock and heavy metal salute and a general "let's go." It is the emoji most often confused with 🤟; the test is whether the thumb sticks out.

The [call me hand](/emojis/call-me-hand/) 🤙 has two lives. With the hand read as a telephone, it means "call me." With the hand read as the shaka, a gesture rooted in Hawaii and spread worldwide by surfing, it means "hang loose," "all good," or "thanks." "Landed safe 🤙" is the relaxed version; "🤙 when you're free" is the phone version.

[Pinched fingers](/emojis/pinched-fingers/) 🤌 arrived in 2020. In Italy the gesture, usually with a small shake of the wrist, asks "What do you want?" or "What on earth are you saying?" English-speaking internet users took it up for mock exasperation and Italian-themed jokes, and it merged with the "chef's kiss" idea of something being exquisite: "That bassline 🤌." In Israel and in parts of the Arab world a similar gesture means "wait" or "be patient."

The [oncoming fist](/emojis/oncoming-fist/) 👊 is a fist bump among friends, "nice one" or "respect," and occasionally a punch. The left- and right-facing fists 🤜🤛 together make the bump explicit.

The [raised fist](/emojis/raised-fist/) ✊ signals solidarity and resistance. It has a long history in labor, civil rights, and other political movements, and with a dark skin tone it is closely tied to Black Lives Matter. It is a statement, not a casual "yeah!"

The flexed biceps 💪 means strength: gym progress, encouragement ("You've got this 💪"), or pride in an effort.

Nail polish 💅 is technically an object, but it works as a gesture: unbothered confidence, sass, "anyway, I look great."

Two more that people look for here: the writing hand ✍️ means "taking notes," often as a joke after good advice, and the saluting face 🫡, a face with a hand at its brow, means "yes, boss," sincerely or with irony.

## Look-alike gestures and how to tell them apart

| Pair | The difference |
|---|---|
| 🤟 vs 🤘 | Thumb out is "I love you"; thumb tucked is "rock on" |
| 🙏 vs 🙌 | Palms together is prayer or thanks; two hands up is celebration |
| 🙏 vs 🤲 | Pressed palms versus open, cupped palms |
| 👌 vs 🤌 | A ring of thumb and index versus all fingertips bunched |
| 👌 vs 🤏 | A closed ring versus a small gap for "tiny" |
| 🤞 vs 🫰 | Index and middle fingers crossed versus thumb and index crossed |
| ✋ vs 👋 | Still palm for "stop" versus motion lines for a wave |
| 👊 vs ✊ | Knuckles toward you for a bump versus fist held up for solidarity |
| 🤙 vs 🤟 | Thumb and pinky only versus thumb, index, and pinky |

The artwork also differs by vendor, with hands drawn at different angles on Apple, Google, Samsung, and Microsoft devices, which makes small distinctions like thumb position harder to see at text size. If your message depends on one of these distinctions, add a word.

## Gestures that offend, and where

Hand gestures are among the least universal signals people have. The table lists the cases most often reported. Customs vary within countries and are changing as global media spreads the American meanings, so treat these as cautions, not certainties.

| Emoji | Where to be careful | How it can be read there |
|---|---|---|
| 🖕 | Almost everywhere | Obscene insult |
| 👌 | Brazil, Turkey | Obscene gesture |
| 🤘 | Italy, Spain, Portugal, Brazil, parts of Latin America | "Your partner is unfaithful" |
| ✋ | Greece | The moutza, a traditional insult made by thrusting an open palm at someone |
| 👍 | Parts of the Middle East and West Africa | Traditionally a rude gesture |
| 🤞 | Vietnam | Considered obscene |
| ✌️ reversed | UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand | Insult (the emoji itself shows the polite, palm-out form) |
| 🤏 | South Korea | Has been at the center of public controversy as a perceived mocking gesture |

The [middle finger](/emojis/middle-finger/) 🖕 needs no translation. Between close friends it can be a joke; anywhere else it is as rude as the real thing, and in a workplace it can be a disciplinary matter.

With international colleagues, clients, or new acquaintances, the lowest-risk positive signals are 🙂, 😊, and plain words. A deeper country-by-country treatment is in [emojis across cultures](/guides/emojis-across-cultures/).

## Skin tones and hand emojis

Hand emojis appear in a default yellow and in five skin tones based on the Fitzpatrick scale. On most keyboards you press and hold a hand to choose a tone, and the keyboard remembers the choice for that emoji. People generally pick the tone closest to their own or stay with yellow, which is meant to be neutral.

A technical note: for years the handshake could not take skin tones at all, because it shows two hands. Support for separate tones on each hand came later, so mixed-tone handshakes may not display on old devices. The details, along with common etiquette questions, are in [emoji skin tones explained](/guides/emoji-skin-tones-explained/).

## Hand emojis at work

- **Good defaults:** 👍 for acknowledgment, 🙏 for thanks, 👏 and 🙌 for praise, 🤝 for agreement, 👀 for "looking into it."
- **Use with thought:** 🤞 when someone wants a commitment, 👌 with international teams, 💪 when the problem is serious.
- **Leave out:** 🖕 in any form, 🤏 jokes, and the clap between words.
- **As reactions:** a 👍 reaction on a message is lighter than a 👍 reply. It confirms you saw it without ending the thread.

More on tone in professional settings is in [emojis at work etiquette](/guides/emojis-at-work-etiquette/).

## A rule of thumb for choosing a gesture

Before sending a hand emoji, check three things.

1. **Would I make this gesture to this person's face?** If the physical gesture would be odd or rude, the emoji will be too.
2. **Does it answer the message, or just end it?** A lone 👍 is right for "see you at 6" and thin for three paragraphs about someone's bad week.
3. **Does the reader share my conventions?** For anyone abroad or unknown, prefer 🙏, 👏, or 🙌, which travel better than 👌 or 🤘, or simply use words.
