Gen Z, roughly people born from the late 1990s to the early 2010s, uses emojis less as pictures of feelings and more as punchlines. The skull means laughing, the crying face means laughing harder, the plain smile often means the opposite of a smile, and a stone statue means "no comment." If you read these emojis literally, most messages from a younger person will seem alarming or baffling.

This guide decodes the main ones, explains where they came from, and is honest about which are still current and which have already faded.

## Why the meanings flipped

Three habits explain almost all of it.

**Irony is the default register.** Much Gen Z humor is deadpan, self-deprecating and exaggerated. A sincere yellow smiley fits that humor poorly. An emoji that overshoots (a skull for a mild joke) or undershoots (a stone face for chaos) fits it well.

**Comment sections are the training ground.** Earlier emoji habits formed in one-to-one texting. Gen Z habits formed in public comments on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Discord, where short replies compete for likes and in-jokes spread to millions of people within days. The guide to [TikTok emoji meanings](/guides/tiktok-emoji-meanings/) covers that environment specifically.

**Using the "wrong" emoji marks you as in on the joke.** When everyone's parents adopted 😂, moving to something else became a quiet signal of belonging. This is ordinary slang behavior. Every generation does it with words; this one also does it with pictographs.

One more piece of context: a large share of what gets called "Gen Z slang" originated in African American English and in Black and LGBTQ online communities, then spread through social media. "Cap," "slay," "tea" and "periodt" all followed that path, and so did the emojis attached to them.

## The laughing set: 💀, 😭 and what happened to 😂

The biggest shift is how laughter gets expressed.

- **💀 [Skull](/emojis/skull/).** "I'm dead." The standard reaction to something funny. Repeating it (💀💀💀) means it was funnier.
- **☠️ Skull and crossbones.** Used the same way, sometimes for extra drama.
- **😭 [Loudly crying face](/emojis/loudly-crying-face/).** Laughing to the point of tears, or overwhelmed by any feeling at all: "he waved at me 😭."
- **😂 [Face with tears of joy](/emojis/face-with-tears-of-joy/).** Still used by people of every age, but many younger users consider it dated or reserve it for ironic use.
- **😹 Cat with tears of joy.** Laughing at someone in a mocking, taunting way.
- **🫵😂 Pointing and laughing.** The index pointing at the viewer plus a laugh means "laughing at you specifically."

A typical exchange: one person sends a video of a failed backflip, and the reply is "the way he just laid there 💀😭." Nobody is dead and nobody is sad. For a full comparison of the laugh emojis across generations, see [laughing and crying emojis](/guides/laughing-and-crying-emojis/).

## Smileys used sarcastically

Several friendly faces have been repurposed to mean something close to the reverse.

### 🙂 and 😃

The [slightly smiling face](/emojis/slightly-smiling-face/) is the face of someone holding it together. "My flight got canceled 🙂" is barely contained frustration. The wide-eyed grinning face with big eyes does similar work for stunned disbelief: "so the exam is today 😃." The guide to [passive-aggressive emojis](/guides/passive-aggressive-emojis/) explains why these read as cold.

### 🤡 Clown face

The [clown face](/emojis/clown-face/) calls someone a fool. Very often that someone is the sender: "thought he'd text back 🤡" means "I was the clown for believing it." Aimed at another person, it is an insult to their opinion or behavior.

### 🤓 Nerd face

The [nerd face](/emojis/nerd-face/) mocks a pedantic correction, frequently paired with ☝️ and the phrase "um, actually." Posting it under someone's comment says "nobody asked for this lecture."

### 🤨 Face with raised eyebrow

Suspicion or disbelief. The raised eyebrow reacts to anything questionable or "sus," and to comments that sound unintentionally suggestive.

### 🗿 Moai

The [moai](/emojis/moai/) is the ultimate deadpan. It answers something awkward or absurd with a stone face, in place of "bruh." It also appears in jokes about being stoic and unbothered.

### 🧍 Person standing

A stiff figure with arms at their sides, used for awkwardness: standing there with nothing to say. "She said 'you too' to the waiter and then just 🧍."

## Emojis that replace a slang word

These work like a rebus. Each stands for a specific word.

| Emoji | Stands for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 🧢 | Cap: a lie | "You ran a 5-minute mile? 🧢" |
| 🐐 | GOAT: greatest of all time | "She's the 🐐 of this league" |
| 🔥 | Fire: excellent, attractive | "New album is 🔥" |
| 💅 | Slay; unbothered confidence | "Got the job and the apartment 💅" |
| ☕ or 🐸☕ | Tea: gossip | "Spill the ☕" |
| 🚩 | Red flag: warning sign | "He doesn't like dogs 🚩" |
| 💯 | Keep it 100: total agreement | "Facts 💯" |
| 👀 | Watching; tell me more | "Wait, who said that 👀" |
| 🤝 | Agreement; shared experience | "Me 🤝 you: ignoring the group project" |
| ✍️ | Taking notes | "Lower rent trick? ✍️✍️" |

A few notes. The goat is always praise, never an insult. The [nail polish](/emojis/nail-polish/) emoji conveys poise and a touch of smugness, and comes from drag and LGBTQ slang. The 🚩 emoji, officially the [triangular flag](/emojis/triangular-flag/), became the standard "red flag" after a wave of posts in late 2021 that listed dating warning signs followed by rows of flags. The frog-and-tea pairing refers to a Kermit meme captioned "but that's none of my business," and now marks gossip or a pointed observation. The fire and hundred points emojis are older than Gen Z and have crossed into general use.

Sparkles deserve a separate mention. Wrapping a word in [sparkles](/emojis/sparkles/) adds ironic emphasis: "I am ✨struggling✨" presents something unpleasant as though it were glamorous.

## Meme-era emojis and where they came from

Some emojis became slang overnight because of a single trend. These date quickly, and several are now used mostly as nostalgia.

**🪑 The chair.** In 2021, TikTok commenters started posting rows of chairs where a laughing emoji would normally go. It began as a deliberate in-joke: a replacement laugh that outsiders would not understand. Once everyone understood it, the joke was over, and "chair era" is now shorthand for that period of comment-section humor.

**👁👄👁 Eye, mouth, eye.** An eye, a mouth and another eye form a blank, staring face. It spread in 2020 as a reaction meaning "I am witnessing this and have no words," often with the caption "it is what it is."

**🗣️🔥 Speaking head and fire.** The [speaking head](/emojis/speaking-head/) followed by flames hypes up a statement: "say it louder." It is used sincerely for a line someone agrees with and ironically for nonsense, with a growing number of fire emojis.

**🤫🧏 Shush and point.** A shushing face followed by a person pointing toward their jaw refers to "mewing," a tongue-posture trend claimed to sharpen the jawline. The joke is "I can't answer, I'm mewing." It peaked around 2024 and is used almost entirely as a gag.

**🥀💔 Wilted flower and broken heart.** The [wilted flower](/emojis/wilted-flower/) and broken heart perform theatrical sadness over minor problems. This pair became widespread in 2025.

**😭🙏 Crying and folded hands.** "Please, I'm begging" or "I can't with this." It is one of the most common reaction pairs in comments, and it softens a blunt statement into a joke.

Brand-new emojis get absorbed quickly too. The [melting face](/emojis/melting-face/) and saluting face, both from Emoji 14.0, became slang almost as soon as they reached phones, and the face with bags under eyes from Emoji 16.0 was adopted right away for exhaustion. More combinations are collected in the [emoji combos guide](/guides/emoji-combos-ideas/).

## Sincere emojis: affection, embarrassment and burnout

Not everything is ironic. A set of emojis handles real feelings, usually with a layer of self-awareness.

- **🥺 [Pleading face](/emojis/pleading-face/).** Big wet eyes: begging, being touched, or acting cute to get something.
- **👉👈 Pointing fingers together.** Shyness, fidgeting before asking a favor. Often follows 🥺.
- **🥹 [Face holding back tears](/emojis/face-holding-back-tears/).** Sincerely moved, proud, or grateful.
- **🫶 Heart hands.** Warm appreciation between friends; the default friendly heart.
- **🫠 Melting face.** Embarrassment, heat, or slowly dissolving under stress while staying polite.
- **🫡 Saluting face.** "Yes, chief." Dutiful acceptance of a task, sometimes grim: "working the double shift 🫡."
- **😮‍💨 Face exhaling.** Relief, exhaustion, or a sigh.
- **🫥 Dotted line face.** Feeling invisible or wanting to disappear.

Hearts have their own conventions by color, covered in [heart emoji colors](/guides/heart-emoji-colors-meaning/).

## How to read a message full of slang emojis

When you get a text or comment you cannot parse, a short routine works.

1. **Assume humor first.** If the emoji is a skull, coffin, crying face or clown, the default is a joke.
2. **Find the emotion in the words.** The emoji sets the intensity. The sentence tells you whether it is delight, embarrassment or annoyance.
3. **Check for a rebus.** If an object emoji makes no sense, ask whether it stands for a word: cap, goat, tea, flag.
4. **Look at who is being mocked.** Clown and nerd emojis are often self-directed. "Me thinking I'd finish early 🤡" is not an attack on you.
5. **Take real distress seriously.** Irony drops away when someone is upset. Plain sentences, no emojis, or a direct statement like "I'm not joking" mean exactly what they say.

That last point matters for parents and teachers. A teenager who writes "this homework is killing me 💀" is fine. A message without the comic framing deserves a direct, calm question.

## Using Gen Z emojis without overdoing it

If you are outside the age group, or new to a particular online community, a few rules keep you from sounding like an advertisement.

- **Borrow the widespread ones only.** 💀, 😭, 🔥, 👀 and 🫶 have crossed into general use. Niche meme emojis have not.
- **Use one, not six.** Slang stacked on slang reads as a costume.
- **Skip trends you had to look up.** By the time a meme emoji has been explained in an article, including this one, its peak has usually passed.
- **Keep work messages literal.** A skull in a client thread can be misread badly. See [emojis at work](/guides/emojis-at-work-etiquette/).
- **Respect the source.** Many of these terms come from specific communities. Using them is fine; using them to mock how people talk is not.
- **Do not police other people.** Someone who sends 😂 sincerely is communicating clearly. Nobody needs correcting.

## Quick decoder

| If you see | It most likely means | It probably does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| 💀 | That was hilarious | Death, a threat |
| 😭 | Laughing, overwhelmed, touched | Real crying |
| 🙂 | Annoyed but holding it in | Simple happiness |
| 🤡 | I was (or you are) a fool | Circus, fun |
| 🗿 | Deadpan, no reaction | Statues, travel |
| 🧢 | That's a lie | A hat |
| 🐐 | The greatest | Livestock, an insult |
| 🥀💔 | Mock tragedy | A real breakup (check the words) |
| 🫡 | On it, reluctantly | Military service |
| 👀 | Interested, watching | Literally looking |

The rule underneath all of it: for Gen Z, emojis are tone and comedy before they are pictures. Read the sentence for the content, read the emoji for the attitude, and if a symbol seems to make no sense, it is probably standing in for a slang word or a meme.
