Every current operating system has a built-in emoji picker, and each one opens with a shortcut: **Windows key + period** on Windows, **Control + Command + Space** or the **Globe key** on a Mac, the **smiley key** on iPhone and Android keyboards, and **Search + Shift + Space** on a Chromebook. Once the picker is open, type a word such as "fire" to search rather than scrolling.

The rest of this guide gives the exact steps for each device, the search and skin-tone tricks that save the most time, and the methods that work when no picker is available.

## Quick reference: emoji shortcuts by device

| Device | Open the picker | Search | Skin tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Windows 10 and 11 | Windows key + . (or ;) | Just start typing | Tone selector in the people tab |
| Mac | Control + Command + Space, or Globe key | Just start typing | Click and hold the emoji |
| iPhone and iPad | Smiley or globe key, bottom left | "Search Emoji" field | Touch and hold the emoji |
| Android (Gboard) | Smiley key beside the space bar | Search bar above the grid | Touch and hold the emoji |
| Samsung Keyboard | Smiley icon in the toolbar | Magnifier icon | Touch and hold the emoji |
| Chromebook | Search/Launcher + Shift + Space | Search field at the top | Click and hold or arrow on the emoji |
| Linux (GNOME) | Control + . or Control + ; in most apps | Just start typing | Right-click or long-press the emoji |
| Linux (KDE Plasma) | Meta (Super) + . | Just start typing | Varies by version |

Details and exceptions for each are below.

## Windows 10 and Windows 11

Click into any text field, then press **Windows key + period (.)**. **Windows key + semicolon (;)** does the same thing. The emoji panel opens next to the cursor.

1. Press Windows + period.
2. Start typing a description, such as "laugh" or "thumbs". The panel filters as you type, and the search text is not inserted into your document.
3. Use the arrow keys to move and Enter to insert, or click an emoji.
4. Press Escape to close the panel.

On Windows 11 the panel has tabs along the top for emoji, GIFs, kaomoji (text faces like `¯\_(ツ)_/¯`), symbols and clipboard history. Windows 10 has emoji, kaomoji and symbols. The symbols tab is the quickest built-in way to reach characters such as the degree sign, arrows and currency symbols.

For skin tones, open the people category and use the tone selector shown there. Your choice applies to the emojis that support tones until you change it.

Things to know on Windows:

- **Flags show as letters.** Microsoft's emoji font does not include country flags, so a flag such as the [United States flag](/emojis/flag-united-states/) appears as the two letters "US" in many Windows apps. The character is still correct and displays as a flag for recipients on other platforms. Some browsers and apps ship their own emoji font and do show flags. Our guide to [flag emojis](/guides/flag-emojis-explained/) explains why.
- **Touch keyboard.** On a tablet or touchscreen, the on-screen keyboard has its own emoji button. You can add the touch keyboard icon to the taskbar from taskbar settings.
- **Older apps.** A few legacy programs do not draw color emoji and show a black-and-white outline or a box instead. The character is still there.
- **Alt + X in Word.** In Microsoft Word, type an emoji's hexadecimal code point, for example `1F525`, and press Alt + X to convert it into the [fire emoji](/emojis/fire/). This works in Word and a few other Microsoft editors, not system-wide.

## Mac

Press **Control + Command + Space** in any text field. A compact picker appears at the cursor.

1. Press Control + Command + Space.
2. Type a word to search. "Heart" lists every heart, "flag" lists flags.
3. Press Return to insert the highlighted emoji, or click one.

On Macs with a **Globe key** (marked 🌐, usually sharing the Fn key), pressing it once opens the same picker. If it does something else on your Mac, such as switching input languages or starting dictation, open **System Settings > Keyboard** and change the "Press 🌐 key to" option to **Show Emoji & Symbols**. Almost every app also has **Edit > Emoji & Symbols** in the menu bar.

Useful extras:

- **Skin tones.** Click and hold a hand or person emoji to choose a tone. macOS remembers it for that emoji.
- **Full Character Viewer.** Click the small icon at the top corner of the picker to expand it into the Character Viewer, a larger window with every emoji, symbol, arrow and pictograph, a favorites list, and the Unicode name of whatever you select.
- **Text replacements.** In **System Settings > Keyboard > Text Replacements**, you can make a shortcut such as `:shrug:` expand to any emoji or string. Replacements sync to your iPhone and iPad through iCloud.
- **Touch Bar models.** MacBook Pros with a Touch Bar show an emoji button there in apps like Messages.

Exact layout of the picker has changed across macOS versions, so yours may look slightly different, but the shortcut has stayed the same for years. For favorites, text replacements by app and fixes when the Globe key misbehaves, see the full guide on [how to get emojis on a Mac](/guides/how-to-get-emojis-on-mac/).

## iPhone and iPad

The emoji keyboard is installed by default. Tap the **smiley key** at the bottom left of the keyboard. If you have more than one keyboard language installed, that position shows a **globe key**; tap it to cycle keyboards or touch and hold it and choose Emoji.

1. Tap a text field to bring up the keyboard.
2. Tap the smiley or globe key.
3. Tap **Search Emoji** above the grid and type a word, or swipe through the categories.
4. Tap an emoji to insert it. Tap **ABC** to return to letters.

If the key is missing, the keyboard was removed. Go to **Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Add New Keyboard** and choose **Emoji**.

Faster ways to work:

- **Predictive emoji.** As you type a word like "pizza", the suggestion bar often offers the [pizza emoji](/emojis/pizza/). Tap it to replace the word, or type a space first to add the emoji after the word.
- **Tap to replace.** In Messages, type your sentence, switch to the emoji keyboard, and words that have a matching emoji are highlighted. Tap a highlighted word to swap it.
- **Skin tones.** Touch and hold a hand such as the [thumbs up](/emojis/thumbs-up/) and slide to a tone. For emojis with two people, you can set each person's tone separately. More in our [skin tones guide](/guides/emoji-skin-tones-explained/).
- **Text Replacement.** **Settings > General > Keyboard > Text Replacement** lets you set a short code that expands into an emoji or a whole string of them.
- **Hardware keyboards.** With an iPad keyboard attached, the Globe key opens the emoji picker.

Recent iOS versions also put stickers alongside emoji in the same keyboard, and on iPhones that support Apple Intelligence you can create custom Genmoji from a text description. Those are sent as images rather than as standard emoji characters, so they will not appear inline in every app.

## Android: Gboard, Samsung Keyboard and others

Android does not have one keyboard; it depends on the phone maker. The two you are most likely to have are Google's Gboard (default on Pixel and many other phones) and Samsung Keyboard (default on Galaxy devices).

### Gboard

1. Tap a text field.
2. Tap the **smiley key** to the left of the space bar. On some layouts it shares a key with the comma; touch and hold the comma and slide to the smiley. In some apps the Enter key shows the smiley instead when you touch and hold it.
3. Use the **search bar** at the top of the emoji panel, or scroll the categories.
4. Tap **ABC** to return to letters.

Touch and hold any emoji with a small triangle in the corner to choose a skin tone or gender variant. Gboard also suggests emojis in the strip above the keys as you type, if emoji suggestions are turned on in **Gboard settings > Emojis, Stickers & GIFs**. When you pick certain pairs of emojis, Gboard offers blended stickers; that feature has its own [Emoji Kitchen guide](/guides/emoji-kitchen-guide/).

### Samsung Keyboard

1. Tap a text field.
2. Tap the **smiley icon** in the toolbar above the keys. If the toolbar is hidden, look for the smiley on the key beside the space bar, or turn the toolbar on in **Settings > General management > Samsung Keyboard settings**.
3. Tap the magnifier to search.

Touch and hold for skin tones, as on Gboard. Other keyboards such as Microsoft SwiftKey follow the same pattern: a smiley key near the space bar, and a search field inside the panel.

One Android-specific caveat: which emojis are available depends on your Android version and the phone maker's updates. If the newest emojis are missing from your keyboard, a system update is usually what adds them. Our article on [why emojis show as boxes](/guides/why-emojis-show-as-boxes/) covers the receiving side of the same problem.

## Chromebook

On current ChromeOS versions there are two routes:

- **Keyboard:** press **Search + Shift + Space**. On newer Chromebooks the Search key is labeled as the Launcher key; it is the same key, where Caps Lock sits on other laptops.
- **Mouse:** right-click (or two-finger tap) inside a text field and choose **Emoji**.

The picker has a search field at the top and tabs for emoji, symbols and kaomoji; newer versions add GIFs. Emojis that support skin tones show a small indicator; click and hold or open the variant menu to pick one. In tablet mode, the on-screen keyboard has its own smiley button.

Some recent models, including many Chromebook Plus laptops, have a Quick Insert key that can also bring up emojis, depending on the model. If the shortcut does nothing, check that your device is up to date in **Settings > About ChromeOS**, since the picker was added and redesigned across several releases. Our [Chromebook emoji guide](/guides/how-to-do-emojis-on-chromebook/) goes further, including school-managed devices and tablet mode.

## Linux

Linux varies by desktop environment, but the mainstream ones all have something built in.

- **GNOME (Ubuntu, Fedora Workstation and others).** In most GTK apps, press **Control + period** or **Control + semicolon** to open the emoji chooser, then type to search. You can also right-click a text field and choose **Insert Emoji**. GNOME's Characters app offers a searchable catalog you can copy from.
- **IBus users.** Older setups use **Control + Shift + E**, which shows an underlined "e"; type a name such as `joy` and press Space to see matches. The default binding changed in later IBus versions, and it can be customized in IBus preferences under the Emoji tab.
- **KDE Plasma.** Press **Meta + period** (Meta is the Super or Windows key) to open the Emoji Selector, which copies your choice to the clipboard or inserts it depending on version.
- **Any desktop: code point entry.** In GTK apps and many terminals, press **Control + Shift + U**, type the hexadecimal code point (for example `1f44d`), and press Enter or Space.

If emojis appear as empty boxes or monochrome outlines, the system is missing a color emoji font. Installing the Noto Color Emoji package from your distribution's repositories normally fixes it.

## Methods that work inside apps

When you are in a browser or a chat app, the app's own tools are sometimes quicker than the system picker.

- **Shortcodes.** Slack, Discord, GitHub and many forums convert names between colons into emoji. Type a colon and a few letters, such as `:thu`, and an autocomplete menu appears. Shortcode names are not standardized: the [face with tears of joy](/emojis/face-with-tears-of-joy/) is `:joy:` on most of them, but names for newer emojis differ between apps.
- **Google Docs.** Use **Insert > Emoji**, or type a colon followed by a word to search inline.
- **Emoticon conversion.** Some apps turn typed emoticons into emoji, so `:)` becomes a [slightly smiling face](/emojis/slightly-smiling-face/). This can usually be switched off in the app's settings if it gets in the way, which matters at work because that particular smile [can read as cold](/guides/passive-aggressive-emojis/).
- **Copy and paste.** An emoji is ordinary text. You can copy one from any web page, including the emoji pages on this site, and paste it anywhere that accepts text. Browse by category, for example [smileys and emotion](/categories/smileys-and-emotion/) or [symbols](/categories/symbols/), then copy what you need.

## When the emoji will not type or looks wrong

- **The shortcut does nothing.** Make sure the cursor is in a text field. On Windows, check that another program has not claimed the shortcut. On a Mac, check the Globe key setting described above.
- **You see a box, a question mark or two separate emojis.** The app or device does not support that emoji yet. Combined emojis such as family groups and professions are built from several characters and fall apart on older systems.
- **The emoji looks different after sending.** That is normal. Apple, Google, Samsung and Microsoft each draw their own versions; see [iPhone vs Android emojis](/guides/iphone-vs-android-emojis/).
- **A form rejects it.** Some websites, usernames and older databases do not accept emoji at all. No keyboard trick gets around that.
- **A screen reader user is in the conversation.** Each emoji is read aloud by name, so a row of ten is tedious. Our [accessibility guide](/guides/emoji-accessibility-screen-readers/) has practical limits.

## A setup worth five minutes

If you use emojis daily, these small changes pay off on every device:

1. Memorize the one shortcut for your computer: Windows + period, Control + Command + Space, or Search + Shift + Space.
2. Get in the habit of typing to search as soon as the picker opens.
3. Set your preferred skin tone once on the hands you use most, such as [folded hands](/emojis/folded-hands/) and [waving hand](/emojis/waving-hand/).
4. Create two or three text replacements for the emojis or combinations you send constantly.
5. Keep your operating system updated, since new emojis arrive with system updates rather than app updates on most devices.
