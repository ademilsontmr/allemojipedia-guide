The main Christmas emojis are the Christmas tree 🎄, Santa Claus 🎅, the wrapped gift 🎁, the bell 🔔, the star ⭐, and the snowman ☃️, supported by winter symbols like the snowflake ❄️ and by red and green hearts. Most of them mean exactly what they show, so the useful questions are which one fits which message, what to use for the many Christmas symbols that have no emoji, and how to greet people who celebrate something else in December.

This guide covers each group in turn, then gives greeting and caption ideas you can copy.

## The core Christmas emojis at a glance

| Emoji | Official name | What it says in a message |
|---|---|---|
| 🎄 | Christmas tree | Christmas itself; decorating; "the season has started" |
| 🎅 | Santa Claus | Santa, gift-giving, the childhood side of Christmas |
| 🤶 | Mrs. Claus | Santa's partner; often used by women for themselves in Santa mode |
| 🧑‍🎄 | Mx Claus | Gender-neutral Santa figure |
| 🎁 | Wrapped gift | Presents, a surprise, "I got you something" |
| 🔔 | Bell | Jingle bells, church bells, carols |
| ⭐ 🌟 | Star, glowing star | The tree topper, the Star of Bethlehem, general sparkle |
| ☃️ ⛄ | Snowman (with and without snow) | Winter fun, snow days, cold weather |
| ❄️ | Snowflake | Snow, winter, cold; a neutral "holiday season" marker |
| 🦌 | Deer | Reindeer, Rudolph, Santa's sleigh team |
| 🕯️ | Candle | Candlelight services, Advent, coziness, remembrance |
| 🧦 | Socks | Christmas stockings |
| 🛷 | Sled | Sledding; Santa's sleigh by stand-in |
| 👼 | Baby angel | Angels, the nativity story, tree-top angels |
| ⛪ | Church | Christmas Eve or Christmas Day services |

The [Christmas tree](/emojis/christmas-tree/) does the most work. On its own it can mean "Merry Christmas," "we put the tree up," or simply "December." Paired with the [wrapped gift](/emojis/wrapped-gift/) it shifts toward presents and shopping; paired with a [candle](/emojis/candle/) or a star it reads as quieter and more traditional.

The [bell](/emojis/bell/) is a general-purpose emoji that Christmas borrows. In December it means sleigh bells and carols. The rest of the year the same symbol means notifications, so "🔔 new post" in a caption is not festive unless something around it makes it so.

## Santa, Mrs. Claus, and Mx Claus

There are three Santa-family emojis. Santa Claus 🎅 has been part of the standard Unicode set since 2010, Mrs. Claus 🤶 arrived in 2016, and Mx Claus 🧑‍🎄 was added in 2020 as a gender-neutral option. All three accept skin tone modifiers, which is how you get a Santa in any of the five tones as well as the default yellow. If you have never changed a tone, [emoji skin tones explained](/guides/emoji-skin-tones-explained/) shows where the setting lives on each keyboard.

A few usage notes:

- **🎅 is the default "Christmas person."** People use it for Santa himself, for Secret Santa exchanges ("Who's my 🎅?"), and for anyone handing out presents.
- **🤶 is often self-referential.** A parent wrapping gifts at midnight might caption the photo "🤶 on duty."
- **🧑‍🎄 is a joined sequence.** It is built from the person emoji and the Christmas tree emoji, glued together invisibly. On an older phone or an app that has not been updated, it can break apart into a person followed by a tree. If your recipient has an old device, 🎅 is safer. The same splitting effect is explained in [why emojis show as boxes](/guides/why-emojis-show-as-boxes/).

There is no emoji for a Christmas elf. The elf emoji 🧝 is the pointed-eared fantasy kind, drawn like a character from a fantasy novel, though plenty of people use it for Santa's helpers anyway. Context makes it work: "🧝 wrapping station is open" is clear in December.

## Christmas symbols with no emoji, and the usual stand-ins

A surprising number of Christmas icons are missing from the Unicode set. As of Emoji 17.0 there is no dedicated emoji for any of the following, and the right-hand column lists what people send instead.

| Missing symbol | Common stand-in |
|---|---|
| Candy cane | 🍭 lollipop or 🍬 candy |
| Wreath | 🎄 with 🎀, or 🟢 🎀 |
| Holly or mistletoe | 🌿 herb, 🍒 or ❤️💚 |
| Stocking | 🧦 socks |
| Tree ornament or bauble | 🔴 red circle, 🪩 mirror ball, ✨ |
| Gingerbread man | 🍪 cookie, sometimes 🫚 ginger root with 🍪 |
| Sleigh | 🛷 sled |
| Reindeer | 🦌 deer (🔴 added for Rudolph's nose) |
| Nativity scene | ⭐ 👶 🐑 🐫 👑, or ⛪ 🌟 |
| String lights | ✨ sparkles or 💡 light bulb |
| Eggnog or mulled wine | 🥛 glass of milk, 🍷 wine glass |
| Christmas cracker | 🎉 party popper |

Two of these stand-ins are worth a closer look. The ribbon 🎀 is a pink bow on most platforms, so it suggests gift wrap more than a traditional red bow; put it next to a tree or a gift and nobody will mind. The mirror ball 🪩 has become a popular ornament substitute because it is round, shiny, and hangs from a string, but it also says "party," which fits New Year's Eve better than Christmas morning.

If you want a missing symbol badly enough, Google's Emoji Kitchen can blend two emojis into a sticker, and some combinations come out convincingly festive. The [Emoji Kitchen guide](/guides/emoji-kitchen-guide/) explains where it works. A sticker is an image, not a character, so it cannot go in a caption or a subject line.

New emojis are added through public proposals to the Unicode Consortium, and seasonal symbols face a specific hurdle: the selection criteria favor symbols that will be used year-round. That process is described in [how new emojis get approved](/guides/how-new-emojis-get-approved/).

## Winter, snow, and cozy-season emojis

These emojis are not tied to Christmas, which is exactly why they are useful. They carry the season without naming a holiday.

- **❄️ [Snowflake](/emojis/snowflake/).** Snow, cold, winter. It is one of the older symbols in Unicode and depends on a hidden variation selector to display in color, so in a few apps and fonts it appears as a thin black snowflake instead. Outside December it has a second life as slang for someone seen as overly sensitive, but next to a tree or a mug nobody reads it that way.
- **☃️ and ⛄ Snowman.** The first has snowflakes falling around it; the second is officially the [snowman without snow](/emojis/snowman-without-snow/). Use either. ⛄ displays in color more reliably.
- **🥶 Cold face.** Freezing weather: "Waiting for the parade 🥶."
- **☕ [Hot beverage](/emojis/hot-beverage/).** Coffee, tea, hot chocolate, or mulled anything. With 🍪 it is the standard "cozy night in" pair.
- **🧣 🧤 Scarf and gloves.** Bundling up; also handy for gift hints.
- **⛷️ 🏂 ⛸️ Skier, snowboarder, ice skate.** Winter trips and rink outings.
- **🔥 Fire.** A fireplace, in context. Without a mug, blanket, or tree nearby it will be read as its usual slang meaning of "excellent" or "attractive."
- **✨ Sparkles.** Lights, frost, magic. The most flexible decoration in the set.

Remember that December is summer for a large part of the world. Friends in Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, or Brazil are more likely to send 🎄☀️🏖️ than 🎄❄️, and a snowman in a message to them is a joke, not a weather report.

## The religious side: nativity, Advent, and church

For many people Christmas is first a Christian feast marking the birth of Jesus, and the emoji set has a small group of symbols for that side of the day.

- **⭐ or 🌟** stands for the Star of Bethlehem. The [glowing star](/emojis/glowing-star/) is the more common choice because it looks like it is shining.
- **👼 Baby angel** covers the angels of the nativity story and tree-top angels.
- **⛪ Church** marks a service: "Midnight Mass ⛪🕯️."
- **🕯️ Candle** fits candlelight services and Advent. Western churches begin Advent on the fourth Sunday before Christmas, and many households mark it with a wreath of four candles, lighting one more each week.
- **✝️ [Latin cross](/emojis/latin-cross/)** appears in explicitly faith-centered greetings, though the cross is more closely tied to Easter than to Christmas.
- **🙏 [Folded hands](/emojis/folded-hands/)** adds prayer, gratitude, or blessing: "Wishing you a blessed Christmas 🙏."
- **👑 🐫 🎁** together suggest the Magi, whose visit is celebrated at Epiphany on January 6. In Spain and much of Latin America that day, Three Kings' Day, is the main occasion for giving gifts.

Dates differ between churches as well. Some Orthodox churches follow the Julian calendar for fixed feasts and keep Christmas on January 7, so "Merry Christmas 🎄" on that date is correct, not late. For a fuller treatment of faith symbols through the church year, see [Christian emojis and their meanings](/guides/christian-emojis-meanings/).

## Hanukkah, Kwanzaa, New Year, and other December holidays

"Holiday emojis" covers more than Christmas, and using the right symbol for the right celebration is a small courtesy that people notice.

**Hanukkah.** The menorah emoji 🕎 has nine branches, which makes it a Hanukkah menorah specifically. It is usually combined with the Star of David ✡️, a blue heart 💙, and sometimes 🍩 for the jelly doughnuts traditionally eaten during the festival. Hanukkah moves around the calendar from year to year because it follows the Hebrew calendar, so check the dates before sending a greeting, and do not attach a Christmas tree to it.

**Kwanzaa.** Kwanzaa runs from December 26 to January 1. There is no emoji for the kinara, its seven-branched candleholder, so people use 🕯️ with hearts in the holiday's colors: ❤️🖤💚.

**St. Nicholas Day and St. Lucia.** In parts of Europe, December 6 is when children find sweets in their shoes (👞🍬🎅), and on December 13 Scandinavian countries celebrate St. Lucia with candles (🕯️👑).

**Las Posadas.** The piñata emoji 🪅 fits the nine nights of processions and parties held in Mexico before Christmas.

**New Year.** The set changes completely on December 26. New Year's Eve belongs to [fireworks](/emojis/fireworks/) 🎆, the [bottle with popping cork](/emojis/bottle-with-popping-cork/) 🍾, [clinking glasses](/emojis/clinking-glasses/) 🥂, and the [party popper](/emojis/party-popper/) 🎉. The pine decoration emoji 🎍 shows a kadomatsu, a traditional Japanese New Year decoration, so it is not a Christmas plant despite appearing near the tree on the keyboard.

For greetings that need to cover everybody, such as a company-wide message, stay with ❄️ ✨ ⛄ ☕ 🎁. They are seasonal without assuming which holiday the reader keeps.

### Cards and texts to family and friends

- Merry Christmas! 🎄❤️
- Merry Christmas from our house to yours 🏡🎄✨
- Thinking of you this Christmas 🤍🕯️
- Wishing you a blessed Christmas 🙏🌟
- Santa's on his way 🎅🛷🦌

### Photo captions

- Tree's up 🎄✨
- All I want for Christmas is this view ❄️🏔️
- Cookie production line 🍪👩‍🍳🎄
- Matching pajamas, no regrets 🎅🤶
- Christmas, southern hemisphere edition 🎄☀️🏖️

### Work and group chats

- Happy holidays, team ❄️✨
- Secret Santa names are out 🎅🤫
- Office closed until January 2. Enjoy the break ⛄☕
- Thank you for a great year 🙌🎁

### Small combos that tell a story

- 🎄 + 🎁 + 😴 = Christmas Eve, waiting for morning
- ☕ + 🍪 + 🎬 = holiday movie night
- ✈️ + 🏡 + 🎄 = flying home for Christmas
- 🦌 + 🔴 = Rudolph
- 🛍️ + 😅 + 🎁 = last-minute shopping

Building sequences like these follows a few simple patterns, such as ordering emojis the way the event unfolds; [emoji combos and ideas](/guides/emoji-combos-ideas/) has the general method.

## Getting the tone right

Christmas emojis are low-risk, but a few habits make greetings land better.

**Match the emoji to the relationship.** A red heart is natural for family and close friends. For a client or a manager, a tree or a snowflake carries the warmth without the intimacy. The red and green pair ❤️💚 reads as "Christmas colors" rather than romance, which makes it a friendly middle option.

**Be gentle with people having a hard December.** The holidays are difficult for anyone grieving or alone. A row of 🎅🎉🎁 can feel loud. A candle and a white heart, as in "Thinking of you this Christmas 🕯️🤍," acknowledges the season without demanding cheer.

**Do not replace words with pictures.** "Merry 🎄" is cute once. A sentence where every noun is an emoji is hard to read, and screen readers announce each emoji by its full name, so "🎄🎄🎄🎄🎄" becomes "Christmas tree" five times. The guide to [emoji accessibility and screen readers](/guides/emoji-accessibility-screen-readers/) explains what listeners hear.

**Expect designs to differ.** The tree, Santa, and the gift are drawn differently by Apple, Google, Samsung, and Microsoft. Gift wrap colors, the ornaments on the tree, and Santa's expression all vary. The meaning survives, but a color-coordinated caption on your phone may not match on someone else's, as [iPhone vs. Android emojis](/guides/iphone-vs-android-emojis/) shows.

**In marketing, use them as an accent.** A single 🎄 or 🎁 at the start of a subject line signals a seasonal offer at a glance. Every inbox is full of them in December, though, so the emoji will not make a message stand out by itself; see [emojis in marketing](/guides/emojis-in-marketing/) for what tends to work.

## A quick checklist before you send

1. **Which holiday does the reader keep?** Christmas symbols for Christmas, 🕎 for Hanukkah, ❄️✨ when you are not sure.
2. **Is it a religious or a secular greeting?** ⛪🌟🙏 for the former, 🎅🎁⛄ for the latter, 🎄 for both.
3. **Does the symbol exist?** If you are hunting for a candy cane, wreath, or holly, stop looking and use a stand-in from the table above.
4. **Will it display?** Prefer ⛄ over ☃️ and 🎅 over 🧑‍🎄 when the recipient's device is old.
5. **Is two to four enough?** It almost always is. Put them at the end of the sentence and let the words carry the wish.
