😂 and 🤣 mean straightforward laughter; 😭 and 💀 usually mean laughter too, in an exaggerated "I'm crying, I'm dead" way that younger users favor; and 🥲 means smiling through something that hurts. Real sadness is more often shown with the quieter 😢 or with no emoji at all. The meanings overlap, so the words around the emoji and the age of the sender tell you which reading applies.

This guide takes the faces one at a time, then explains the generational shift and how to separate jokes from real tears.

## Quick reference: what each one signals

| Emoji | Official name | Main meaning today | Also used for | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 😂 | Face with tears of joy | That's funny | Laughing something off | Sincere, universal |
| 🤣 | Rolling on the floor laughing | That's hilarious | Teasing | Sincere, loud |
| 😆 | Grinning squinting face | Light laugh | Glee | Friendly |
| 😹 | Cat with tears of joy | Laugh, cat edition | Cat content | Playful |
| 😭 | Loudly crying face | Crying laughing; overwhelmed | Real grief, cuteness, frustration | Dramatic |
| 💀 | Skull | "I'm dead" (laughing) | Cringe, shock; literal death | Deadpan |
| 🥲 | Smiling face with tear | Smiling through pain | Proud or grateful tears | Bittersweet |
| 🥹 | Face holding back tears | Moved, touched | Pleading, pride | Tender |
| 😢 | Crying face | Sad | Disappointment, sympathy | Sincere, quiet |

## 😂 Face with tears of joy

[Face with tears of joy](/emojis/face-with-tears-of-joy/) is the standard laugh: closed, curved eyes, a wide grin, and a tear at each eye. It says "that made me laugh" with no second layer.

Its place in emoji history is unusual. Oxford Dictionaries chose it as Word of the Year for 2015, the first time a pictograph received the title, citing how widely it was being used. It has stayed at or near the top of usage rankings ever since; when the Unicode Consortium published emoji frequency data in 2021, 😂 was still ranked first.

Typical uses:

- A real laugh: "You locked yourself out AGAIN 😂"
- Softening a tease: "Nice parking 😂"
- Laughing at yourself: "I just waved at someone who wasn't waving at me 😂"
- Taking the sting out of a complaint: "Three hours in this line 😂"

Two cautions apply. First, it laughs at whatever it is attached to, so placed after someone else's sincere statement it can read as mockery. "I've decided to start my own business" answered with "😂" is an insult. Second, a single 😂 from someone who normally sends rows of them can feel like a polite, obligatory chuckle. People notice the drop.

## 🤣 Rolling on the floor laughing

[Rolling on the floor laughing](/emojis/rolling-on-the-floor-laughing/) arrived in 2016 and takes its name from the old chat abbreviation ROFL. The face is tilted, the mouth is wider, and the tears fly sideways. It is 😂 with the volume up.

- "The dog's face when the toy squeaked 🤣"
- "I can't believe you actually said that to him 🤣🤣"

Because of the tilt and the sheer size of the laugh, 🤣 feels more physical and less controlled. It is especially popular in family group chats and on platforms with an older user base, and it is common as a reaction to shared jokes and videos. It carries the same mockery risk as 😂, slightly amplified: laughing this hard at something a person did not intend as funny stings more.

If you are choosing between the two, use 😂 for "that's funny" and 🤣 for "I actually laughed out loud."

## 😭 Loudly crying face: the laugh that looks like sobbing

The [loudly crying face](/emojis/loudly-crying-face/) shows a wide open mouth and two streams of tears. On paper it is the saddest face on the keyboard. In practice it has become one of the most flexible, and for many users it is the default way to laugh.

Its main modern uses:

1. **Crying with laughter.** "He replied all to the whole company 😭" means the sender is in stitches, not in mourning.
2. **Overwhelmed by cuteness or affection.** "Look at his tiny paws 😭" or "You remembered my birthday 😭."
3. **Dramatic frustration.** "I have three exams tomorrow 😭" is a complaint played up for effect.
4. **Real sorrow.** "We had to put Max down today 😭" is exactly what it looks like.

The logic is exaggeration. Saying "I'm crying" about a joke is the same figure of speech as "I'm dying"; the emoji just illustrates it. Since the picture is so over the top, it works for any feeling that is too big to contain, whether happy or sad.

That flexibility is also the risk. A reader who does not know the convention may think you are upset, and using 😭 about a small inconvenience in the same chat where someone has shared real bad news can look thoughtless. For serious sadness, plain words are clearer than any face.

## 💀 Skull: "I'm dead"

The [skull](/emojis/skull/) as a laugh comes from slang: "I'm dead," "that killed me," "I'm deceased." Sending 💀 after a joke means it was so funny, or so painfully awkward, that the sender has figuratively died.

- "She called the teacher 'mom' 💀"
- "Not me spending 20 minutes looking for the phone in my hand 💀"
- "'Per my last email' 💀💀💀"

Compared with 😂, the skull is dry. It has no smile, so it suits deadpan humor, secondhand embarrassment, and the kind of joke you laugh at while shaking your head. It often carries a note of "I can't believe this" that the laughing faces lack.

Related symbols extend the bit. The [skull and crossbones](/emojis/skull-and-crossbones/) ☠️ is used the same way by some people, though it leans more toward literal danger and poison warnings. The [coffin](/emojis/coffin/) ⚰️ and [headstone](/emojis/headstone/) 🪦 take the "I have died" joke one step further.

The skull still has its literal meanings: death, danger, Halloween, horror, and heavy metal aesthetics. Misunderstandings happen mostly between generations. A parent who receives "💀" in reply to a family photo may be alarmed; the teenager meant "this is hilarious." The wider vocabulary this belongs to, including how other symbols were repurposed, is explained in [Gen Z emoji slang](/guides/gen-z-emoji-slang/).

One real caution: do not send 💀 in response to news about illness, accidents, or an actual death. There the literal meaning takes over, and the joke reading does not rescue it.

## 🥲 and 🥹: smiling through it and holding it in

These two newer faces cover feelings between laughing and crying.

### 🥲 Smiling face with tear

The [smiling face with tear](/emojis/smiling-face-with-tear/), added in 2020, has a gentle closed smile and a single tear. It has two main readings.

- **Smiling through pain.** "Just paid my car repair bill 🥲" or "It's fine, I didn't want a weekend anyway 🥲." The sender is hurt or annoyed and putting a brave face on it.
- **Happy tears.** "My little brother graduated today 🥲" expresses pride and tenderness.

It differs from the [upside-down face](/emojis/upside-down-face/) 🙃, which is sarcastic about a bad situation; 🥲 admits that the situation actually hurts. It also differs from the [melting face](/emojis/melting-face/) 🫠, which is about embarrassment or dread more than sadness.

### 🥹 Face holding back tears

The [face holding back tears](/emojis/face-holding-back-tears/), added in 2022, has large, glassy eyes and a small wavering smile. It means being moved: by kindness, by something adorable, by pride. "You made this for me? 🥹" is the typical use. It resembles the [pleading face](/emojis/pleading-face/) 🥺, but 🥺 asks for something and 🥹 reacts to something.

Both faces are sincere in a way that 😭 and 💀 are not. Nobody sends 🥹 ironically very often.

## Actual sadness: the crying faces that mean it

When people are truly sad, they tend to use quieter emojis, or none.

- **😢 [Crying face](/emojis/crying-face/).** One tear, a small frown. This is the main emoji for real, moderate sadness and for sympathy: "I'm so sorry to hear that 😢."
- **😞 [Disappointed face](/emojis/disappointed-face/)** and **😔 [pensive face](/emojis/pensive-face/).** Downcast eyes, no tears. Letdown, regret, low mood.
- **😿 Crying cat.** A softer, slightly cute version of 😢.
- **💔 [Broken heart](/emojis/broken-heart/).** Heartbreak and grief, though it is also used lightly ("They discontinued my favorite snack 💔").

Two faces are often mistaken for crying. The [sad but relieved face](/emojis/sad-but-relieved-face/) 😥 has a drop on its brow, which is sweat, not a tear; it shows a narrow escape or lingering worry. The [sleepy face](/emojis/sleepy-face/) 😪 has a bubble at its nose, a comic-book convention from Japanese manga for someone asleep, yet many Western users read it as a tear and send it for sadness. On both, check the context before assuming.

### How to tell a joke from real distress

| Signal | Probably joking | Probably serious |
|---|---|---|
| Content | A meme, an anecdote, a compliment | A loss, a diagnosis, a breakup |
| Emoji count | Repeated: 😭😭😭 | One, or none |
| Capitalization | ALL CAPS, stretched words ("nooooo") | Plain lowercase, full sentences |
| Which face | 😭 💀 | 😢 😞 💔 |
| What follows | More jokes, quick replies | Silence, short answers |

No single signal is decisive. "I failed my exam 😭" could be a shrug or a crisis. If you cannot tell and you care about the person, the best response is a question: "Oh no, are you okay?" works either way.

## Why the laugh emojis split by generation

The different laughs have become, loosely, markers of age and of how online someone is. The pattern looks like this:

- **😂 and 🤣** are used by everyone and are the mainstream laugh for most adults.
- **😭 and 💀** are the preferred laughs of many teenagers and people in their twenties, some of whom consider 😂 dated.

The shift is easy to explain without statistics. When an emoji becomes universal, used by parents, brands, and news anchors, it stops feeling expressive to people who want their messages to sound like their own group. They reach for something stronger and less official. A sobbing face and a skull are more extreme than a laughing face, so they feel like a bigger reaction, and they began as in-jokes that outsiders did not immediately understand. The same cycle shaped older text laughter, from "lol" losing its literal meaning to "lmao" and beyond.

A few practical consequences follow:

- **Nobody is wrong.** 😂 from a forty-year-old and 😭 from a nineteen-year-old can express the identical reaction.
- **Do not overcorrect.** An adult who suddenly switches to 💀 to seem current may come across as trying too hard. Using the emoji that is natural to you reads better than using the fashionable one awkwardly.
- **Platforms differ.** Comment sections on TikTok lean heavily on 😭 and 💀, while family WhatsApp groups run on 😂 and 🤣. See [TikTok emoji meanings](/guides/tiktok-emoji-meanings/) and [WhatsApp emoji meanings](/guides/whatsapp-emoji-meanings/) for the local habits.
- **Trends keep moving.** Whatever is current will be replaced. The meanings in this guide describe usage as of late 2026.

## How strong is the laugh? An intensity ladder

Repetition and choice of emoji together work like volume control. Roughly, from quietest to loudest:

1. **"haha" or 😄**: acknowledged as amusing.
2. **😆**: a real but light laugh.
3. **😂**: properly funny.
4. **🤣 or 😂😂**: laughed out loud.
5. **😭 or 💀**: funniest thing today.
6. **😭😭😭, 💀💀💀, or a mix like 😭💀**: cannot recover.

Baselines matter more than the absolute level. Someone who ends every sentence with 😂 is not laughing each time; the emoji has become punctuation that keeps the tone light. For that person, a message with no emoji is the notable one. Reading tone from changes in a person's habits is covered more fully in [emoji meanings in texting](/guides/emoji-meanings-in-texting/).

The plainer smiling and grinning faces sit below this ladder, and [smiley face emojis explained](/guides/smiley-face-emojis-explained/) compares them one by one.

## Choosing the right one for your audience

**Friends your own age.** Whatever the group uses. Mirroring the other person's laugh emoji is a small, natural way to match their tone.

**Family chats and mixed ages.** 😂 and 🤣 are understood by all. If you send 😭 or 💀 to an older relative, be ready to explain that nobody is hurt.

**Work.** 😂 and 😆 are safe for humor among colleagues. 💀 is fine on many informal teams but can puzzle or unsettle people who do not know the slang, and 😭 about a workload can be read as a real complaint. When writing to clients or senior people, 😄 or words are safer. [Emojis at work etiquette](/guides/emojis-at-work-etiquette/) has more on reading the room.

**Someone sharing something sincere.** No laughing emoji unless they laughed first. Respond to the feeling, not the comic potential.

**Someone who might be struggling.** Skip the dramatic faces and write a sentence.

## A rule of thumb before you send

Ask one question: **could the person on the other end take this literally?**

- If you are laughing and they will know it, send whichever laugh is natural to you.
- If a crying or skull emoji might be taken as real distress or bad taste, switch to 😂 or add words: "😭 I'm laughing so hard."
- If you are actually sad, say so in a sentence and let 😢 or 💔 support it.
- If they sent you tears and you cannot tell which kind, ask.
