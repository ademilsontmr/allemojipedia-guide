The new emojis on phones in 2026 come from **Emoji 17.0**, which the Unicode Consortium approved on September 9, 2025. It added eight new characters: distorted face, fight cloud, hairy creature, orca, trombone, landslide, treasure chest and apple core. It also added a ballet dancer and new skin tone options for two existing emoji. They rolled out to devices between late 2025 and 2026, depending on the platform.

If you came here because someone sent you a blank box, skip to the section on getting them. Otherwise, here is each one, what it is for, and what it replaces.

## The Emoji 17.0 list at a glance

| Emoji name | Category | What people use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Distorted face | Smileys | Overwhelm, shock, a reaction too big for a normal face |
| Fight cloud | Smileys (emotion symbols) | Arguments, chaos, playful scuffles |
| Hairy creature | People and fantasy | Bigfoot and other cryptids; looking unkempt |
| Orca | Animals | The animal; ocean and wildlife topics |
| Trombone | Objects (instruments) | Music, brass bands, the "sad trombone" joke |
| Landslide | Travel and places | The natural disaster; a landslide win or collapse |
| Treasure chest | Objects | Rewards, loot, hidden gems, pirates |
| Apple core | Food | Finished, used up, nothing left; composting |
| Ballet dancer | People | Ballet and dance, in a gender-neutral form with skin tones |
| Skin tones for people with bunny ears and people wrestling | People | Mixed and matched skin tones for each person |

Counting every skin tone and gender combination, the release adds more than 160 emoji. Counting ideas, it adds nine. That gap is normal: most of the total in any recent release is variations.

## The two new faces and feelings

### Distorted face

The distorted face is a yellow face that appears stretched or warped, with large startled eyes. It is the headline addition because faces are what people use most.

It fills a spot between several existing emoji. The [melting face](/emojis/melting-face/) is resigned and slowly giving up. The [face with spiral eyes](/emojis/face-with-spiral-eyes/) is dizzy or hypnotized. The [woozy face](/emojis/woozy-face/) is tipsy or lovestruck. The distorted face is sharper than all three: something just happened and your brain has not caught up.

Typical uses:

- "They moved the deadline to tomorrow" followed by the distorted face.
- Reacting to a photo, a price or a plot twist you were not ready for.
- Describing sensory overload, lack of sleep or too much caffeine.

Because it is new, there is no settled slang meaning yet. Expect that to change; faces with exaggerated expressions tend to pick up ironic uses quickly, as covered in [Gen Z emoji slang](/guides/gen-z-emoji-slang/).

### Fight cloud

The fight cloud is the comic-strip dust cloud that appears when cartoon characters brawl, usually drawn with stars or limbs poking out. It belongs with the other comic symbols in the [smileys and emotion category](/categories/smileys-and-emotion/), such as the [anger symbol](/emojis/anger-symbol/), [collision](/emojis/collision/) and [dashing away](/emojis/dashing-away/).

It is a light way to talk about conflict. Use it for siblings arguing over the remote, a comment section that got out of hand, or a group chat debating where to eat. Because the cartoon framing makes any fight sound silly, avoid it when the conflict is serious to the other person. "Heard you two had a fight cloud last night" can come across as mocking.

## The hairy creature and the ballet dancer

### Hairy creature

Hairy creature is a tall, fur-covered figure in the style of Bigfoot or Sasquatch. The name is generic on purpose. Unicode's guidelines steer away from emoji tied to one specific named character, and a neutral name lets the same emoji stand for a yeti, a Sasquatch or any regional equivalent.

It joins the fantasy figures alongside the [ogre](/emojis/ogre/), [goblin](/emojis/goblin/), [ghost](/emojis/ghost/) and [alien](/emojis/alien/). In practice people use it three ways: for the cryptid itself and anything "rarely seen" ("look who finally showed up to the group chat"), for outdoors and camping posts, and as a self-deprecating description of going too long without a haircut or a shave.

### Ballet dancer

Existing dance emoji are specific: the [woman dancing](/emojis/woman-dancing/) is in a flamenco-style dress and the [man dancing](/emojis/man-dancing/) is in a disco pose. Emoji 17.0 adds a ballet dancer that is not tied to a gender and supports skin tones.

Technically it is not a new character. It is a sequence: a person emoji joined to the existing ballet shoes emoji with an invisible zero width joiner. On a device that has not updated, it falls back to those two emoji shown next to each other, so the meaning survives even when the new artwork is missing.

### New skin tone combinations

Two older emoji that show a pair of people, people with bunny ears and people wrestling, previously came only in the default yellow. Emoji 17.0 lets each person have a different skin tone, the same treatment couples and handshakes received in earlier releases. This accounts for most of the release's large raw count. How the modifiers work is covered in [emoji skin tones explained](/guides/emoji-skin-tones-explained/).

## Orca, trombone, landslide, treasure chest and apple core

### Orca

The orca joins the marine animals in [animals and nature](/categories/animals-and-nature/), next to the [shark](/emojis/shark/) and [octopus](/emojis/octopus/). There were already a spouting whale, a blue whale and a dolphin, but nothing with the orca's black and white pattern, and it had been a common request for years. Beyond the literal animal, expect it in posts about the Pacific Northwest, marine conservation and anything described as a "killer" performance.

### Trombone

The wind instruments previously included a trumpet and a saxophone. The trombone fills out band and orchestra talk, and it has a ready-made joke meaning: the descending "sad trombone" sound for an anticlimax. "Drove 40 minutes and the place was closed" plus a trombone is likely to become a standard use. Pair it with [musical notes](/emojis/musical-notes/) when you mean actual music, so the tone is clear.

### Landslide

Landslide shows rocks and earth sliding down a slope. It joins the small group of natural-hazard symbols, alongside the [cyclone](/emojis/cyclone/) and the volcano. It has a literal use in news and safety contexts, and two figurative ones that pull in opposite directions: "won by a landslide" for an overwhelming victory, and "everything is sliding downhill" for a collapse. Add a word or a second emoji, such as a [trophy](/emojis/trophy/), if the meaning could be read either way.

### Treasure chest

A wooden chest, usually drawn open with gold inside. This is the most flexible of the new objects. It works for:

- **Rewards and loot** in gaming, next to the [video game](/emojis/video-game/) controller or a [gem stone](/emojis/gem-stone/).
- **Hidden gems**: a favorite small restaurant, a secondhand find, a saved playlist.
- **Savings and windfalls**, as a friendlier alternative to the [money bag](/emojis/money-bag/).
- **Pirate themes**, with the [pirate flag](/emojis/pirate-flag/).

For marketers it is an obvious fit for giveaways and loyalty rewards, with the usual caution that a brand-new emoji will not display for a share of your audience yet. See [emojis in marketing](/guides/emojis-in-marketing/) before putting one in a subject line.

### Apple core

An apple eaten down to the core. Food emoji usually show the food at its best, so this one stands out for showing what is left. The [red apple](/emojis/red-apple/) says fresh, healthy, teacher, school. The apple core says finished, used up, nothing left. It also suits composting and food-waste topics. In slang it is likely to be used the way "cooked" is: "after that exam I am an apple core."

## How to get the new emojis on your device

Emoji are part of your operating system's font. Installing a keyboard app does not add them, because the keyboard can only type characters the system knows how to draw. You need a system update.

**iPhone and iPad.** Go to Settings, then General, then Software Update. For several years Apple has added the previous autumn's emoji in a spring point release (the ".4" update), and Emoji 17.0 followed that general schedule in 2026. If your iPhone cannot run the current iOS version, it will not get the new emoji.

**Google Pixel.** Google publishes its Noto Color Emoji designs around the time Unicode approves a release, and Pixel phones are usually the first Android devices to receive them through a system update. Check Settings, System, Software updates.

**Samsung Galaxy.** Samsung draws its own emoji and ships them with One UI updates, which generally trail Pixel phones. Check Settings, Software update. Older Galaxy models may wait longer or not receive the update.

**Other Android phones.** Timing depends on the manufacturer. Some apps, including WhatsApp, use their own emoji artwork and add new emoji through an app update, so you may see new emoji inside one app before the rest of your phone has them.

**Windows and Mac.** macOS gets new emoji in the update that matches the iOS release. Windows adds them through Windows 11 updates, typically later than phones.

**The web.** Sites like X and Discord draw emoji with their own image sets, so support depends on the site, not your device.

Step-by-step ways to open the emoji picker on each system are in [how to type emojis on any device](/guides/how-to-type-emojis-on-any-device/).

## What the other person sees

Sending a new emoji is a small gamble until most devices have updated. Three outcomes are possible.

1. **They see the emoji**, though possibly drawn quite differently. New faces vary most between platforms in their first year. Compare designs in [iPhone vs Android emojis](/guides/iphone-vs-android-emojis/).
2. **They see a box or a question mark.** Their system has no image for that code point. This happens with the eight new characters.
3. **They see separate pieces.** For sequences such as the ballet dancer or the new skin tone pairs, older devices show the components side by side.

The causes and fixes are in [why emojis show as boxes](/guides/why-emojis-show-as-boxes/). A practical rule for the first year: use new emoji freely with friends, and avoid relying on them to carry the meaning in work messages, customer emails or anything a screen reader user must understand. Screen readers on older software may announce nothing at all for a character they do not recognize, which is discussed in [emoji accessibility and screen readers](/guides/emoji-accessibility-screen-readers/).

## Why there are so few new emojis each year

Eight new characters is small compared with releases a decade ago, and it is intentional. The group that reviews proposals at Unicode has moved toward approving fewer emoji, favoring ones that have several meanings or fill a real gap, and expanding existing emoji through sequences instead of new characters. The previous release, in 2024, was similarly small; its best-known addition was the [face with bags under eyes](/emojis/face-with-bags-under-eyes/).

Every emoji in the 17.0 set went through the same route: a written proposal with evidence of expected use, review, a published candidate list, and final approval about two years after submission. The full process, including what gets rejected, is in [how new emojis get approved](/guides/how-new-emojis-get-approved/), and the longer backstory is in the [history of emoji](/guides/history-of-emoji/).

## What comes after Emoji 17.0

Unicode works on a yearly cycle, with each new version normally finalized in September and reaching phones over the following months. Candidate lists for upcoming versions are published in advance, but candidates can be renamed, redesigned or dropped before approval, and vendor artwork is not final until it ships.

For that reason, treat any "leaked new emojis" image with caution, especially ones showing polished Apple-style artwork for emoji that have not been approved. Those are usually mockups made by fans or reference sites. The authoritative sources are the [Unicode emoji pages](https://home.unicode.org/emoji/) and the [full emoji list](https://unicode.org/emoji/charts/full-emoji-list.html), which is updated when a version is released.

## Quick reference: using the new emojis well

| If you want to say | Use | Instead of |
|---|---|---|
| "My brain just broke" | Distorted face | Melting face (too calm) |
| "We bickered, nothing serious" | Fight cloud | Angry face (too harsh) |
| "Haven't been seen in weeks" | Hairy creature | Ghost (implies ghosting someone) |
| "Well, that was an anticlimax" | Trombone | Clown face (insulting) |
| "Found a hidden gem" | Treasure chest | Gem stone (reads as luxury) |
| "I have nothing left" | Apple core | Skull (reads as laughing) |
| "It all went downhill" | Landslide plus a word of context | Landslide alone (ambiguous) |

And a three-point check before you send one:

1. **Updated?** If you cannot find it in your keyboard, update your operating system first.
2. **Will they see it?** With people on older phones, add a word so the message survives a blank box.
3. **Is the meaning settled?** These emoji are new enough that readers may take them literally. Give them context until usage catches up.
