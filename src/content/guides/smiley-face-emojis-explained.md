The smile emojis differ mainly in two things: whether the eyes are smiling and how wide the mouth is open. 🙂 is a small, neutral smile that can read as polite or cold; 😊 and ☺️ are warm, blushing smiles; 😀, 😃, and 😄 are open-mouth grins of rising enthusiasm; and 😁 is a toothy beam that covers both excitement and awkwardness. If you only remember one rule, pick a face with curved eyes when you want to sound warm.

The rest of this guide compares them in detail, with example messages for each.

## Quick comparison of the main smiles

| Emoji | Official name | Eyes | Mouth | Reads as | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🙂 | Slightly smiling face | Open ovals | Small closed curve | Polite, calm, neutral | Can look strained or sarcastic |
| 😊 | Smiling face with smiling eyes | Curved, closed | Closed smile, blush | Warm, pleased, grateful | Very little; mildly bashful |
| ☺️ | Smiling face | Curved, closed | Small smile, blush | Gentle, content, touched | May display as an outline |
| 😀 | Grinning face | Round, open | Open, teeth showing | Cheerful, upbeat | Can look fixed or generic |
| 😃 | Grinning face with big eyes | Tall, wide open | Open | Eager, bright, surprised-happy | Can seem over-keen |
| 😄 | Grinning face with smiling eyes | Curved, closed | Open | Delighted, laughing lightly | Very little |
| 😁 | Beaming face with smiling eyes | Curved, closed | Full row of teeth | Thrilled, proud, or sheepish | Mistaken for a grimace |
| 😆 | Grinning squinting face | Scrunched shut | Open wide | Laughing hard | Too much for mild humor |
| 😅 | Grinning face with sweat | Curved, closed | Open, sweat drop | Relief, nervous laughter | Can look like dodging blame |

The names in the table are the current short names from Unicode's CLDR project, which is what screen readers read aloud. Several were changed over the years. 😁, for example, was originally called "grinning face with smiling eyes," the name 😄 carries today, which is one reason older articles about these faces contradict each other.

## Why the eyes decide everything

In real faces, people judge whether a smile is sincere largely by the eyes: a felt smile narrows them and lifts the cheeks, while a posed one moves only the mouth. Emoji designers draw on the same instinct. The faces fall into three groups.

- **Open, round eyes (🙂 😀 😃).** The mouth smiles; the eyes stay neutral. The result is friendly on the surface and slightly unreadable underneath. These are the smiles that get accused of being fake.
- **Curved "smiling" eyes (😊 ☺️ 😄 😁 😅).** The eyes are drawn as upward arcs, the cartoon shorthand for a face that is smiling all over. These read as sincere almost regardless of context.
- **Scrunched eyes (😆).** Drawn like the symbols > and <, they show a face squeezed shut with laughter.

Blush works as a second signal. Pink cheeks on 😊 and ☺️ add a note of pleasure or shyness, which is why those two feel personal and the plain grins feel public.

Mouth width then sets the volume. A closed mouth is a quiet smile; an open mouth is a laugh starting; visible teeth are either a wide laugh or tension. Once you look at eyes, cheeks, and mouth separately, the whole family becomes predictable.

## 🙂 The slightly smiling face

The [slightly smiling face](/emojis/slightly-smiling-face/) is the minimum viable smile: open eyes, a short curve for a mouth. It arrived in 2014, several years after most of the other smileys, to fill the gap between the neutral face 😐 and the full grins.

Used sincerely, it is calm and courteous:

- "Sounds good, see you then 🙂"
- "Thanks for the update 🙂"

The trouble is that it does only just enough to count as a smile. When the words are short or the situation is tense, that restraint looks deliberate:

- "Fine 🙂"
- "As I said earlier 🙂"

Many younger users now treat it as the default face for irony or contained annoyance, while many older users send it as their everyday friendly smile. Neither reading is wrong, which is exactly what makes it risky between generations. The full story of how it and a few other emojis picked up a cold reputation is in [passive-aggressive emojis](/guides/passive-aggressive-emojis/).

Its mirror image, the [upside-down face](/emojis/upside-down-face/) 🙃, is the same drawing flipped over, and it has lost its literal meaning almost entirely. It signals irony, silliness, or "everything is going wrong and I am smiling anyway."

**Use 🙂 when** you want a light, neutral touch with someone who uses it the same way. **Skip it when** the message could already be taken as curt.

## 😊 and ☺️ The blushing smiles

### 😊 Smiling face with smiling eyes

The [smiling face with smiling eyes](/emojis/smiling-face-with-smiling-eyes/) is the most reliable friendly emoji in the set. Curved eyes and rosy cheeks make it look pleased, and there is not much sarcasm you can hang on it.

- "Thank you so much 😊"
- "Happy to help 😊"
- "That made my day 😊"

It suits thanks, compliments, good wishes, and any message where 🙂 might be misread. The blush gives it a faintly bashful quality, so in flirting it reads as sweet and interested without being forward; [the flirty emojis guide](/guides/flirty-emojis-guide/) places it at the gentle end of the scale.

### ☺️ Smiling face

The [smiling face](/emojis/smiling-face/) looks like a sibling of 😊, and on most platforms the two are close: closed, curved eyes, pink cheeks, a modest smile. ☺️ tends to be drawn a touch softer and smaller in the mouth, and some designs give it eyebrows.

Its history is different, though. The character has been in Unicode since the early 1990s as a plain black-and-white symbol, long before phones had emoji keyboards, and it was later adopted into the emoji set. Because of that origin, it relies on an invisible "variation selector" to request the color version. Where an app or font ignores the selector, you see a thin outlined face ☺ instead of the yellow one. That is the main practical difference between the two.

In tone, ☺️ is gentle and content. People use it for being touched or quietly happy: "That's so kind of you ☺️." If you want a guarantee that the yellow, blushing face shows up everywhere, send 😊.

## 😀 😃 😄 The three open-mouth grins

These three are easiest to understand as a ladder.

**😀 [Grinning face](/emojis/grinning-face/).** Round open eyes, open mouth with teeth. This is the generic happy face, the one that represents "emoji" on keyboard tabs and in app icons. It says cheerful and upbeat: "Good morning 😀." Because the eyes do not join in, some people find it a little fixed, like a smile held for a photo. It is rarely read as hostile, only as plain. A small historical oddity: although it sits first in the emoji list, it was added to Unicode in 2012, more than a year after 😁, 😃, and 😄.

**😃 [Grinning face with big eyes](/emojis/grinning-face-with-big-eyes/).** The eyes are taller and wider, which adds energy and a hint of surprise. It suits eagerness and bright greetings: "You're coming? 😃" or "Oh, that's a great idea 😃." In a calm conversation it can come across as a notch too enthusiastic.

**😄 [Grinning face with smiling eyes](/emojis/grinning-face-with-smiling-eyes/).** The same open mouth with curved, closed eyes. This is 😊 turned up: fully happy, bordering on a laugh. "We got the tickets 😄" or "Haha, that's brilliant 😄." Because the eyes are smiling, it is the least ambiguous of the three and a good default when you are happy and want it to show.

In practice many people pick one of these three and use it for everything, and recipients rarely notice the difference. The distinction matters most when you want to avoid seeming flat (prefer 😄) or when you want to show eagerness (😃).

## 😁 The beaming face

The [beaming face with smiling eyes](/emojis/beaming-face-with-smiling-eyes/) pairs smiling eyes with a wide mouth full of teeth, top and bottom rows together. It has two established uses.

1. **Unrestrained delight or pride.** "Passed my driving test 😁" or "Guess who got the job 😁."
2. **A sheepish, cheeky, or awkward grin.** "I may have eaten the last slice 😁" or "So... about that deadline 😁."

The second meaning comes straight from the teeth. Clenched teeth are also the defining feature of the [grimacing face](/emojis/grimacing-face/) 😬, and the two can blur. This was a well-known problem with Apple's earlier design, which looked so tense that many iPhone users sent it as a grimace while people on other platforms received a happy grin. A 2016 study by the GroupLens research lab at the University of Minnesota, which asked people to rate how positive or negative various emoji renderings were, found this face to be among those interpreted most differently from platform to platform. Apple has since redrawn it, and current designs are unmistakably happy, but the awkward-grin usage survives as a habit.

Send it to friends freely. Be more careful in apologies or bad news, where a big toothy grin can look as though you are not taking the matter seriously.

## 😆 and 😅 Where smiling turns into laughing

Two more faces sit on the border between smile and laugh.

The [grinning squinting face](/emojis/grinning-squinting-face/) 😆 has eyes screwed shut. It is a real laugh, lighter than 😂: "You did not just say that 😆." It works well as a reaction to a friend's joke and is seldom misread.

The [grinning face with sweat](/emojis/grinning-face-with-sweat/) 😅 adds a single drop of sweat to 😄. It means relief or nervous laughter: "Made the train with ten seconds to spare 😅" or "Sorry, just saw this 😅." It softens small confessions well. Leaned on too often in apologies, it starts to look like laughing off responsibility.

Beyond these, laughter has its own vocabulary with its own generational politics; [laughing and crying emojis](/guides/laughing-and-crying-emojis/) compares 😂, 🤣, 😭, and 💀.

## Other smiles in the family

Several faces have "smiling" in their names but do specialized jobs.

- **😇 [Smiling face with halo](/emojis/smiling-face-with-halo/):** innocence, usually tongue in cheek. "Who, me? 😇"
- **🥰 [Smiling face with hearts](/emojis/smiling-face-with-hearts/):** affection, feeling loved or adoring something.
- **🤗 [Smiling face with open hands](/emojis/smiling-face-with-open-hands/):** a hug, a warm welcome, or excitement. The hands are drawn differently across platforms, and some people read them as "jazz hands."
- **😌 [Relieved face](/emojis/relieved-face/):** a closed-eye, contented smile; calm, peace, satisfaction.
- **😏 [Smirking face](/emojis/smirking-face/):** a one-sided smile; smugness, innuendo, flirtation.
- **🥲 [Smiling face with tear](/emojis/smiling-face-with-tear/):** smiling through sadness, pride mixed with pain, or grateful tears.
- **🫠 [Melting face](/emojis/melting-face/):** a smile dissolving into a puddle; embarrassment, heat, or quiet dread.

None of these substitutes for a plain friendly smile, and all of them can be found alongside the rest under [smileys and emotion](/categories/smileys-and-emotion/).

## Choosing a smile by situation

**Thanking someone.** 😊 is the standard. "Thanks 🙂" is fine with people who use 🙂 themselves; "Thanks 😊" is fine with everyone.

**At work.** 🙂 and 😊 for routine politeness, 😄 for good news. Keep 😁 and 😅 for colleagues you know well. Conventions vary by team and tool, which [emojis at work etiquette](/guides/emojis-at-work-etiquette/) covers in more detail.

**With family, especially older relatives.** Expect 🙂 and 😀 to be meant warmly. A parent who ends every message with 🙂 is smiling at you.

**With a crush.** 😊 signals warmth and a little shyness; ☺️ leans tender; 😄 shows you enjoyed the joke. 😏 changes the subject entirely.

**Softening a correction or refusal.** This is where people get into trouble. "I can't make it 🙂" can sound clipped. Adding words works better than swapping emojis: "I can't make it, sorry! Next time 😊."

**Responding to bad news.** No smile. Even 🙂 can look dismissive beside someone's bad day.

## How smiles change across platforms and over time

Unicode defines each emoji as a character with a name, and each company draws its own picture. So the 🙂 you send from a Samsung phone is rendered by the recipient's device in Apple's, Google's, or Microsoft's style, and apps such as WhatsApp use their own artwork on some platforms. Vendors have gradually brought their smiley designs closer together, which has reduced the worst mismatches, but small differences in eye shape and mouth curve remain. For subtle smiles those small differences are the whole message. [iPhone vs. Android emojis](/guides/iphone-vs-android-emojis/) shows where the designs still diverge.

Meanings also drift. The flat reading of 🙂 and the awkward reading of 😁 both grew out of usage, not out of anything Unicode specified. The broader pattern of how context changes what an emoji means is covered in [emoji meanings in texting](/guides/emoji-meanings-in-texting/).

## A rule of thumb for picking the right smile

Run through three questions and you will rarely send the wrong face.

1. **Do I want warmth or just politeness?** Warmth: curved eyes (😊 😄). Politeness: 🙂, if the reader uses it too.
2. **How loud is the feeling?** Quiet: closed mouth (😊 ☺️). Happy: open mouth (😄). Thrilled: teeth (😁). Laughing: scrunched eyes (😆).
3. **Could the words be read as curt without the emoji?** If yes, fix the words first, then add 😊.

| If you mean | Send | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Thank you, sincerely | 😊 | 🙂 with a one-word message |
| Great news! | 😄 or 😁 | 😀 if you want to sound moved |
| I'm touched | ☺️ or 😊 | 😁 |
| Oops, my fault | 😅 | 😁 in a serious setting |
| Polite acknowledgment | 🙂 or 😊 | 😃 |
| That's funny | 😆 or 😄 | 🙂 |

When in doubt, 😊 is the answer. It is the one smile that nearly everyone, on nearly every device, reads the way you meant it.
