Snapchat friend emojis are small symbols the app places next to a friend's name to describe how the two of you use Snapchat together. Hearts and smiley faces rank best friends, the fire emoji tracks a Snapstreak, and the hourglass warns that a streak is about to die. Snapchat assigns them automatically from your Snap and Chat activity, they update on their own, and only you can see the ones on your list.

That last point matters more than any single definition. These emojis are not messages from your friend. Nobody chose to put a [yellow heart](/emojis/yellow-heart/) next to your name; an algorithm did, based on counts. Reading them the way you would read an emoji someone typed, as covered in our guide to [emoji meanings in texting](/guides/emoji-meanings-in-texting/), leads to the wrong conclusions.

## Where friend emojis appear and who sees them

Friend emojis show up on the Chat screen, to the right of a friend's name, and in the list you see when choosing who to send a Snap to. A friend can have several at once: a heart and a fire emoji with a number is a very common pair.

Three rules explain most of the confusion:

- **They are private.** Your friend emojis are visible only to you. Snapchat does not show anyone a list of your best friends.
- **Some are mutual, some are one-sided.** The hearts require both people to rank each other first, so if you see a heart, your friend sees one too. Others, like the smirk, describe only your side of the relationship.
- **They are customizable.** Any default can be replaced with a different emoji in settings. If your friend says you have a taco next to your name, that is why.

Snapchat describes Best Friends as the friends you Snap and Chat with the most, and you can have up to eight of them. The company does not publish the exact formula, so anyone quoting precise point values is guessing. What is observable is that the list responds to recent activity: a week of heavy Snapping with someone new can reshuffle it.

## The full friend emoji chart

These are the default emojis and their official meanings. If you or your friend have customized emojis, the symbol will differ but the relationship behind it is the same.

| Emoji | Snapchat's name | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 💛 | Besties | You are each other's number one best friend |
| ❤️ | BFF | You have been each other's number one best friend for two weeks in a row |
| 💕 | Super BFF | You have been each other's number one best friend for two months in a row |
| 😊 | BFs | This person is one of your best friends, but not number one |
| 😎 | Mutual Besties | One of your best friends is also one of their best friends |
| 😬 | Mutual BFs | Your number one best friend is also their number one best friend |
| 😏 | Their BF, not yours | You are one of their best friends, but they are not one of yours |
| 🔥 | Snapstreak | You have Snapped each other every day; the number is the count of days |
| ⌛ | Streak ending | The Snapstreak is about to expire |
| 💯 | 100-day streak | Shown beside the fire on the day a streak reaches 100 |
| 🎂 | Birthday | Today is this friend's birthday, if they added it to their profile |
| 👶 | New friend | You recently became friends on Snapchat |
| 📌 | Pinned | You pinned this conversation to the top of your Chat screen |

Snapchat has also used ✨ for group chats and 🌟 to show that someone replayed a friend's Snap in the last 24 hours. Emojis like these have come and gone across app versions, so you may or may not see them in your build.

## The heart ladder: yellow, red, pink

The three hearts are one relationship at three ages. They all mean the same thing, that each of you sends more Snaps to the other than to anyone else, and they differ only in how long that has been true.

1. **[Yellow heart](/emojis/yellow-heart/) (Besties).** Appears as soon as you become each other's number one.
2. **[Red heart](/emojis/red-heart/) (BFF).** Replaces the yellow heart once you have held the mutual top spot for two weeks straight.
3. **[Two pink hearts](/emojis/two-hearts/) (Super BFF).** Replaces the red heart after two months straight.

The ladder has no memory. If either of you starts Snapping someone else more, even for a few days, the heart can vanish, and when you regain the top spot you start again at yellow. This is why a red heart sometimes drops back to yellow after a vacation with other friends: nothing went wrong between the two of you, the counts simply moved.

Outside Snapchat, heart colors carry social meanings people argue about, such as yellow for friendship and red for romance. Snapchat's ladder ignores all of that. A red heart next to a coworker means only that you two Snap each other a lot. ## The smiley faces: best friends who are not number one

The four face emojis describe the looser parts of your best friends list, and two of them tell you something about a third person.

### Smiling face: one of your best friends

The [smiling face with smiling eyes](/emojis/smiling-face-with-smiling-eyes/) marks someone on your best friends list who is not your number one. You Snap them often, just not the most. Most people have several of these.

### Sunglasses: a shared best friend

The [face with sunglasses](/emojis/smiling-face-with-sunglasses/) means you and this person have a best friend in common. Somebody who is close to the top of your list is also close to the top of theirs. It usually shows up inside a friend group where everyone Snaps the same few people.

### Grimace: you share a number one

The [grimacing face](/emojis/grimacing-face/) is the awkward one. It means your number one best friend is also their number one best friend: the person you send the most Snaps to is the same person they send the most Snaps to. The emoji choice is a small joke about competing for the same friend. It often appears next to your best friend's partner, sibling or roommate.

### Smirk: they Snap you more than you Snap them

The [smirking face](/emojis/smirking-face/) means you are one of their best friends, but they are not one of yours. Elsewhere the smirk is a flirty or smug emoji, which makes this one easy to misread. On Snapchat it is purely arithmetic: they send you plenty, and you send more to other people.

## Fire, hourglass and 100: how Snapstreaks work

The [fire emoji](/emojis/fire/) appears next to a friend once the two of you have each sent the other a Snap within 24 hours for at least three consecutive days. The number beside it is the length of the streak in days. It rises by one for every day both of you keep it going.

### What counts toward a streak

Only direct photo or video Snaps count. According to Snapchat's own support pages, the following do not:

- Text chats, voice notes and stickers sent in Chat
- Snaps sent to a group chat
- Posts to your Story, even if the friend views them
- Snaps sent from Memories or from Spectacles

Both people have to send. If you send a Snap every day and your friend only opens them, the streak ends. The content is irrelevant, which is why long streaks are often maintained with a photo of a ceiling and the letter S.

### The hourglass warning

The [hourglass](/emojis/hourglass-not-done/) appears beside the fire when the 24-hour window is close to running out. Snapchat does not state exactly how much time is left when it appears; users widely report a few hours. The safe reading is that you should send a Snap now and nudge your friend to do the same, because the hourglass stays until both sides have sent one.

### The 100 and long streaks

On the day a streak hits 100, the [hundred points emoji](/emojis/hundred-points/) appears next to the fire for that day, then the display returns to the fire and the number. You will see claims online about special emojis for extremely long streaks. Snapchat's documentation does not list any, so treat those as rumor unless you see one yourself.

### If a streak ends by mistake

When a streak expires, Snapchat usually shows a Restore option in that chat or on the friend's row for a limited time. How many restores are free has changed more than once, and at the time of writing Snapchat+ subscribers get more generous terms than free accounts, with extra restores sold as an in-app purchase. If you believe the streak ended because of an app error rather than a missed day, Snapchat Support has a form for reporting a lost Snapstreak. Neither route is guaranteed, so the reliable protection is not letting the hourglass sit.

## Birthday cake, baby, pin and zodiac signs

A few emojis have nothing to do with ranking.

- **[Birthday cake](/emojis/birthday-cake/).** Shown on a friend's birthday, but only if they entered a birth date and left the birthday setting switched on. No cake does not prove it is not their birthday.
- **Baby.** Marks a friend you added recently. It disappears on its own after a short time.
- **[Pushpin](/emojis/pushpin/).** Appears on conversations you pinned yourself by pressing and holding the chat and choosing the pin option. The number of chats you can pin is limited.
- **Zodiac symbols.** Open a friend's profile and you may see a small astrological sign, such as [Leo](/emojis/leo/) or [Scorpio](/emojis/scorpio/), derived from their birthday. Tapping it opens Snapchat's astrology features. These live on profiles rather than in the chat list and are not friend emojis in the strict sense.

## Icons that look like friend emojis but are not

The Chat screen also uses colored arrows and squares to show message status. People often search for these alongside friend emojis, so here is the short version.

| Icon | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Red arrow or square | A Snap without audio |
| Purple arrow or square | A Snap with audio |
| Blue arrow or square | A text chat |
| Filled arrow | You sent it and it has been delivered |
| Hollow arrow | Your friend opened it |
| Filled square | You have something unopened from them |
| Gray arrow | Pending, often because the person has not accepted your friend request |

Arrows are things you sent; squares are things you received. The double-arrow icons mean a screenshot was taken or a Snap was replayed.

## How to change your friend emojis

Every default in the chart can be replaced. The menu location has moved between versions, but the route is consistently through settings:

1. Open Snapchat and tap your profile icon in the top corner.
2. Tap the gear icon to open Settings.
3. On Android, look for **Customize Emojis**. On iPhone, scroll to **Additional Services**, tap **Manage**, then **Friend Emojis**.
4. Tap a relationship, for example Snapstreak or Super BFF.
5. Pick the emoji you want. It applies immediately.
6. To undo everything, use **Reset to default** at the bottom of the list.

Popular swaps include replacing the streak fire with a [sparkles emoji](/emojis/sparkles/) or a [crown](/emojis/crown/) for the Super BFF slot. The change is cosmetic and local: your friend still sees whatever their own settings specify. Snapchat+ adds further options, including pinning one friend as your number one and assigning custom emojis to individual friends. The paid feature list changes often, so check the Snapchat+ screen in your app for what is currently included.

## What friend emojis cannot tell you

Because the emojis look emotional, people use them to answer emotional questions. Most of those readings do not hold up.

- **A missing heart is not a breakup signal.** It means one of you Snapped someone else more this week. A group trip, a new job or a new friend is enough.
- **The smirk does not mean someone is into you.** It means they send you more than you send them. Whether that is interest, habit or streak maintenance, the emoji cannot say.
- **The grimace does not show who someone else's partner is.** It shows that two people Snap the same third person the most.
- **No emoji shows blocking or removal.** If a friend's emojis and Snap score vanish and your Snaps stay gray and pending, they may have removed you, but the emojis themselves are silent on it.
- **Emojis lag.** They update periodically rather than the instant a Snap is sent, so a change you expect may take a while to show.

If you want to know how someone feels, the emojis they type are better evidence than the ones Snapchat assigns. ## A 30-second decoder

When an unfamiliar emoji shows up next to a name, run through these questions in order.

1. **Is it a heart?** You are each other's number one. Yellow is new, red is two weeks, pink is two months.
2. **Is it a face?** Smile means a best friend, sunglasses means a shared best friend, grimace means a shared number one, smirk means they Snap you more than you Snap them.
3. **Is it fire with a number?** That is a streak and its length in days. An hourglass next to it means send a Snap now.
4. **Is it a cake, a baby or a pin?** Birthday, new friend, or a chat you pinned.
5. **Is it none of the above?** Someone customized their emojis, probably you. Check the Friend Emojis list in settings to see which relationship the symbol is attached to.
