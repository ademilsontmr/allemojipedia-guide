Lent and Easter are written in emoji with a small, steady set of symbols: the [Latin cross](/emojis/latin-cross/) ✝️, [folded hands](/emojis/folded-hands/) 🙏 and a [purple heart](/emojis/purple-heart/) 💜 for the forty days of Lent, a palm tree 🌴 for Palm Sunday, a quiet ✝️ and candle 🕯️ for Good Friday, and ✝️ 🌅 🕊️ 🤍 ✨ for Easter morning. The main rule is to let the emojis follow the mood of the day: restrained through Lent and Holy Week, joyful from Easter Sunday on.

This guide walks through the season in order, using the 2027 dates on the Western (Latin) calendar: Ash Wednesday on February 10, Palm Sunday on March 21, Good Friday on March 26, Easter Sunday on March 28, and Pentecost on May 16. For the symbols themselves and their history, see the companion guide to [Christian emojis and their meanings](/guides/christian-emojis-meanings/); this one focuses on what to post, and when.

Easter moves every year, and Ash Wednesday and Pentecost move with it. Eastern Orthodox churches calculate the date on the Julian calendar; in 2027 Orthodox Pascha falls on May 2.

## Ash Wednesday: expressing ashes without an ashes emoji

Ash Wednesday opens Lent. Catholics and many Anglicans, Lutherans and Methodists receive ashes on the forehead, usually traced in the shape of a cross, while the minister says "Remember that you are dust, and to dust you shall return" or "Repent, and believe in the Gospel."

There is **no ashes emoji**. Unicode has never encoded an ash smudge or a forehead cross. People work around it in a few common ways:

- **The cross stands in for the ashes.** "Ashes on ✝️" or simply "✝️ #AshWednesday" is the most common pattern.
- **Grey and black for the ash itself.** The [grey heart](/emojis/grey-heart/) 🩶, the black circle ⚫ or the fog 🌫️ hint at the color and texture. They work best next to ✝️, since alone they read as moody or minimalist.
- **Purple for the season.** A 💜 says "Lent has started" to anyone who knows the liturgical colors.
- **A photo does the rest.** With a photo of your ashes, the caption only needs a cross and a line of text.

Caption ideas for the day:

- "Remember that you are dust, and to dust you shall return. ✝️🩶"
- "Ashes on, phone down for 40 days 💜🙏"
- "Ash Wednesday Mass at 7 pm, all welcome ⛪✝️"

Avoid jokes about "forgetting to wash your face" unless you know the audience well, and avoid party emojis: Ash Wednesday is a day of fasting and abstinence for Catholics, so 🍕 or 🍔 in a caption can read as a pointed joke.

## Purple and the forty days of Lent

Purple, called violet in church usage, is the color of Lent in the Western churches. It signals penance, waiting and preparation, which is why the purple heart 💜 has become the season's emoji shorthand. You will see it in parish posts and bios: "✝️💜 Lent 2027."

A few details that make posts more accurate:

- **Forty days.** Lent recalls the forty days Jesus spent fasting in the desert, which is why the desert 🏜️ shows up in Lenten captions. The count is traditional; Sundays are not treated as fast days.
- **Laetare Sunday.** The fourth Sunday of Lent (March 7, 2027) takes its name from the Latin opening of the Mass, "Rejoice." Priests may wear rose vestments, so a 🌸 or pink heart 🩷 next to 💜 is a knowing touch that day.
- **No Alleluia.** In the Latin rite, the Alleluia is not sung or said during Lent. Some people keep it out of their posts until the Easter Vigil, too.
- **Hourglass.** The hourglass ⏳ is a simple way to count down: "Day 12 of 40 ⏳💜."

Outside church, 💜 alone usually reads as plain affection; the guide to [heart emoji colors](/guides/heart-emoji-colors-meaning/) covers the everyday readings.

## Prayer, fasting and almsgiving in emoji

The three traditional Lenten practices are prayer, fasting and almsgiving, drawn from Jesus' teaching in the Gospel of Matthew. Each has an emoji vocabulary of its own. If you want a fuller explanation of the rules and the spiritual reasoning behind them, this [complete Catholic Lent guide](https://guidecatholic.com/blog/complete-catholic-lent-guide/) goes through them step by step.

### Prayer

Folded hands 🙏, [prayer beads](/emojis/prayer-beads/) 📿 for the rosary, the open book 📖 for scripture reading, and the candle 🕯️. Many parishes hold the Stations of the Cross on Fridays in Lent; "Stations at 6 pm tonight ✝️🙏" is a typical announcement. There is no emoji for the stations themselves.

### Fasting and abstinence

For Catholics, Ash Wednesday and Good Friday are days of fasting (one full meal, with two smaller ones allowed, for ages 18 to 59) and abstinence from meat (from age 14). Every Friday of Lent is a day of abstinence. That is where the fish 🐟 and the Friday fish fry come in: "Fish fry Friday 🐟🍟" is a familiar parish post in the United States. Other churches have their own customs, and many Christians give up something specific, such as sweets, social media or coffee. The no entry sign ⛔ or prohibited sign 🚫 next to the item is the usual way to announce it: "🍫🚫 until Easter."

### Almsgiving

Giving to people in need. The handshake 🤝, open palms 🤲, heart hands 🫶 and, for collections, the money bag 💰 cover it. A food drive post might read "Lenten food drive: drop off canned goods by Palm Sunday 🥫🤲💜."

## Palm Sunday and the start of Holy Week

Palm Sunday (March 21, 2027) recalls Jesus entering Jerusalem on a donkey while the crowd spread palm branches and cloaks on the road. Churches bless palms and read the account of the Passion, so the day holds both welcome and the shadow of the coming week. The vestments are red.

The [palm tree](/emojis/palm-tree/) 🌴 is the standard emoji, even though it shows a whole tree rather than a branch; the herb 🌿 is the closest thing to a frond. The [donkey](/emojis/donkey/) 🫏, added in Emoji 15.0, finally gives the scene its animal. The crown 👑 recalls the crowd greeting a king, and [raising hands](/emojis/raising-hands/) 🙌 echoes the "Hosanna."

Captions:

- "Hosanna in the highest 🌴🙌"
- "Palm Sunday. Holy Week begins ✝️🌴"
- "Don't forget your palms after Mass 🌿⛪"

The palm tree also means vacations and beaches, so pair it with ✝️ or the word "Hosanna" to make the meaning clear.

## The Triduum: Holy Thursday to the Easter Vigil

The Easter Triduum, the "three days," runs from the evening Mass of the Lord's Supper on Holy Thursday to evening prayer on Easter Sunday. It is the center of the whole church year. For a deeper look at what each day commemorates, this guide to [the meaning of Holy Week](https://catholicbibleonline.com/blog/meaning-holy-week-complete-guide/) lays out the events in order.

### Holy Thursday (March 25, 2027)

The evening Mass commemorates the Last Supper, the institution of the Eucharist and the priesthood, and Jesus washing the feet of his disciples. Bread 🍞, the [wine glass](/emojis/wine-glass/) 🍷 and grapes 🍇 stand in for the Eucharist, since there is no chalice emoji, and the droplet 💧 with the foot 🦶 suggests the washing of feet. After the Mass, the altar is stripped and many parishes keep watch before the Blessed Sacrament late into the night; the candle 🕯️ and crescent moon 🌙 fit that vigil.

- "Do this in memory of me. Holy Thursday 🍞🍷🙏"
- "Watching one hour with him tonight 🕯️🌙"

### Holy Saturday and the Easter Vigil (March 27, 2027)

Holy Saturday is a day of waiting, with no Mass during the day. After nightfall the Easter Vigil begins in darkness with a new fire 🔥, from which the paschal candle 🕯️ is lit and carried into the church. The Exsultet is sung, the Alleluia returns, and in many parishes adults are baptized 💧 and received into the Church. A post from the Vigil can move from 🌑 to 🕯️ to ✨ as the night does.

## Good Friday: what to post and what not to post

Good Friday (March 26, 2027) commemorates the crucifixion and death of Jesus. Catholics fast and abstain from meat; there is no Mass, but there is a Celebration of the Lord's Passion in the afternoon, with the reading of the Passion, the veneration of the cross and Communion from hosts consecrated the day before. The vestments are red. Many Protestant churches hold services of their own, often in the afternoon or evening.

**What fits:** ✝️ above all, with 🙏 and 🕯️. A [black heart](/emojis/black-heart/) 🖤 for mourning or a [red heart](/emojis/red-heart/) ❤️ for love and sacrifice. The broken heart 💔 is used by some, though it can read as personal heartbreak. The wilted flower 🥀 and the mountain ⛰️ (Calvary) are less common but understood in context.

**What does not fit:** anything celebratory. [Party popper](/emojis/party-popper/) 🎉, 🥳, 🐣, 🐰, bunny ears filters and "Happy Good Friday" all clash with the day. The "Good" in the name is commonly explained as "holy." If you run a brand account, the safest choice is to post nothing promotional on Friday and save the egg hunt for Sunday.

Respectful captions:

- "It is finished. ✝️"
- "Good Friday. Greater love has no one than this ✝️❤️"
- "Veneration of the cross at 3 pm ✝️🕯️"

## Easter Sunday: He is risen

Easter Sunday (March 28, 2027) celebrates the resurrection of Jesus, the oldest and greatest Christian feast. The vestments are white or gold, the Alleluia rings out, and the emoji mood flips from muted to bright. This overview of how [Catholics celebrate Easter](https://guidecatholic.com/blog/catholic-easter/) explains the liturgy and the customs if you want the background.

The core set, and why each fits:

- **✝️ Latin cross.** The center of the feast, now as a sign of victory rather than mourning.
- **🌅 [Sunrise](/emojis/sunrise/).** The women found the tomb empty early in the morning, so dawn stands for the resurrection. Sunrise services are a long tradition in many churches.
- **🕊️ [Dove](/emojis/dove/).** Peace, the Holy Spirit and new life.
- **🤍 [White heart](/emojis/white-heart/).** The white of the Easter liturgy and of baptismal garments.
- **✨ [Sparkles](/emojis/sparkles/).** Light and glory; the most common "joy" accent.
- **💐 [Bouquet](/emojis/bouquet/) and 🌷.** Churches fill with lilies and spring flowers. There is no lily emoji, so the tulip, bouquet and cherry blossom 🌸 stand in.
- **🐣 🥚.** The hatching chick and the egg, symbols of new life that cross over into the cultural side of the holiday.

The traditional greeting is "Christ is risen!" answered by "He is risen indeed!" In text, people write it as a call and response in the group chat:

- "He is risen! ✝️🌅"
- "He is risen indeed! Alleluia! 🙌🤍"
- "Happy Easter from our family to yours ✝️💐🐣"
- "Alleluia is back 🎉✝️✨" (here a party popper is welcome)

On Orthodox Pascha, the greeting is "Christ is risen!" answered by "Truly he is risen!", and the [Orthodox cross](/emojis/orthodox-cross/) ☦️ often replaces ✝️, along with red eggs 🥚❤️, a well-known Orthodox custom.

## Religious and secular Easter emojis

Easter in many countries has two layers: the Christian feast and a spring holiday of candy, egg hunts and the Easter bunny. Both use emojis, and they mix freely, but it helps to know which is which.

| Emoji | Name | Religious reading | Secular reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✝️ | Latin cross | The resurrection | None; always religious |
| 🌅 | Sunrise | The empty tomb at dawn | Early morning, a nice view |
| 🕊️ | Dove | Holy Spirit, peace | Peace, weddings |
| 🐑 | Ewe | The Lamb of God | Spring farm animals |
| 🥚 | Egg | New life, the tomb | Egg hunts, dyeing eggs |
| 🐣 | Hatching chick | New life | Spring, cuteness |
| 🐇 🐰 | Rabbit, rabbit face | None in scripture | The Easter bunny, chocolate |
| 🍫 | Chocolate bar | None | Easter candy |
| 🌷 | Tulip | Easter flowers in church | Spring |

The [rabbit](/emojis/rabbit/) 🐇 and rabbit face 🐰 have no Christian meaning; the Easter bunny comes from European folk custom. That does not make them wrong to use, and Christian families send them all the time. But a post that uses only 🐰🥚🍫 reads as "spring candy holiday," while one that adds ✝️ or 🌅 reads as the feast. For audiences of mixed faiths, such as a workplace channel, the secular set plus "Happy Easter" is the inclusive default; the guide to [emojis at work](/guides/emojis-at-work-etiquette/) covers that kind of setting.

## The Easter season to Pentecost

Easter is not one day but a season of fifty days. The first eight days form the Octave of Easter, each one celebrated as a solemnity in the Catholic calendar. The Octave ends on the Second Sunday of Easter, which Catholics also call Divine Mercy Sunday (April 4, 2027); a red and white heart pair ❤️🤍 echoes the two rays in the Divine Mercy image.

Forty days after Easter comes the Ascension, Thursday, May 6, 2027, though in many dioceses, including most in the United States, its celebration is moved to the following Sunday. The cloud ☁️ and raising hands 🙌 are the usual emojis.

The season closes with Pentecost (May 16, 2027), which commemorates the Holy Spirit coming upon the apostles. The Acts of the Apostles describes a sound like a rushing wind and tongues of fire, so the emojis are:

- **🔥 [Fire](/emojis/fire/)** for the tongues of fire.
- **🕊️ Dove** for the Holy Spirit.
- **🌬️ Wind face** for the rushing wind.
- **❤️ Red heart** for the red vestments of the day.

"Come, Holy Spirit 🕊️🔥" is the classic caption, and it suits confirmations too, many of which are scheduled in the Easter season. Remember that 🔥 alone means "amazing" in everyday slang, so the dove beside it keeps the meaning clear.

## Quick rule of thumb for each day

Use this as a checklist before you post:

1. **Ash Wednesday:** ✝️ plus a purple or grey heart. No food jokes.
2. **Lent:** 💜 🙏 and a sign for what you gave up. Hold the Alleluia.
3. **Palm Sunday:** 🌴 with ✝️ or "Hosanna" so nobody thinks you are on vacation.
4. **Holy Thursday:** 🍞 🍷 🕯️. Quiet and grateful.
5. **Good Friday:** ✝️ and one or two muted symbols. No 🎉, no bunnies, nothing promotional.
6. **Easter Sunday:** ✝️ 🌅 🕊️ 🤍 ✨ 💐, and now the party popper is welcome.
7. **Easter season and Pentecost:** 🕊️ 🔥 ❤️, all the way to May 16.

If you are writing to someone whose beliefs you do not know, the secular spring set (🐣 🌷 🥚) with "Happy Easter" is friendly and safe. If you are writing to fellow believers, the cross and a line of scripture will always say more than a long string of symbols.
