On TikTok, emojis in the comments are mostly jokes, reactions, and coded slang, and many do not mean what they show. The skull and the crying face mean laughter, the moai means a deadpan stare, corn stands in for a word people avoid typing, and a blue baseball cap means "that's a lie." TikTok also has a set of hidden emojis of its own that appear when you type a code such as `[smile]` in square brackets.

This guide covers both: the comment-section vocabulary first, then the bracket codes and how to use them.

## Why TikTok's comment section has its own dialect

On most apps, comments respond to a post. On TikTok, comments are part of the entertainment. People open them to find the funniest reply, and the top comments, ranked largely by likes, often get as much attention as the video. That rewards short, sharp reactions, and emojis are the shortest available.

Three habits follow from that:

- **Emojis as punchlines.** A single well-chosen emoji is a complete comment. The skill is in choosing the one that captures the exact flavor of the reaction.
- **In-group codes.** Meanings are assigned by trends and spread through repetition. If you were not on the app that month, the emoji makes no sense, which is part of the appeal.
- **Avoiding certain words.** Many users believe that some words reduce a video's reach or attract moderation, so they substitute emojis and misspellings. This is known as algospeak.

The vocabulary overlaps heavily with what is covered in [Gen Z emoji slang](/guides/gen-z-emoji-slang/), since TikTok is where much of that slang took hold. The focus here is on what you will see in comments on the app itself.

## The core comment emojis

These are the ones you will meet on almost any popular video.

**💀 [Skull](/emojis/skull/).** "I'm dead": this is hilarious. The default TikTok laugh. Also used for secondhand embarrassment.

**😭 [Loudly crying face](/emojis/loudly-crying-face/).** Laughing so hard you are crying, or being overwhelmed by something cute, relatable, or absurd. Actual sadness is the least common meaning. The combination 😭🙏 is an exaggerated "please," as in begging someone to stop because it is too funny.

**😂 [Face with tears of joy](/emojis/face-with-tears-of-joy/).** Still laughter, but many younger users see it as dated. Its presence often says more about the commenter's age than about the video. [Laughing and crying emojis](/guides/laughing-and-crying-emojis/) traces that shift.

**🗿 [Moai](/emojis/moai/).** A blank, stony reaction. Used when something is awkward, unfunny, or when the commenter is performing stoic indifference.

**🤡 [Clown face](/emojis/clown-face/).** Someone is being foolish. Aimed at the creator, it is an insult. Aimed at oneself ("me thinking he'd text back 🤡"), it is self-mockery.

**🧢 Billed cap.** "Cap" is slang for a lie. A cap emoji alone means "you're lying," and "no 🧢" means "no lie, seriously."

**✨ [Sparkles](/emojis/sparkles/).** Wrapped around a word for emphasis that is usually sarcastic: "✨anxiety✨," "✨we don't talk about that✨."

**👁️👄👁️ Eye, [mouth](/emojis/mouth/), eye.** A blank, wide-eyed stare of shock or discomfort, built from three emojis.

**🧍 Person standing.** Awkwardly standing there with nothing to say. Posted when a video leaves the commenter stunned or uncomfortable.

**💅 [Nail polish](/emojis/nail-polish/).** Sass, confidence, and being unbothered.

**🫡 [Saluting face](/emojis/saluting-face/).** Mock-solemn respect or obedience: "understood, reporting for duty," or a tribute to someone who took a risk for the joke.

**🐐 [Goat](/emojis/goat/).** GOAT, "greatest of all time." High praise for an athlete, creator, or anyone who did something impressive.

**🔥 [Fire](/emojis/fire/)** and **🥶 [cold face](/emojis/cold-face/).** Both are praise. Fire means excellent. The cold face means someone is "cold," that is, impressively smooth or ruthless, and has nothing to do with temperature.

**👀 [Eyes](/emojis/eyes/).** Watching with interest, usually where drama or a reveal is expected.

## Combos and trend emojis

Much of TikTok's emoji slang is built from pairs, and many come from specific trends. A trend's peak can be short, but the combos tend to linger in comment sections long afterward.

| Combo | Meaning | Background |
|---|---|---|
| 🥺👉👈 | Shy, nervously asking for something | Pleading face with two fingers touching, popular since 2020 |
| 🤨📸 | "Caught in 4K": I saw that, and I have proof | Raised eyebrow plus a camera flash |
| 🤓☝️ | "Erm, actually": mocking a know-it-all correction | Nerd face with a raised finger |
| 🗣️🔥 | "Say it louder," someone is speaking truth, often ironic | Repeated in rows under bold statements |
| 🤫🧏 | "Mewing," a joke about silently flexing the jawline | Spread widely in 2024 |
| 💔🥀 | Mock-dramatic heartbreak or disappointment | Common in 2025 comment sections |
| ✍️✍️ | "Taking notes" | Under advice, or ironically under bad advice |
| 🪑 | "Laughing" (historical) | A 2021 in-joke replacing laughing emojis |
| 🅿️ | "Pushin P," keeping it real | From the 2022 Gunna song |

Notes on a few of these. The [pleading face](/emojis/pleading-face/) with the two [pointing](/emojis/backhand-index-pointing-right/) [fingers](/emojis/backhand-index-pointing-left/) imitates someone shyly pressing their index fingers together. The [face with raised eyebrow](/emojis/face-with-raised-eyebrow/) and [camera with flash](/emojis/camera-with-flash/) pair mimics snapping a photo of someone caught doing something questionable. The [nerd face](/emojis/nerd-face/) with an [index finger pointing up](/emojis/index-pointing-up/) is posted in reply to a pedantic comment, or preemptively by someone about to make one. The [speaking head](/emojis/speaking-head/) with fire began as real hype and is now mostly used with irony. The [shushing face](/emojis/shushing-face/) combo refers to "mewing," a tongue-posture practice that became a meme; the second emoji is the deaf person emoji, borrowed only because the hand appears to trace the jaw, a use many deaf users have objected to. The [broken heart](/emojis/broken-heart/) with a [wilted flower](/emojis/wilted-flower/) is attached to trivial complaints for comic melodrama. The [writing hand](/emojis/writing-hand/) says "noted."

The chair is worth remembering because it shows how arbitrary the system can be. Commenters agreed to use a chair instead of a laughing face purely to baffle outsiders, and for a while it worked. When you see a seemingly random object repeated down a comment section, a similar in-joke is the likeliest explanation.

## Algospeak: emojis that stand in for words

TikTok moderates content under its Community Guidelines, and creators widely believe that certain words can limit a video's distribution. TikTok does not publish a list of such words, so much of this is folk knowledge. Whatever the truth for any particular word, the result is a habit of swapping sensitive terms for emojis and soundalikes. The following are in common use; they are listed here so that you can recognize them.

**Sex and adult content**

- 🌽 [Ear of corn](/emojis/ear-of-corn/): pornography ("corn" for "porn").
- 🍆 [Eggplant](/emojis/eggplant/): penis.
- 🍑 [Peach](/emojis/peach/): buttocks.
- 💦 [Sweat droplets](/emojis/sweat-droplets/): sexual fluids or arousal.
- 🧠 [Brain](/emojis/brain/): oral sex, from the slang "giving brain." In most comments it still means intelligence, so context decides.

**Drugs**

- 🍃 [Leaf fluttering in wind](/emojis/leaf-fluttering-in-wind/), along with other leaf and tree emojis: marijuana.
- ❄️ [Snowflake](/emojis/snowflake/): cocaine.
- 💊 [Pill](/emojis/pill/): pills, prescription or otherwise.
- 🔌 Electric plug: a "plug," meaning a dealer or supplier. It can also mean any useful connection.

**Politics and identity**

- 🍉 [Watermelon](/emojis/watermelon/): solidarity with Palestinians. The fruit's red, green, white, and black match the Palestinian flag, and it has a history as a substitute symbol that predates TikTok.
- 🥷 Ninja: used by some commenters as a stand-in for a racial slur. It is offensive in that use.

Two cautions apply. First, every one of these also appears with its plain meaning; a snowflake under a ski video is a snowflake. Read the video and the surrounding comments before assuming. Second, parents and teachers sometimes encounter alarming lists of "secret emoji codes." The drug and sex meanings above are real slang, but a teenager posting a single emoji is far more likely to be using it literally or as a joke than making a coded transaction.

## TikTok's hidden emojis: the bracket codes

Separate from standard emojis, TikTok has a set of its own small illustrated faces that do not appear on the emoji keyboard. You produce them by typing a code word inside square brackets. They are sometimes called TikTok's secret emojis.

What they are, technically: they are not Unicode emojis. They are images that the TikTok app substitutes for a specific piece of text, in the same way that some Chinese apps, including WeChat, use bracketed codes for their sticker-like emoticons. Because they are not real characters, they exist only inside TikTok. Copy one into another app and you get the bracketed text.

### How to use them

1. Open the TikTok app on a phone and start a comment or a caption.
2. Type an opening square bracket, the code word in lower case, and a closing square bracket, with no spaces: `[smile]`.
3. The app converts the code into the graphic, either as you type or once the comment is posted.
4. Mix them with normal text and standard emojis as you like.

If a code stays as plain text, check the spelling first. Beyond that, support has varied by app version and platform, and the codes are less reliable on the TikTok website than in the mobile app. TikTok can also add or retire codes without announcement.

### Code list

The following codes are the widely documented set. The first group is drawn as flat, round faces in the style of simple stickers; the second group looks closer to conventional yellow emoji.

| Group | Codes |
|---|---|
| Round sticker faces | `[smile]` `[happy]` `[angry]` `[cry]` `[embarrassed]` `[surprised]` `[wronged]` `[shout]` `[flushed]` `[yummy]` `[complacent]` `[drool]` `[scream]` `[weep]` `[speechless]` `[funnyface]` `[laughwithtears]` `[wicked]` `[facewithrollingeyes]` `[sulk]` `[thinking]` |
| Yellow emoji-style faces | `[lovely]` `[greedy]` `[wow]` `[joyful]` `[hehe]` `[slap]` `[tears]` `[stun]` `[cute]` `[blink]` `[disdain]` `[astonish]` `[rage]` `[cool]` `[excited]` `[proud]` `[smileface]` `[evil]` `[angel]` `[laugh]` `[pride]` `[nap]` `[loveface]` `[awkward]` `[shock]` |

In practice a handful get most of the use. `[wronged]` (a pouting, hard-done-by face) fills the same role as 🥺. `[proud]` and `[complacent]` are smug. `[slap]` and `[speechless]` suit exasperated replies. `[cry]` and `[weep]` follow the same rule as 😭 and usually mean laughter or being overwhelmed.

They carry a slightly different tone from standard emojis. Using one shows familiarity with the app, and their cartoon style makes them read as playful and ironic. They are a poor choice for anything you want understood off TikTok, and because screen readers have no standard name to announce for them, they are less accessible than Unicode emojis. ## How to read an emoji you do not recognize

New meanings appear constantly, and no list stays complete. A method works better than a glossary.

1. **Check the video's topic.** A 🍃 under a cooking video is an herb. Under a video about a party, it may not be.
2. **Read the replies.** If thirty people have posted the same odd emoji, it is a trend or an in-joke, and someone in the replies has usually asked what it means.
3. **Look at repetition.** An emoji repeated three or more times is a reaction (laughing, hype, shock). A single emoji dropped into a sentence is more likely standing in for a word.
4. **Consider irony.** TikTok humor leans on saying the opposite. Hearts and sparkles next to a harsh sentence are sarcasm.
5. **Search the emoji in TikTok.** If it belongs to a trend, the top results will show the videos that started it.
6. **Do not assume permanence.** A meaning from two years ago may be dead or reversed.

It also helps to know that emoji designs differ between phones, so a face that looks one way on an iPhone may look slightly different on a Samsung; [iPhone vs. Android emojis](/guides/iphone-vs-android-emojis/) shows the variation. And TikTok slang spreads outward. Many of these uses now appear on Instagram, where the context is more compliment-driven, as the [Instagram emoji meanings guide](/guides/instagram-emoji-meanings/) explains, and in group chats, where older relatives may take them literally.

## Using TikTok emojis without embarrassing yourself

A few practical rules for commenting:

- **Match the room.** Scroll the top comments before posting. If everyone is using 💀, a row of 😂 will stand out.
- **One idea per comment.** The best comments are a short line and one emoji, or one emoji alone.
- **Do not force old trends.** The chair and "pushin P" are finished. Using them now reads as either nostalgia or a late arrival.
- **Avoid emojis that mock real groups.** Some combos borrow disability or ethnic imagery for a joke, and the people depicted have said so. Skipping those costs nothing.
- **Remember that comments are public.** Coded or not, they are attached to your account and can be screenshotted.
- **Keep algospeak for where it is needed.** Off TikTok, substituting 🌽 for a word confuses people and adds nothing.
