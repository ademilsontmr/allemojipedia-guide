The core Thanksgiving emojis are the turkey 🦃, the pie 🥧, the ear of corn 🌽, the fallen leaf 🍂, and folded hands 🙏 for gratitude. Copy them from the sets above, add two or three to the end of a greeting, and the message reads as Thanksgiving at a glance. US Thanksgiving 2026 is Thursday, November 26.

The rest of this guide explains what each emoji means, what to use for the dishes that have no emoji, and gives greetings and captions you can send as they are.

## What each Thanksgiving emoji means

Most of these emojis are ordinary food, animal, and nature symbols that become "Thanksgiving" through context. Next to a leaf or the word "thankful," a poultry leg is dinner; on its own in July it is just chicken.

| Emoji | Official name | What it says in late November |
|---|---|---|
| 🦃 | Turkey | Thanksgiving itself; the bird at the center of the table |
| 🍗 | Poultry leg | The cooked meal, "carving time," leftovers |
| 🥧 | Pie | Dessert: pumpkin, apple, pecan, or whatever your family bakes |
| 🌽 | Ear of corn | Harvest, autumn table decorations, corn dishes |
| 🍠 | Roasted sweet potato | Sweet potatoes or yams, often the casserole |
| 🥔 | Potato | Mashed potatoes |
| 🍞 🥖 🧈 | Bread, baguette, butter | Rolls and the bread basket |
| 🍂 | Fallen leaf | Fall in general; the most versatile seasonal emoji |
| 🍁 | Maple leaf | Autumn color; also Canada |
| 🎃 | Jack-o-lantern | Pumpkin, by stand-in |
| 🌾 | Sheaf of rice | Harvest, wheat, fields |
| 🙏 | Folded hands | Thanks, gratitude, saying grace |
| 🧡 🤎 | Orange heart, brown heart | Warm autumn-colored affection |
| 🏈 | American football | The Thanksgiving Day games |
| 🎈 | Balloon | The parade's giant character balloons |
| 😴 | Sleeping face | The post-dinner nap |

The [turkey](/categories/animals-and-nature/) emoji was added to Unicode in 2015 as one of a batch of animals, not as a holiday symbol, which is why it shows a live bird rather than a roast. Apple, Google, Samsung, and Microsoft all draw it as a standing turkey, most with its tail fanned out. If you want the cooked version, [face savoring food](/emojis/face-savoring-food/) 😋 plus 🍗 makes the point.

The [ear of corn](/emojis/ear-of-corn/) does double duty. It can mean corn on the cob, cornbread, or the dried decorative corn hung on doors in the fall. In a caption like "Table's set 🌽🍂🕯️" it reads as decoration rather than food.

The [fallen leaf](/emojis/fallen-leaf/) and the [maple leaf](/emojis/maple-leaf/) are the safest Thanksgiving emojis because they say "autumn" without saying anything about food, family, or faith. If you are unsure whether someone celebrates, a leaf is never wrong.

## Turkey, pie, and the dishes with no emoji

Several Thanksgiving staples are not in the Unicode emoji set as of Emoji 17.0. People improvise, and these stand-ins are widely understood.

- **Stuffing or dressing.** No emoji. 🍞 bread or 🥖 baguette gets the idea across.
- **Gravy.** No emoji. Some people use 🥣 bowl with spoon or 🫗 pouring liquid.
- **Cranberry sauce.** No cranberry emoji. 🍒 cherries or 🫐 blueberries are the closest berries; 🥫 canned food is the joke version for the jellied kind.
- **Green bean casserole.** 🫛 pea pod or 🫘 beans, depending on how literal you want to be.
- **Mac and cheese.** 🧀 cheese with 🍝 spaghetti, or 🧀 alone.
- **Pumpkin.** There is no plain pumpkin emoji. The [jack-o-lantern](/emojis/jack-o-lantern/) 🎃 has a carved face, but in November nobody reads it as Halloween when it sits next to 🥧.
- **Cornucopia.** No emoji. 🌽🍎🍇🍐 in a row does the same job.
- **Wishbone.** No emoji. 🦴 bone with 🤞 crossed fingers is the usual stand-in for making a wish.
- **Pilgrim hat.** No emoji, and the closest options (🎩 top hat) look nothing like one.

### Is 🥧 a slice or a whole pie?

Unicode names it simply "pie," and every major platform draws a whole pie seen from the side or slightly above, usually with a golden crust. There is no pie-slice emoji. The triangular slice you may be thinking of is 🍰, which Unicode calls shortcake and most platforms draw as a slice of layered cake with a strawberry on top. For Thanksgiving, stick with 🥧. It covers pumpkin, apple, pecan, and sweet potato pie, and adding 🎃 or 🍎 narrows it down.

## Autumn and harvest emojis

These emojis are about the season rather than the meal, which makes them useful for the whole of November, for decor photos, and for people who skip the turkey.

- **🍂 [Fallen leaf](/emojis/fallen-leaf/)** is the general "fall" emoji.
- **🍁 [Maple leaf](/emojis/maple-leaf/)** adds a single bright leaf; popular in Canada for obvious reasons.
- **🍃 [Leaf fluttering in wind](/emojis/leaf-fluttering-in-wind/)** is green, so it reads as breezy rather than autumnal. Use it next to the other two, not instead of them.
- **🌻 [Sunflower](/emojis/sunflower/)** shows up in harvest table arrangements and wreaths.
- **🍄 [Mushroom](/emojis/mushroom/)** suits foraging and cottagecore fall posts.
- **🌰 Chestnut** and **🥜 peanuts** cover nuts and pecan pie by stand-in.
- **☕ [Hot beverage](/emojis/hot-beverage/)** is coffee, tea, cider, or the pumpkin spice latte.
- **🕯️ [Candle](/emojis/candle/)** sets a cozy or formal table.

For more fruit, vegetables, and dishes, browse the [food and drink](/categories/food-and-drink/) category, where everything from 🥕 to 🍯 is listed.

## Gratitude and family emojis

Thanksgiving is the one holiday named after a feeling, and a few emojis carry that feeling better than any food.

**🙏 [Folded hands](/emojis/folded-hands/).** In the US this reads as "thank you" or "grateful" first and prayer second, which makes it a good fit for both secular and religious greetings. "So thankful for you all 🙏" works in any family chat. (In parts of East and South Asia the same gesture is a greeting or apology; the [hand gesture guide](/guides/hand-gesture-emojis-meaning/) covers those differences.)

**Autumn-colored hearts.** The [orange heart](/emojis/orange-heart/) 🧡 and [brown heart](/emojis/brown-heart/) 🤎 match the season and are softer than a red heart, so they work for friends, in-laws, and coworkers you like. A [yellow heart](/emojis/yellow-heart/) 💛 is friendly and warm without any romantic undertone. The [heart colors guide](/guides/heart-emoji-colors-meaning/) explains what each shade tends to signal.

**🫶 [Heart hands](/emojis/heart-hands/)** and **🤗 hugging face** say "love you, see you soon." **🫂 [People hugging](/emojis/people-hugging/)** is the more literal hug, good for reunions and for anyone spending the day apart from family.

**Travel.** Thanksgiving weekend is one of the busiest travel periods in the US, and messages about it lean on ✈️ [airplane](/emojis/airplane/), 🚗 car, and 🏡 house: "Landed ✈️ see you at dinner 🏡."

## Football, the parade, and the couch

For many households Thanksgiving Day has a schedule: the parade in the morning, dinner, then football.

- **🏈 [American football](/emojis/american-football/).** The NFL plays games on Thanksgiving every year, and the Detroit Lions and Dallas Cowboys traditionally host. "🏈🦃" is shorthand for the whole afternoon.
- **🎈 [Balloon](/emojis/balloon/).** Stands in for the giant character balloons of the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York. Add 🥁 and 🎺 for the marching bands.
- **📺 🛋️ 🍿** Watching from the couch.
- **😴 [Sleeping face](/emojis/sleeping-face/)** and **🥱 yawning face.** The food coma. 🫃 pregnant man is also used jokingly for a very full stomach; keep that one for friends who share your sense of humor.

Black Friday starts the next morning, and the emoji set shifts toward 🛍️ and 🏷️. That vocabulary has its own guide: [Black Friday emojis](/guides/black-friday-emojis/).

## Canadian Thanksgiving and other harvest holidays

Canada celebrates Thanksgiving on the second Monday of October, which fell on October 12 in 2026. The emojis are the same, with two additions: the [Canadian flag](/emojis/flag-canada/) 🇨🇦 and the maple leaf 🍁, which appears on that flag. "Happy Thanksgiving 🍁🦃" in October is right, not early.

Other harvest celebrations have their own emoji vocabularies. The Mid-Autumn Festival uses 🥮 moon cake and 🏮 red paper lantern, and Sukkot is often marked with 🌿 and 🍋. If a friend celebrates one of these, use its symbols rather than the turkey.

A note on history: the holiday's origin story centers on a 1621 harvest meal shared by the Plymouth colonists and the Wampanoag people. For many Native Americans the day is one of remembrance, and some observe it as a National Day of Mourning. You do not need to change your greeting, but avoid costume-style emojis and imagery (feathered headdresses, "pilgrims and Indians" combos) that turn real peoples into decorations. Food, leaves, family, and gratitude carry the holiday perfectly well.

## Making sure your emojis display and land well

**Designs vary by platform.** The turkey, pie, and sweet potato look noticeably different on Apple, Google, Samsung, and Microsoft devices, down to the color of the pie crust and the pose of the bird. The meaning survives, but a color-coordinated caption may not match on the other person's phone. [iPhone vs. Android emojis](/guides/iphone-vs-android-emojis/) shows why.

**Newer emojis can break.** 🫛 pea pod, 🫚 ginger root, and 🫃 pregnant man are newer additions. On an older phone they can appear as empty boxes; the [guide to emojis showing as boxes](/guides/why-emojis-show-as-boxes/) explains the fix. The family emoji 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 is a joined sequence and can split into four separate people on outdated software.

**Do not spell out the message in pictures.** Screen readers announce each emoji by name, so "🦃🦃🦃🦃" is heard as "turkey" four times. The [accessibility guide](/guides/emoji-accessibility-screen-readers/) has more on writing messages that work for everyone.

## A rule of thumb before you hit send

1. **Words first, emojis last.** "Happy Thanksgiving!" followed by two or three emojis reads better than emojis sprinkled through the sentence.
2. **Match the emoji to the relationship.** 🧡🦃 for family, 🫶🍂 for friends, 🍂 or 🙏 alone for coworkers and clients.
3. **When in doubt, use a leaf.** 🍂 and 🍁 are seasonal without assuming anything about how someone spends the day.
4. **Missing dish? Use a stand-in.** 🍞 for stuffing, 🍒 for cranberry sauce, 🎃 with 🥧 for pumpkin pie.
5. **Check the date for Canadians.** Their Thanksgiving was in October; on November 26 they are just having a Thursday.
