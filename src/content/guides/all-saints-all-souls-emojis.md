All Saints' Day (November 1) celebrates all the saints in heaven, and All Souls' Day (November 2) is a day of prayer for everyone who has died, so the emojis that suit them are quiet ones: the candle 🕯️, folded hands 🙏, the cross ✝️, a white heart 🤍, flowers 🌹 💐, and prayer beads 📿. The ones to avoid are the jokey and spooky symbols that fill the same week online, above all the skull 💀, which in chat usually means laughter rather than death.

This guide explains what each day commemorates in Catholic and other Christian traditions, which emojis fit which message, examples you can adapt for parish posts and for friends who are grieving, and how the two days relate to Halloween and Día de los Muertos.

## What the two days commemorate

**All Saints' Day, November 1.** The feast honors all the saints, the holy men and women the Church believes are with God in heaven. That includes the canonized saints with their own feast days, but the point of the day is the countless others who are not named on any calendar: grandparents, parishioners, martyrs whose names were never recorded. The Western Church has kept the feast on November 1 since the early Middle Ages. In the Catholic Church it is a solemnity and a holy day of obligation, though in the United States the obligation is lifted when the day falls on a Saturday or a Monday. The liturgical color is white, the color of joy and of the great feasts.

**All Souls' Day, November 2.** Formally the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed, this is a day of prayer for everyone who has died. In Catholic teaching, the prayers of the living can help souls still being purified in purgatory, and the day is devoted to that intercession. The custom of a yearly commemoration of the dead on this date is generally traced to Abbot Odilo of Cluny around the end of the 10th century and spread from his monasteries through the Western Church. The liturgical color is violet or black. Many Catholics visit and tidy family graves, have Masses offered for their loved ones, and treat the whole month of November as a time to pray for the dead.

**Other Christian traditions.** Many Anglican, Lutheran, and Methodist churches keep All Saints' Day, often on November 1 or the following Sunday, and some read aloud the names of members who died during the past year. The Church of England also keeps the Commemoration of the Faithful Departed on November 2. Eastern Orthodox churches observe an All Saints' Sunday on the first Sunday after Pentecost and pray for the dead on several Saturdays of the year rather than on November 2.

For the history, the Mass readings and the customs of both days in more depth, see Guide Catholic's [guide to All Saints' Day and All Souls' Day](https://guidecatholic.com/blog/all-saints-day-all-souls-day/) and Catholic Bible Online's [All Saints' Day feast guide](https://catholicbibleonline.com/blog/all-saints-day-catholic-feast-guide/).

| Emoji | Official name | How it reads around November 1-2 |
|---|---|---|

| ✝️ | Latin cross | Christian faith; explicitly religious posts |
| 😇 | Smiling face with halo | Saints, holiness; can read as playful elsewhere |

**The [candle](/emojis/candle/).** Lighting candles at graves is one of the oldest and most widely shared customs of these days. In Poland, families travel to cemeteries on November 1 and leave thousands of lit lanterns; in the Philippines, families gather at graves for Undas; in many parishes, a candle is lit as each name of the year's dead is read. 🕯️ is also respectful for any audience, religious or not.

**[Folded hands](/emojis/folded-hands/).** 🙏 is the standard sign for prayer in English-speaking contexts. It also means "please" or "thank you" in other settings, but next to 🕯️ or a name its meaning is clear. The many readings of this gesture are covered in [hand gesture emojis and their meanings](/guides/hand-gesture-emojis-meaning/).

**The [Latin cross](/emojis/latin-cross/) and [prayer beads](/emojis/prayer-beads/).** ✝️ and 📿 make a post explicitly Christian. 📿 was designed as a general prayer-bead symbol used by several religions, but Catholics commonly use it for the rosary. Both are right for a parish post and for a friend you know shares the faith.

**The [smiling face with halo](/emojis/smiling-face-with-halo/).** 😇 is the natural "saint" emoji, but in daily chat it often means "innocent, who, me?" in a teasing way. On All Saints' Day, next to ✨ or 👑, it reads correctly; in a condolence message it can come across as flippant, so leave it out there.

**Flowers.** The [rose](/emojis/rose/) 🌹 and the [bouquet](/emojis/bouquet/) 💐 stand for the flowers carried to cemeteries. In much of Europe, chrysanthemums are the traditional grave flower in early November; there is no chrysanthemum emoji, so 🌼 or 🏵️ stand in. The [wilted flower](/emojis/wilted-flower/) 🥀 suggests grief, but it also carries a moody, heartbroken meaning online, so use it sparingly.

**The [headstone](/emojis/headstone/).** 🪦 was added in 2020 and shows a rounded gravestone. It fits "visiting Grandpa's grave today 🪦💐," but on its own it can look stark, and it is also used ironically ("my social life 🪦"). Pair it with flowers or a candle.

**The [white heart](/emojis/white-heart/).** 🤍 matches the white of All Saints' Day and has become a common heart for remembrance and sympathy. The full range of heart colors and what they signal is in [heart emoji colors and their meanings](/guides/heart-emoji-colors-meaning/).

## Emojis for All Saints' Day

All Saints' Day is a feast, not a funeral. Its messages can be joyful and hopeful: the saints are celebrated as friends and models, and Catholics often call on their patron saints that day.

- **😇 ✨ 👑** Holiness and the "crown of glory" the saints receive. The [crown](/emojis/crown/) recalls Christian art showing saints crowned in heaven.
- **🌴** The palm is the traditional sign of martyrs. There is no palm-branch emoji; the [palm tree](/emojis/palm-tree/) 🌴 or the herb 🌿 stands in.
- **🤍 and 🌟** White and light for the feast's color and the saints as lights for the Church.
- **⛪ and 🙏** Mass and prayer. All Saints' Day is a holy day, so "Mass times for All Saints ⛪" is a common parish post.

Many Catholic schools and parishes hold All Saints' parties where children dress as their favorite saints, a deliberate alternative or complement to Halloween costumes. A photo caption like "Our little Saint Francis and Saint Thérèse 😇🌹🐦" captures it well; Saint Thérèse of Lisieux is associated with roses, and Saint Francis with animals.

For individual saints, borrow an emoji from the saint's story: 🔑 for Saint Peter, 🌹 for Thérèse, 🐦 or 🐺 for Francis, ☘️ for Patrick. A longer list of feast days and their symbols is in [Christian emojis and their meanings](/guides/christian-emojis-meanings/).

## Emojis for All Souls' Day and remembering the dead

All Souls' Day is quieter. The tone is grief held within hope: Christians pray for those who have died and trust in the resurrection.

- **🕯️🙏** The essential pair. It says "I am praying for them" with no further words needed.
- **🪦💐** A cemetery visit, flowers on a grave.
- **📿🕯️** Praying the rosary for the dead, a common Catholic devotion in November.
- **💜 or 🖤** The [purple heart](/emojis/purple-heart/) for the violet vestments, or the [black heart](/emojis/black-heart/) for mourning. 🖤 is more somber and also has gothic and edgy uses online, so in a personal message 🤍 is often gentler.
- **🕊️** The [dove](/emojis/dove/) for peace and the soul at rest. It suits mixed audiences because it carries no specific creed.

The traditional Catholic prayer for the dead begins "Eternal rest grant unto them, O Lord, and let perpetual light shine upon them." Many people post its first line with 🕯️🙏, or the shorter "May they rest in peace 🤍."

### For family and personal posts

- Remembering Grandma and Grandpa today. Eternal rest grant unto them, O Lord 🕯️🙏
- Took flowers to Dad's grave this morning 💐🪦🤍
- Lit a candle for everyone we lost this year 🕯️
- All you holy men and women, pray for us 😇🙏
- Praying the rosary for the holy souls this November 📿🕯️

### For a friend who is grieving

The first All Souls' Day after a death can be hard. A short message tells your friend that you remember too.

- Thinking of you and of your mom today 🤍
- Lighting a candle for Mark this All Souls' Day 🕯️ Sending you love.
- I know this time of year is hard. I'm here if you want to talk or just get coffee 🤍☕
- Praying for your dad's soul and for you today 🙏 (if your friend shares your faith)
- Remembering her with you today 🕊️

Name the person when you can; hearing a loved one's name is often a comfort. One or two emojis at the end are enough, and words matter far more than symbols. If you are unsure of the friend's beliefs, choose 🕯️, 🤍, or 🕊️ rather than ✝️ or 📿.

## What to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| 💀 Skull | Usually means "I'm dead (laughing)" in chat | 🕯️ 🤍 |
| ☠️ Skull and crossbones | Poison, pirates, danger | 🪦 🕯️ |
| 😂 🤣 Laughing faces | Read as mockery next to grief | Nothing, or 🤍 |
| 🎃 👻 🦇 Halloween emojis | Costume-party mood, not prayer | 😇 for saints, 🕯️ for souls |
| ⚰️ Coffin | Stark; also used as a death-by-cringe joke | 💐 🪦 |
| 🥀 alone | Can read as moody heartbreak | 💐 🌹 |
| 😇 in condolences | Can seem playful | 🕊️ 🤍 |

The [skull](/emojis/skull/) deserves the most caution. For many people under 30 it has replaced 😂 as the reaction to something extremely funny, a shift covered in [Gen Z emoji slang](/guides/gen-z-emoji-slang/) and [laughing and crying emojis](/guides/laughing-and-crying-emojis/). Put next to a name in a remembrance post, it can be read as a joke or as disrespect. The [ghost](/emojis/ghost/) 👻 and [jack-o'-lantern](/emojis/jack-o-lantern/) 🎃 are harmless on October 31, but they belong to Halloween's playful side and clash with prayers for the dead the day after.

## How the days relate to Halloween and Día de los Muertos

**Halloween (October 31).** The name is a contraction of All Hallows' Eve, the evening before All Hallows' Day, an older English name for All Saints' Day; "hallow" means a saint. In the liturgy, a major feast begins on the evening before, which is why the eve and the solemnity are linked. Halloween's popular customs, such as costumes, carved pumpkins, and trick-or-treating, developed from a mix of later folk traditions and are now largely secular. The two sets of emojis stay apart: 🎃👻🍬 on the 31st, 😇🕯️🙏 on the 1st and 2nd.

**Día de los Muertos (November 1-2).** The Day of the Dead is a Mexican tradition, also kept in other parts of Latin America and by many Mexican American families, that coincides with All Saints' and All Souls' Days. It blends Indigenous practices of honoring the dead with the Catholic feasts. Families build ofrendas, home altars with photographs of the dead, candles, marigolds (cempasúchil), pan de muerto, and the deceased's favorite foods, and visit and decorate graves. In many communities November 1 is dedicated to children who have died and November 2 to adults. UNESCO lists the Indigenous festivity dedicated to the dead in Mexico as Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Its emojis are colorful: 🌼 or 🏵️ for marigolds, [orange heart](/emojis/orange-heart/) 🧡, 🕯️, 🍞, 💐, and the Mexican flag. Here, and only here among these three occasions, the 💀 skull is welcome, because decorated sugar skulls and the elegant skeleton figure La Catrina are joyful parts of the celebration rather than symbols of fear. Día de los Muertos is not "Mexican Halloween," and treating it as a costume party can offend; the [emojis across cultures](/guides/emojis-across-cultures/) guide covers other symbols whose meaning changes with the setting.

## A rule of thumb before posting

1. **Saints or souls?** November 1 is a feast: 😇 ✨ 🤍. November 2 is prayer for the dead: 🕯️ 🙏 💐.
2. **Who is reading?** A parish page can use ✝️ and 📿 freely; a mixed audience or a friend of unknown belief is better served by 🕯️ 🤍 🕊️.
3. **Could it read as a joke?** If an emoji is common in memes, such as 💀, ⚰️, or 😇 on its own, leave it out of anything about a real person who has died.
4. **Is the name there?** Words carry the remembrance; one or two emojis support them.
5. **Will it display?** 🪦 dates from 2020 and can appear as a box on old devices; 🕯️ and 🙏 are safe everywhere.
