Emoji Kitchen is a Google feature that fuses two emojis into one custom sticker, for example a ghost wearing a cowboy hat or a cactus with heart eyes. It is built into Gboard, Google's keyboard for Android, and there is a web version inside Google Search that works on any phone or computer. The results are images rather than real emoji characters, which explains nearly every limit described below.

## What Emoji Kitchen is

Google introduced Emoji Kitchen in Gboard for Android in February 2020. The idea is simple: pick one emoji, pick a second, and Gboard offers a sticker that combines features of both. Choose the [ghost](/emojis/ghost/) and the [fire emoji](/emojis/fire/) and you get some kind of flaming ghost. Choose the same emoji twice and you usually get an exaggerated version of it.

Three facts about how it works are useful to know before you start.

- **The blends are drawn, not computed.** Google's designers create the combinations and add them to a catalog. Gboard looks up your pair and shows what exists. This is why the results look polished, and also why some pairs return nothing.
- **The catalog grows.** Google has expanded it many times since launch, including whenever new emojis are added to Android. A pair that did nothing last year may work now.
- **The output is a sticker.** Gboard pastes a small image into the message. It does not insert a character. Anything that only accepts text will not accept it.

Emoji Kitchen is also different from the multi-part emojis defined by the Unicode Consortium, such as family groups or the [heart on fire](/emojis/heart-on-fire/), which is a real emoji built from a heart and a flame joined by an invisible character. Those are text and work wherever emojis work. Kitchen stickers are pictures and work wherever pictures work.

## How to use Emoji Kitchen in Gboard on Android

You need Gboard installed and selected as your keyboard. It is the default on Pixel phones and many others; on a Samsung Galaxy you can install it from the Play Store and switch to it in the keyboard settings.

1. Open an app that supports image stickers. Google Messages, WhatsApp, Telegram, Messenger, Instagram direct messages and Snapchat chats generally do.
2. Tap the message field, then tap the smiley key to open the emoji keyboard.
3. Tap an emoji. A strip of sticker suggestions appears above the emoji grid showing blends based on that emoji.
4. Tap a second emoji. The strip updates to show the combination of the two, usually first in the row, followed by related alternatives.
5. Tap the sticker you want. It is inserted into the message, or sent immediately in apps that send stickers on tap.

A few things make this smoother:

- **Order can matter.** Picking A then B generally leads with the same sticker as B then A, but the alternatives in the strip can differ. If you do not see what you hoped for, try the reverse.
- **Scroll the strip.** It often holds more results than fit on screen.
- **Double up.** Tapping the same emoji twice gives the "more of the same" version: a face pushed to an extreme, or an object multiplied.
- **Find recent creations.** Stickers you have sent are kept in Gboard's sticker and recent sections, so you do not need to rebuild a favorite each time.

### If the suggestions do not appear

Work through this list in order.

1. **Check the setting.** Open Gboard's settings (touch and hold the comma key and tap the gear, or find Gboard under your phone's keyboard settings). In **Emojis, Stickers & GIFs**, make sure the emoji stickers option is on. Google has renamed this toggle more than once, so look for wording that mentions emoji stickers or Emoji Kitchen.
2. **Check the app.** The strip only appears in fields that accept images. A browser address bar, a notes app's title field or a banking app's message box will not show it. Some apps show a notice that they do not support image insertion.
3. **Check the emoji.** Try a mainstream smiley such as the [smiling face with heart-eyes](/emojis/smiling-face-with-heart-eyes/). If that works, your original choice simply has no combinations.
4. **Update Gboard.** New combinations and fixes arrive through Play Store updates.

## How to use Emoji Kitchen in Google Search

Since 2023, Google Search has had a built-in version, which is the easiest route on an iPhone, a Mac, a Windows PC or a Chromebook.

1. Open google.com in any browser, or the Google app.
2. Search for **emoji kitchen**.
3. Tap or click **Get cooking** on the card at the top of the results.
4. Pick an emoji for each of the two slots, or use the randomize button, if your version shows one, to get a surprise pair.
5. When the result appears, copy it. You can then paste it into a chat, or save the image.

The web tool draws from Google's catalog but is simpler than the keyboard: you get one result per pair instead of a strip of alternatives. Because it hands you an image on the clipboard, pasting works in apps that accept pasted images and fails in those that do not. On a computer, you can usually also right-click the result to save it as a file, which is handy for slides or documents.

Availability of Search features can vary by country and language. If the card does not appear, try searching in English.

## What kinds of combinations you get

The catalog has recognizable patterns. Knowing them helps you guess what a pair will produce.

- **Face plus face.** The sticker takes the shape or color of one and the expression or accessory of the other. Mixing the [smiling face with sunglasses](/emojis/smiling-face-with-sunglasses/) with a crying face tends to give a crying face in shades.
- **Face plus object.** The object usually gets a face. Combine a smiley with a [pizza](/emojis/pizza/), a mushroom or a cloud and the object takes on that expression.
- **Accessory emojis.** The [cowboy hat face](/emojis/cowboy-hat-face/) lends its hat, the [smiling face with halo](/emojis/smiling-face-with-halo/) lends its halo, and the [partying face](/emojis/partying-face/) lends its hat and confetti to almost anything they are paired with.
- **Texture and material emojis.** Pairing with fire, [ice](/emojis/ice/), a [rainbow](/emojis/rainbow/) or the [ghost](/emojis/ghost/) often remakes the other emoji in that material: on fire, frozen, rainbow-colored or ghostly.
- **Hearts.** Adding a [red heart](/emojis/red-heart/) or sparkling heart usually produces a love-struck version, with the object holding or surrounded by hearts.
- **Animals.** Animal pairs give hybrids, and animal plus food gives the animal eating, wearing or made of the food. The [turtle](/emojis/turtle/), octopus and monkey face have some of the largest sets.

Because artists made each one, there are jokes hidden in the catalog: visual puns, references to idioms, and pairings that produce something unexpected instead of a literal blend. Part of the appeal is trying a pair to see what the designers decided it should be. The specific artwork for a given pair can also be revised between Gboard versions, so a blend you saw in a screenshot may look a little different on your phone.

## Limits you should know about

### Not every emoji is supported

Smileys, animals, foods, weather and common objects are well covered. Country flags, most arrows and signs in the [symbols category](/categories/symbols/), and many people and profession emojis have few combinations or none. When an emoji is unsupported, Gboard either shows nothing or falls back to generic stickers.

### They are images, with everything that implies

- You cannot use a Kitchen sticker in a username, display name, bio, hashtag, email subject line or any text-only form field.
- In most apps the sticker is sent as its own message or attachment rather than sitting inline with your words.
- Over SMS, an image turns the message into MMS, which may be compressed, delayed or charged differently depending on the carrier. Over RCS and in chat apps this is not an issue.
- The recipient sees exactly the picture you sent. That is an advantage over normal emojis, which are redrawn in each platform's own style, as explained in [iPhone vs Android emojis](/guides/iphone-vs-android-emojis/).
- The recipient cannot copy it as text, and searching the conversation for an emoji name will not find it.

### Accessibility

A standard emoji has a name that screen readers announce. A sticker is an image, and whether it gets a useful description depends on the app. If you are writing to someone who uses a screen reader, or posting publicly, do not let a Kitchen sticker carry information that is not also in the words. Our guide to [emoji accessibility](/guides/emoji-accessibility-screen-readers/) covers the principles.

### Work and formal settings

A blended sticker reads as playful, more so than a single emoji. It suits group chats with friends and is a gamble in a message to a client. The same judgment that applies to reaction GIFs applies here; see [emojis at work](/guides/emojis-at-work-etiquette/).

## Emoji Kitchen, Genmoji and custom emoji compared

Several features now let you send "an emoji that does not exist". They work differently.

- **Emoji Kitchen (Google).** A fixed, designer-drawn catalog of two-emoji blends. Free, in Gboard and Search. Output is a sticker image.
- **Genmoji (Apple).** On iPhones that support Apple Intelligence, you type a description and the phone generates a new emoji-style image. Inside Apple's Messages it can sit inline with text; elsewhere it generally arrives as an image.
- **Custom emoji in Slack, Discord and similar apps.** Images uploaded by a workspace or server and typed with a shortcode. They exist only inside that workspace.
- **New Unicode emojis.** The only route to a real, universal emoji is a proposal to the Unicode Consortium, described in [how new emojis get approved](/guides/how-new-emojis-get-approved/).

The first three are all pictures dressed as emojis. They are fine for expression and unreliable for anything that has to survive being copied, searched or read aloud.

## Ideas for using it well

- **Reactions with more precision.** A melting face blended with a heart says "this is so sweet I can't cope" better than either alone.
- **Running jokes.** Pick a friend group's mascot emoji, such as the [frog](/emojis/frog/), and blend it with whatever fits the moment.
- **Seasonal greetings.** Combine a [Christmas tree](/emojis/christmas-tree/), [jack-o'-lantern](/emojis/jack-o-lantern/) or birthday cake with a favorite smiley for a quick custom greeting.
- **Slides and documents.** Save a blend from Google Search as an image when you want a small illustration that no one else has.

If what you want is a sequence of ordinary emojis that tells a story or sets a mood in plain text, where it will work in bios and captions too, that is a different technique, covered in our guide to [emoji combos](/guides/emoji-combos-ideas/).

## Quick checklist before you send

1. Is the field a chat that accepts images? If not, use regular emojis.
2. Does the pair exist? Try both orders, then swap one emoji for a more common one.
3. Will the meaning survive if the image fails to load or is not described? Add a few words.
4. Is the audience right for a playful sticker?
5. On iPhone or desktop, use Google Search's version and paste the result.
