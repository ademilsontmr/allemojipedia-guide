To do emojis on a Chromebook, click into any text box and press **Search + Shift + Space** (on newer keyboards the Search key is labeled **Launcher**). If you prefer the mouse or touchpad, right-click inside the text box and choose **Emoji** from the menu. Both open the same ChromeOS emoji picker, where you can type a word like "heart" to find what you want and click it to insert.

That covers most people. The rest of this guide explains what is inside the picker, how to get emojis in tablet mode and on school Chromebooks, how they behave in Google apps and Android apps, and what to check when the shortcut does nothing. If you also use a Windows PC, Mac or phone, our overview of [how to type emojis on any device](/guides/how-to-type-emojis-on-any-device/) has the shortcuts for each.

## Two ways to open the emoji picker

### The keyboard shortcut

1. Click or tap inside a place where you can type: a Google Doc, the Gmail compose window, a search box, a chat message field.
2. Hold **Search** (or **Launcher**) and **Shift**, then press **Space**.
3. The picker appears next to your cursor.

The Search or Launcher key is on the left side of the keyboard, where Caps Lock is on most other laptops. Older Chromebooks print a magnifying glass on it. Newer models print the Launcher symbol instead. For this shortcut there is no difference: whichever icon your key has, it is the key you press. Because that key took over Caps Lock's spot, people sometimes go looking for the missing Caps Lock; it is **Search (or Launcher) + Alt**.

### The right-click menu

1. Move the pointer into a text field.
2. Right-click. On a touchpad that means a two-finger click or tap, or pressing Alt while you click.
3. Choose **Emoji** near the top of the menu.

The right-click route is handy when you only need one emoji, or when you are using a Chromebook with an unfamiliar keyboard layout. It only appears where the system knows you can type. If you right-click on ordinary page text, a picture or empty space, you get a different menu without the Emoji option.

### A dedicated key on some newer models

Some recent Chromebooks, particularly in the Chromebook Plus range, have a key for Google's Quick Insert feature, which brings together search, emojis and other insert tools in one menu. Whether your keyboard has it depends on the model. If yours does not, nothing is lost: the shortcut and right-click menu above work the same way.

## What is inside the ChromeOS emoji picker

The picker has been redesigned several times since Google added it to ChromeOS, so the exact layout on your screen depends on your version. The parts below are the ones you will find on a reasonably current, up-to-date Chromebook.

- **Search field.** Sits at the top, and the cursor is already in it when the picker opens. Typing filters every section at once.
- **Recently used.** Emojis you picked before appear first, so the ones you send every day stay one click away.
- **Emoji categories.** Small icons along the top or side jump to groups such as smileys, people, animals, food, travel, activities, objects, symbols and flags. These follow the same Unicode groups you can browse on this site, from [smileys and emotion](/categories/smileys-and-emotion/) to [flags](/categories/flags/).
- **Symbols.** A separate section holds characters that are not emoji: arrows, math signs, currency symbols, Greek letters, bullets and similar marks. This is the quickest built-in way to type a degree sign or a euro sign on a Chromebook.
- **Emoticons.** Text faces, often called kaomoji, such as `(^_^)` and `¯\_(ツ)_/¯`, inserted as ordinary characters.
- **GIFs.** Newer versions add a GIF section with its own search. Availability depends on your ChromeOS version, your region and, on managed devices, your administrator.

Click an emoji to insert it. The picker usually closes after each pick; open it again for the next one. If you want a string of emojis, it is often faster to insert one, then press the shortcut again and type the next search.

### Keyboard-only use

You do not need the touchpad at all. Press the shortcut, type a word, use the **arrow keys** to move through the results, press **Enter** to insert, and press **Esc** to close without inserting. This works well for people who type fast or use the keyboard for accessibility reasons.

## Searching: the fastest way to find any emoji

Scrolling through a few thousand emojis is slow. Search is not. A few habits make it much faster:

- **Use plain words.** "laugh" finds the [face with tears of joy](/emojis/face-with-tears-of-joy/) and [rolling on the floor laughing](/emojis/rolling-on-the-floor-laughing/). "cry" finds the [loudly crying face](/emojis/loudly-crying-face/) and its quieter relatives.
- **Search by object or idea.** "party" brings up the [party popper](/emojis/party-popper/), "100" brings up [hundred points](/emojis/hundred-points/), and "pray" or "thanks" usually brings up [folded hands](/emojis/folded-hands/).
- **Flags by country name.** Type "united states" or "japan" rather than scrolling to the flags group. The [United States flag](/emojis/flag-united-states/) displays as a real flag on a Chromebook, unlike on many Windows apps.
- **One word is usually enough.** The search matches names and keywords, so "fire" finds the [fire emoji](/emojis/fire/) without "flame" or "hot".
- **Symbols and emoticons are searchable too.** "arrow", "degree" or "shrug" return results from those sections as well.

The search words come from Google's emoji keyword data, which draws on the Unicode Consortium's CLDR project. That is why a search for a feeling often finds faces you would not have guessed by name. If you want to know the official name of an emoji you found, the [full Unicode emoji list](https://unicode.org/emoji/charts/full-emoji-list.html) shows every character with its name.

## Skin tones and other variants

Emojis of hands and people can be shown in five skin tones besides the default yellow. In the ChromeOS picker, an emoji that has variants is marked with a small indicator in its corner. To choose a variant:

1. Find the emoji, for example [thumbs up](/emojis/thumbs-up/) or [waving hand](/emojis/waving-hand/).
2. Right-click it, or click and hold it. On some versions a small arrow or the marker itself opens the menu.
3. Choose the tone you want.

Recent versions remember the tone you picked and show it in your recently used row, so you set it once for the hands you use most. How the picker stores that preference has changed over time; if your choice does not stick, pick the tone again from the variant menu.

The same variant menu covers gender options where an emoji has them, and some emojis with two people let you set a tone for each person, depending on your version. For background on where the five tones come from and the etiquette questions around them, see our guide to [emoji skin tones](/guides/emoji-skin-tones-explained/).

## Tablet mode and the on-screen keyboard

Convertible Chromebooks switch to tablet mode when you fold the screen back or detach the keyboard. With no physical keyboard, the shortcut has nowhere to go, so ChromeOS gives you an on-screen keyboard instead.

1. Tap a text field. The on-screen keyboard slides up.
2. Tap the **smiley key** on the keyboard's bottom row.
3. The emoji panel replaces the letter keys. Swipe through the categories or use its search if your version offers one.
4. Tap an emoji to insert it, then tap the **ABC** key to return to letters.

Touch and hold an emoji on the on-screen keyboard to see its skin tone variants.

### Getting the on-screen keyboard in laptop mode

If your touchscreen Chromebook stays in laptop mode, or a key on your physical keyboard is broken, you can turn the on-screen keyboard on yourself. Open **Settings**, go to **Accessibility**, and look under the keyboard and text input options for the on-screen keyboard setting. The exact section name has shifted between ChromeOS versions, so searching "on-screen keyboard" in the Settings search box is the quickest way to find it. Once it is on, a keyboard icon appears near the clock on the shelf, and tapping it opens the keyboard with its smiley key.

Chromebooks without a touchscreen can still use the on-screen keyboard with the touchpad, which helps if the Shift key or Space bar stops working.

## School and work Chromebooks

Many Chromebooks belong to a school district or an employer rather than to the person using them. These devices are managed through Google's admin tools, and the administrator decides which apps, sites and features are available.

What that means for emojis in practice:

- **The basic picker usually works.** It is a built-in part of ChromeOS, and on most managed Chromebooks both the shortcut and the right-click Emoji option behave the same as on a personal device.
- **Some extras may be off.** Features that reach the internet, such as GIF search, can be turned off by the organization, and you will not find a switch to turn them back on.
- **Updates are controlled.** A school may hold devices on a specific ChromeOS version, so the newest emojis can take longer to appear than on a personal Chromebook.
- **The site or app matters.** Even when the picker works, a learning platform, form or quiz tool can reject emoji characters, or the school can block the chat app you wanted to use them in.

If something emoji-related is missing on a school or work device, ask the IT staff. Settings on managed devices are set on purpose, and trying to get around them can break school rules even when the goal is harmless.

## Emojis in Google Docs, Gmail, Chat and other apps

The system picker works in almost any text field in Chrome and in ChromeOS apps. Several of Google's own web apps also have their own emoji tools, which are sometimes more convenient.

### Google Docs

The ChromeOS shortcut inserts emojis straight into a Doc. Docs also has **Insert > Emoji** in its menu bar, and in many documents you can type a colon followed by a word to search emojis inline. Emojis in Docs are text, so they keep their place when you share or export the document. Separately, you can add emoji reactions to comments, which is useful for a quick acknowledgment like a [check mark button](/emojis/check-mark-button/) on a teacher's or coworker's note.

### Gmail

In the compose window, the toolbar at the bottom includes an emoji button, and the ChromeOS shortcut works in both the subject line and the body. Be aware that emojis in subject lines and bodies are drawn by the recipient's device, so a message that looks one way on your Chromebook may look different in Outlook or on an iPhone. Our guide to [emojis at work](/guides/emojis-at-work-etiquette/) covers when they fit in email and when they do not.

### Google Chat and other chat apps

Google Chat has a smiley button in the message box and lets you react to messages with emojis. Web versions of other messaging apps, such as WhatsApp Web, Slack and Discord in Chrome, each have their own emoji button too. Slack and Discord also turn shortcodes like `:thumbsup:` into emojis. The ChromeOS shortcut works alongside all of these.

### Android apps from the Play Store

Most Chromebooks can run Android apps. When you use them with a physical keyboard, ChromeOS handles the typing, and the system shortcut usually works in their text fields. In tablet mode, the ChromeOS on-screen keyboard appears, smiley key included. If an Android app does not respond to the shortcut, use the app's own emoji button if it has one, or right-click the field and look for the Emoji option. Behavior varies from app to app.

### Linux apps

If you have turned on the Linux development environment, apps running there do not always accept input from the ChromeOS picker. Copying an emoji from a Chrome tab and pasting it in is the reliable fallback.

## Why emojis look the way they do on a Chromebook

ChromeOS draws emojis with **Noto Color Emoji**, the same Google design family used on Pixel phones and many other Android devices. That is why the [grinning face](/emojis/grinning-face/) or [red heart](/emojis/red-heart/) on your Chromebook matches what a Pixel user sees, and not what an iPhone user sees.

When you send an emoji, you send a Unicode character, not a picture. The other person's device then draws it with its own artwork: Apple's on iPhone and Mac, Microsoft's on Windows, Samsung's on Galaxy phones. Most emojis read the same everywhere, but some differ in expression or detail. Our comparison of [iPhone vs Android emojis](/guides/iphone-vs-android-emojis/) shows where the designs drift apart.

New emojis reach Chromebooks through ChromeOS updates, after the Unicode Consortium approves them and Google draws its versions. A Chromebook that has reached the end of its update period keeps its existing emoji set. If a friend sends something newer, your screen may show a box or a pair of separate emojis instead; [why emojis show as boxes](/guides/why-emojis-show-as-boxes/) explains each symptom. You can see which emojis are the latest in [new emojis in 2026](/guides/new-emojis-2026/).

## When the emoji shortcut does nothing

Work through these in order. Most problems are solved by the first two.

1. **Put the cursor in a text field.** The shortcut only works where you can type. Click inside the box until you see a blinking cursor, then try again.
2. **Check that you are pressing the right key.** It is the Search or Launcher key on the left edge of the keyboard, not Ctrl or Alt, and Shift has to be held together with it before you press Space.
3. **Check for remapped keys.** ChromeOS lets you change what the Search, Ctrl and Alt keys do in the keyboard settings. If someone has swapped the Search key for something else, the shortcut moves with it. Search "keyboard" in Settings and look at the key remapping options.
4. **Try the right-click menu.** If right-click > Emoji works but the shortcut does not, the problem is the keys, not the picker.
5. **Try another app or site.** Some web pages capture keyboard shortcuts for their own use. Test in the Chrome address bar or a Google Doc.
6. **Check your input method.** If you use a keyboard layout or input method for another language, switch back to your usual English layout and test again.
7. **Update ChromeOS.** Open **Settings > About ChromeOS** and check for updates, then restart. The picker and its shortcut were added and changed across several releases, so an outdated device may behave differently.
8. **Restart the Chromebook.** A full restart clears a stuck input state more often than you would expect.
9. **Ask your administrator.** On a school or work Chromebook, if none of the above helps, the IT staff can confirm whether a feature is restricted.

As a last resort on any device, copy and paste works. Every emoji page on this site shows the character as plain text, so you can copy the [thinking face](/emojis/thinking-face/) or anything in [symbols](/categories/symbols/) and paste it with **Ctrl + V**.
