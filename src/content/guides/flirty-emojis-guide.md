Flirting with emojis works best when you treat it as a scale. A warm smile shows you enjoy the conversation, a wink or kiss face makes the interest clear, and suggestive emojis belong only where both people have already shown they want that. Most emoji flirting that goes wrong skips a level, either jumping to explicit too early or staying so subtle that nothing registers.

This guide covers the levels, what to send at each stage of getting to know someone, example messages, and the common ways it backfires.

## The three levels of flirty emojis

| Level | What it communicates | Typical emojis | When it fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Warm | "I like talking to you" | 😊 😄 😁 😂 🙈 ☺️ | Any stage, including first messages |
| 2. Flirtatious | "I'm interested in you" | 😉 😘 🥰 😍 😏 🔥 👀 😈 | Once the other person is clearly engaged |
| 3. Explicit | Sexual interest | 🥵 🫦 👅 🍑 🍆 💦 | Only with established, mutual interest between adults |

Two principles apply across all three. First, each level includes everything below it; couples who sext still send 😊. Second, moving up should be a step, not a leap. If you have been at level 1, try a single level 2 emoji and see how it is received.

## Level 1: warm emojis that open the door

Level 1 is deniable in the good sense. These emojis are friendly enough to send to anyone, so there is no awkwardness if the other person is not interested, but used consistently and directed at one person, they create the warmth that flirting builds on.

- **😊 [Smiling face with smiling eyes](/emojis/smiling-face-with-smiling-eyes/).** A sincere, slightly blushing smile. The safest warm emoji there is.
- **😄 and 😁.** Bright, open enthusiasm: "That sounds fun 😄."
- **😂 or 😭 at their jokes.** Laughing at someone's humor is one of the oldest forms of flirting. Add a few words so it does not look like a brush-off: "ok that got me 😂."
- **🙈 [See-no-evil monkey](/emojis/see-no-evil-monkey/).** Bashfulness. Good after you have said something a little bold: "I may have looked at your whole profile 🙈."
- **☺️ Smiling face.** A quiet, pleased blush, for accepting a compliment.

If you are unsure which smile sends which message, the comparison in [smiley face emojis explained](/guides/smiley-face-emojis-explained/) is useful. Avoid 🙂 here; many people read the flat [slightly smiling face](/emojis/slightly-smiling-face/) as cool or sarcastic.

## Level 2: emojis that say "I'm interested"

These are the emojis most people mean by "flirty." Each has a distinct flavor.

### 😉 Winking face

The [winking face](/emojis/winking-face/) signals teasing and shared understanding. It turns a statement into banter: "Guess you'll have to prove it 😉." It is the easiest first step up from level 1 because it can still pass as a joke.

### 😘 Face blowing a kiss

The [face blowing a kiss](/emojis/face-blowing-a-kiss/) is affectionate and direct. It is common as a sign-off ("goodnight 😘") once flirting is mutual. In a first conversation it can feel premature.

### 🥰 Smiling face with hearts

The [smiling face with hearts](/emojis/smiling-face-with-hearts/) expresses fondness and feeling adored. It is sweet, not seductive, and suits replies to kind messages: "you remembered 🥰."

### 😍 Smiling face with heart-eyes

The [heart-eyes face](/emojis/smiling-face-with-heart-eyes/) says "wow" about how something or someone looks. As a reply to a photo it is a clear compliment.

### 😏 Smirking face

The [smirking face](/emojis/smirking-face/) adds innuendo. "I have a few ideas 😏" leaves the idea unstated on purpose. It is the most forward of the non-explicit emojis and the one most likely to seem presumptuous if the interest is not mutual.

### 🔥, 👀 and 😈

[Fire](/emojis/fire/) means "you look hot" when sent in response to a photo. [Eyes](/emojis/eyes/) mean "I noticed" or "tell me more," and work for playful curiosity: "new haircut? 👀." The [smiling face with horns](/emojis/smiling-face-with-horns/) signals mischief and sits at the upper edge of level 2, close to suggestive.

### Hearts

A [red heart](/emojis/red-heart/) is a strong statement, so early on a lighter option such as a pink heart or [two hearts](/emojis/two-hearts/) is easier to receive. Colors carry different weights, explained in the guide to [heart emoji colors](/guides/heart-emoji-colors-meaning/).

## Level 3: explicit emojis

Described plainly: the [hot face](/emojis/hot-face/) and [biting lip](/emojis/biting-lip/) express physical desire. The tongue is used suggestively. The [peach](/emojis/peach/) stands for buttocks, the [eggplant](/emojis/eggplant/) for a penis, and [sweat droplets](/emojis/sweat-droplets/) for sexual fluids or arousal. Combined in a message, they are sexual content, the equivalent of saying the same thing in words.

Three rules apply to this level.

1. **Mutual interest comes first.** These belong in conversations where both adults have already shown, in words, that they welcome sexual messages. An emoji does not make an unwanted advance more acceptable.
2. **The other person sets the ceiling.** If they answer a level 3 message with level 1 warmth or change the topic, return to their level and stay there.
3. **Messages are permanent.** Anything you send can be screenshotted and shared. Some platforms also restrict or filter sexualized emoji use in public posts and ads.

Many long-term couples never use these at all and flirt perfectly well at level 2.

## Words do the flirting; emojis set the tone

A flirty emoji with nothing around it forces the other person to guess. The same emoji attached to specific words is clear. Compare:

- "😉" versus "You always pick the best spots 😉"
- "🔥" versus "That jacket on you 🔥"
- "😏" versus "Careful, I'm very competitive at mini golf 😏"

Three message types reliably work.

**The specific compliment.** Name something particular: their laugh, their playlist, the way they tell a story. "Your voice notes are the best part of my day 😊" beats "ur cute 😍."

**The light tease.** Gentle, clearly affectionate ribbing, with a wink to mark it as play: "Three alarms and you were still late 😉." Tease choices and habits. Do not tease appearance or insecurities.

**The callback.** Refer to something from an earlier conversation. "Passed a taco truck and thought of your very strong opinions 🌮😄" shows attention, which is more attractive than any emoji.

Placement matters too. Put the emoji at the end of the sentence it applies to. One is usually enough; two matching ones add emphasis; a row of five different faces dilutes the message. For reading tone in general, see [emoji meanings in texting](/guides/emoji-meanings-in-texting/).

## What to send at each stage

**First message or reply to a story.** Level 1 only, attached to something specific. "That hike looks unreal 😄 where is it?" A question gives them a reason to answer.

**Early conversation, interest unknown.** Stay at level 1, and introduce one 😉 with a tease. Watch what comes back.

**The talking stage, interest looks mutual.** Level 2 is appropriate. Use 😘 or 🥰 for sign-offs, 😍 or 🔥 for photos, and the occasional 😏. Suggest meeting in person; emojis are a supplement to plans, not a substitute.

**Dating.** Hearts become normal. You will develop private conventions, such as an emoji that only the two of you use a certain way. Level 3 is a matter of mutual preference.

**Long-term relationships.** Flirting tends to fade into logistics ("milk? 👍"). An unprompted "you looked great this morning 😘" still works, perhaps better than it did at the start.

## Reading the reply

Flirting is a two-person exchange, so their response decides your next move.

| Their reply to a flirty message | What it suggests | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Matches or raises your level (😉 back, 😘, a tease in return) | Interest | Continue; consider suggesting plans |
| Warm but level 1 (😊, 😂, keeps chatting) | Friendly, possibly shy or cautious | Stay at their level; try again later, lightly |
| Neutral and brief (👍, "haha"), topic change | Not flirting back | Drop to friendly; do not escalate |
| No reply to the flirty part, or a direct "let's keep it friendly" | A clear no | Accept it and move on |

Remember that some people are sparing with emojis by nature, and effort tells you more than symbols. Someone who sends plain text but asks questions and agrees to meet is interested. ## How the app changes the message

The same emoji carries different weight depending on where it is sent.

- **Dating apps.** Both people are there to meet someone, so level 2 arrives sooner and is less likely to surprise. A specific opener still beats an emoji-only one, and explicit emojis in a first message remain the fastest route to being unmatched.
- **Instagram.** Story replies are a common, low-pressure way to start. A typed reply with one emoji shows more intent than a quick reaction, and a 🔥 or 😍 left as a public comment is visible to everyone who follows that person. The [Instagram guide](/guides/instagram-emoji-meanings/) covers the differences.
- **Text and WhatsApp.** Private one-to-one messages are where flirting usually settles once it is mutual. Tone carries over from day to day, so a sudden change in your usual emojis will be noticed.
- **Reactions.** A heart tapped onto a message is lighter than a heart you type. It is a good way to show warmth without raising the level.

## What backfires

- **Going explicit early.** An eggplant or sweat droplets from someone the recipient barely knows ends most conversations and is experienced by many people as harassment.
- **The smirk on everything.** Constant 😏 makes every message sound like a come-on and gets tiring fast.
- **Emoji-only messages.** A lone 😘 or 👀 with no words gives the other person nothing to respond to.
- **Too many at once.** "Hey 😍😘🔥❤️😉" reads as overeager or copy-pasted.
- **Hearts too soon.** A red heart in the first few messages can feel intense. Build up to it.
- **A wink after an insult.** Adding 😉 to a put-down does not turn it into charm. If the sentence would sting without the emoji, rewrite the sentence.
- **Ignoring a cooler reply.** Repeating a flirty emoji after it was not returned is pressure, not persistence.
- **Mixed-up tone emojis.** 🙃 and 🙂 look cute but often read as sarcastic or annoyed; see [passive-aggressive emojis](/guides/passive-aggressive-emojis/).
- **Assuming a default reaction is flirting.** A 🔥 tapped from Instagram's quick reactions on a story is a one-tap response. It is friendly, and it may or may not be more.

### Where not to flirt with emojis

Context can make even a level 1 emoji inappropriate.

- **Work channels and email.** Winks, kiss faces and hearts toward colleagues, clients or anyone you manage create discomfort and can become a formal problem. The guide to [emojis at work](/guides/emojis-at-work-etiquette/) lists safe options.
- **Any relationship with a power imbalance.** Teacher and student, manager and report, service provider and customer. The other person may not feel free to object.
- **Group chats.** Flirting in front of an audience puts the other person on the spot. Move it to a private message.
- **Minors.** Adults do not send flirtatious or sexual emojis to anyone under 18.
- **People who have said no.** One clear answer is enough.

## A checklist before you hit send

1. Is the emoji attached to words that make my meaning clear?
2. Is this only one step warmer than the last thing they sent me?
3. Would I be comfortable if this message were screenshotted?
4. Is this a setting where they can freely ignore it or say no?
5. If they reply at a cooler level, am I ready to match that and not push?

If all five answers are yes, send it. The rule of thumb underneath the whole guide: flirt with the sentence, use the emoji to show you are smiling while you say it, and let the other person's reply set the pace.
