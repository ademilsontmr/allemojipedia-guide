Emojis on WhatsApp are the same Unicode emojis used everywhere else, so there is no separate WhatsApp code to learn. What differs is the setting: WhatsApp is where grandparents, parents, classmates, coworkers, and neighbors all share one app, across many countries, so the same emoji gets read very differently depending on which chat you are in. Literal meanings dominate in family groups, slang dominates among younger friends, and reactions have their own etiquette.

This guide covers how emojis and reactions work in the app, how they look on different phones, and how to read tone in the kinds of chats WhatsApp is known for.

## How emojis work on WhatsApp

Three practical facts explain most of what people notice.

**WhatsApp has its own emoji artwork on Android and the web.** Most Android apps show the emoji font that comes with the phone, which means Google's designs on a Pixel and Samsung's on a Galaxy. WhatsApp draws its own set instead, so emojis in WhatsApp look the same on any Android phone and on WhatsApp Web. On iPhone, WhatsApp uses Apple's emoji designs. The characters underneath are identical, which is why messages never break between the two, but the drawings differ in small ways: the tilt of a smile, the shade of a heart, the look of a [face with rolling eyes](/emojis/face-with-rolling-eyes/). For the wider picture, see [iPhone vs. Android emojis](/guides/iphone-vs-android-emojis/).

**New emojis arrive on a delay.** After the Unicode Consortium approves new emojis, each company has to draw and ship them. If a friend sends an emoji your version of WhatsApp or your phone does not yet support, you may see an empty box or a question mark. Updating the app (and, on iPhone, the operating system) normally fixes it. [Why emojis show as boxes](/guides/why-emojis-show-as-boxes/) explains the causes.

**Emoji-only messages are shown larger.** A message with only one or a few emojis and no text is displayed at a bigger size. Some emojis also animate when sent alone. The [red heart](/emojis/red-heart/) is the familiar case: sent by itself, it appears large and pulses. Which emojis animate has varied between app versions, so do not be surprised if a friend's phone shows something different from yours.

To type them, use the emoji button next to the message box, where there is a search field and a row of recently used emojis. Pressing and holding a hand or person emoji opens the skin-tone options, described in [emoji skin tones explained](/guides/emoji-skin-tones-explained/). On WhatsApp Web and Desktop you can also type a colon followed by the start of an emoji's name, such as `:smile`, to pull up matching suggestions.

## Reactions: how they work and what they signal

Reactions let you respond to a specific message with a single emoji that attaches beneath it. They cut down on the "ok," "thanks," and "😂" messages that used to flood group chats.

How to use them:

1. **Press and hold** the message on a phone. On WhatsApp Web or Desktop, hover over the message and click the emoji icon that appears.
2. **Pick from the quick row.** WhatsApp launched reactions with six defaults: 👍, ❤️, 😂, 😮, 😢, and 🙏.
3. **Tap the plus button** to choose any other emoji from the full keyboard.
4. **Change or remove it** by pressing the message again and choosing a different emoji, or by tapping your current reaction to take it off.

A few rules of the system are worth knowing:

- Each person can have **one reaction per message**. Choosing a second replaces the first.
- Reactions are **visible to everyone in the chat**. Tap the reaction to see who sent which.
- The sender gets a **notification** by default. Reaction notifications can be switched off in the app's notification settings.
- Removing a reaction does not guarantee the other person never saw it, since the notification may already have arrived.

### What each default reaction usually means

| Reaction | Typical meaning | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| 👍 | Yes, agreed, seen | Curt on long or emotional messages |
| ❤️ | Love it, thank you, support | Reads as romantic only in one-to-one chats with that context |
| 😂 | That is funny | Hurtful on a message that was not a joke |
| 😮 | Surprise, wow | Can look like shock or disapproval |
| 😢 | Sympathy, that is sad | Rarely misread |
| 🙏 | Thanks, please, praying for you | Read as prayer by many users |

The [thumbs up](/emojis/thumbs-up/) is the workhorse. In a group planning dinner, a column of thumbs-up reactions under "8 pm at Rosa's?" is the fastest possible headcount. The [red heart](/emojis/red-heart/) as a reaction is much less loaded than a heart sent as its own message; in groups it mostly means "I appreciate this." The [face with tears of joy](/emojis/face-with-tears-of-joy/) remains the default laugh on WhatsApp, more so than on apps with younger audiences.

A reaction is an acknowledgment, not an answer. If someone asks a question, especially one with options ("Saturday or Sunday?"), a thumbs up leaves them guessing. Reply with words.

## Family groups: a guide to the literal dialect

The family group is WhatsApp's signature setting, and it has recognizable conventions. Older relatives generally use emojis for exactly what they depict, often several at a time, and with complete sincerity.

**[Folded hands](/emojis/folded-hands/) 🙏.** The most versatile emoji in family chats. It means thank you, please, "God willing," "praying for you," or a respectful greeting, depending on the family's background. Under news of a hospital visit, it is sympathy and prayer.

**Flowers and suns.** "Good morning" messages decorated with a [rose](/emojis/rose/), a [sunflower](/emojis/sunflower/), a [hibiscus](/emojis/hibiscus/), or a [sun with face](/emojis/sun-with-face/) are a daily ritual in many families, particularly in South Asia, Latin America, and parts of Africa and southern Europe. They carry no hidden meaning. They are a way of saying "thinking of you," and a heart or folded-hands reaction is a kind response.

**The thumbs up.** From a parent it means yes, I read it, or good. It is not coldness. The generational dispute over this emoji is covered in the [passive-aggressive emojis guide](/guides/passive-aggressive-emojis/).

**Rows of the same emoji.** "Happy birthday 🎂🎂🎂🎉🎉🎉❤️❤️" is enthusiasm, measured in repetition.

**Sincere smiles.** The [slightly smiling face](/emojis/slightly-smiling-face/) from a grandparent is a smile. The ironic reading that younger people give it does not apply.

**Crying faces on sad news.** The [loudly crying face](/emojis/loudly-crying-face/) from an older relative means real sorrow. This is the reverse of how many younger users deploy it, which leads to the main hazard of family groups.

### Where generations collide

Slang that works in a friend chat can go badly wrong with relatives:

- Replying 💀 to a cousin's joke. To you the [skull](/emojis/skull/) means "I'm dead from laughing." To an aunt, a skull under a family photo is alarming.
- Reacting 😭 to a funny story when a relative reads it as distress.
- Using 😂 on a message that was meant seriously. Because reactions are visible to all, the whole family sees it.
- Sending the [upside-down face](/emojis/upside-down-face/) as irony to someone who takes it as a playful smile.

The fix is simple: in mixed-age groups, use emojis that mean what they show. If you want the background on why the meanings split, see [Gen Z emoji slang](/guides/gen-z-emoji-slang/) and [laughing and crying emojis](/guides/laughing-and-crying-emojis/).

## Friends, partners, and one-to-one chats

In private chats the general rules of texting apply, and the [emoji meanings in texting guide](/guides/emoji-meanings-in-texting/) covers them in depth. A few points are specific to how WhatsApp works.

**Reactions versus replies.** In a one-to-one chat, reacting to a message instead of answering it can end the exchange. A heart reaction on "Had such a good time tonight" is warm but closes the topic; a written reply keeps the conversation going. If you are interested in someone, reply.

**Hearts.** Because a lone red heart is displayed large and animated, it feels like a bigger gesture on WhatsApp than on other apps. Friends often prefer other colors for platonic affection; the differences are laid out in [heart emoji colors and meanings](/guides/heart-emoji-colors-meaning/).

**Voice notes and emojis.** WhatsApp users send a lot of voice messages. A common pattern is a voice note followed by a single emoji that sets its mood, such as 😂 or 🙈, so the listener knows what to expect before pressing play.

**Read receipts change the stakes.** When blue ticks are on, the other person knows you have read the message. A reaction is a low-effort way to show you saw something when you cannot reply properly yet, and it is better received than silence after two blue ticks.

**Status replies.** When you respond to someone's Status with an emoji, it is delivered to them privately. Recent versions also include a quick like button on Status. Neither is visible to their other contacts.

## Work, school, and neighborhood groups

In many countries WhatsApp is the default tool for small businesses, school parent groups, sports teams, and apartment buildings. These groups mix strangers and acquaintances, so a neutral register works best.

Good practice in semi-formal groups:

- **React instead of replying** to announcements. Fifty parents each writing "Thank you!" buries the original message; fifty reactions do not.
- **Stick to literal, positive emojis:** thumbs up, folded hands, [clapping hands](/emojis/clapping-hands/), a [check mark button](/emojis/check-mark-button/) for "done" or "paid."
- **Avoid laughing reactions** on anything that might be a complaint.
- **Skip hearts and kisses** toward individuals you do not know well.
- **Keep customer conversations plain.** If you run a business account, one friendly emoji is fine, but confirm orders, prices, and times in words. A thumbs up from a customer is not a reliable confirmation of anything important.

Group admins sometimes set conventions, such as "react 👍 if attending, 👎 if not." Where WhatsApp's built-in polls are available, they are the cleaner option for anything that needs counting, because each person's choice is listed and can be changed.

## The same emoji in different countries

WhatsApp is used heavily across India, Brazil, much of Latin America, Europe, Africa, and the Middle East, so cross-border chats are common, and some emojis shift meaning along the way.

- **🙏** is a greeting and a mark of respect for many Indian users, thanks for most Western users, and prayer in religious contexts everywhere.
- **👍** is cheerful and positive in Brazil and most of Europe and the Americas, but the gesture has traditionally been rude in parts of the Middle East and West Africa. Younger users there generally know the emoji's global meaning; older ones may not.
- **👌** The [OK hand](/emojis/ok-hand/) is approval in the United States and a vulgar gesture in Brazil and some other countries.
- **🤙** The [call me hand](/emojis/call-me-hand/) often means "hang loose" or "all good" rather than "phone me," notably in Brazil and Hawaii.
- **😂** Laughter is also typed out differently, as "kkkk" in Brazil, "jajaja" in Spanish, and "555" in Thailand, sometimes alongside the emoji and sometimes instead of it.

[Emojis across cultures](/guides/emojis-across-cultures/) and the [hand gesture guide](/guides/hand-gesture-emojis-meaning/) go further. When chatting across borders, faces and hearts are safer than hand signs.

## Stickers, avatars, and other things that are not emojis

WhatsApp offers several features that look like emojis but behave differently:

- **Stickers** are images. They are larger, cannot be placed inside a line of text, and will not appear if you copy a message elsewhere.
- **Avatars** are personalized cartoon stickers of yourself, where the feature is available.
- **GIFs** are short looping clips searched from inside the app.

Only true emojis are text characters. That matters for accessibility, because a screen reader can announce an emoji by its name but may have little or nothing to say about a sticker. If someone in your group uses assistive technology, an emoji with words is the most inclusive choice; the [emoji accessibility guide](/guides/emoji-accessibility-screen-readers/) has the details.

## A quick guide to choosing the right emoji for the chat

| Chat type | Works well | Use with care |
|---|---|---|
| Family group | 🙏 👍 ❤️ 🌹 🎂 😂 | 💀 😭 (as laughter) 🙃 |
| Close friends | Anything you all understand | Reactions on serious messages |
| Partner or crush | Replies with ❤️ 😊 😘 | Reaction-only responses |
| Work or client | 👍 ✅ 🙏 👏 | Hearts, winks, laughing reactions |
| School or neighborhood | 👍 🙏 ✅ | Jokes that need context |
| International | Faces, hearts | Hand gestures such as 👌 |

Before you send or react, three questions cover almost every case:

1. **Who can see this?** In a group, everyone can see every reaction and who made it.
2. **Will the oldest person in the chat read this emoji the way I mean it?** If not, pick the literal one.
3. **Does this message need words?** Questions, bad news, and long personal messages do. A reaction is for everything else.
