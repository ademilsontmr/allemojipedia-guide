The red heart means love, most often romantic love, and it is the only heart color with a meaning nearly everyone agrees on. Every other color is a way of adjusting that message: warmer or cooler, friendlier, sadder, more ironic, or matched to a team, a cause or a photo. The color tells the reader how much romance to assume.

Below is what each color and each heart variant signals, how the meaning shifts with the person you are sending it to, and the special cases where an app or a fandom has given a heart its own definition.

## Heart colors at a glance

| Heart | Usual meaning | Safe to send to |
|---|---|---|
| ❤️ Red | Love, deep affection, romance | Partner, family, close friends |
| 🩷 Pink | Sweet, light affection; a soft crush | Friends, partner, someone you are getting to know |
| 🧡 Orange | Warmth and encouragement with little romance | Friends, acquaintances |
| 💛 Yellow | Friendship, happiness, sunshine | Friends, family, coworkers you are close to |
| 💚 Green | Nature, health, growth, good luck | Friends, community and cause posts |
| 💙 Blue | Steady loyalty, calm support, trust | Friends, teammates, brands |
| 🩵 Light blue | Gentle calm, soft aesthetic | Friends, baby and winter posts |
| 💜 Purple | Admiration, glamour, fandom love | Friends, fellow fans |
| 🖤 Black | Dark humor, edge, mourning | Friends who share your humor; condolences |
| 🤍 White | Pure, quiet affection; sympathy | Anyone, including condolences |
| 🩶 Grey | Neutral, muted, low-key | Aesthetic use, understated support |
| 🤎 Brown | Warmth, earthiness, identity and solidarity | Friends, family, community |

Treat the table as the default. The sections below explain where the defaults bend.

## Red heart: the standard for love

The [red heart](/emojis/red-heart/) is the oldest heart on your keyboard. The underlying character entered Unicode in 1993 as a text symbol, long before color emoji fonts existed, which is why it sometimes appears as a small black or red glyph in plain-text environments.

It means love without qualification. Between partners it is "I love you." Between a parent and child, or between very close friends, it is the same statement in a different register. What it is not is casual. Sent to a new match after two days of chatting, it reads as moving fast. Sent to a coworker, it can feel like too much unless your workplace uses it as a routine reaction.

That last point matters because the red heart is the default "like" in many apps. Double-tapping a direct message on Instagram adds a red heart, and the heart is one of the Tapback reactions in Apple's Messages. A heart reaction is acknowledgment, closer to "nice" than to "I love you." A typed red heart in a message is a deliberate choice and carries more weight.

On WhatsApp, a red heart sent as a message by itself appears large and animated, pulsing like a heartbeat, which makes it feel more emphatic there than in other apps. The [WhatsApp guide](/guides/whatsapp-emoji-meanings/) has more on how single emojis behave.

## Pink hearts: plain pink and the decorated family

For years there was no simple pink heart. The plain [pink heart](/emojis/pink-heart/) arrived with Emoji 15.0 and is now the go-to for affection that is sweet instead of serious: thanking a friend, replying to a cute photo, a gentle signal to a crush that stops short of red.

The older pink hearts each have a decoration that changes the tone.

- **💕 [Two hearts](/emojis/two-hearts/).** Love is in the air. Very common between close friends, especially in comments such as "miss you 💕."
- **💖 [Sparkling heart](/emojis/sparkling-heart/).** Excited, glittery affection. Used for compliments and congratulations.
- **💗 Growing heart.** A heart getting bigger, for feelings that are swelling: pride, being touched, a growing crush.
- **💓 Beating heart.** A pulse. Excitement, nerves, a heart that is racing.
- **💞 Revolving hearts.** Two hearts circling each other; mutual affection, often for couples or best friends.
- **💘 Heart with arrow.** Cupid's arrow: being smitten, Valentine's Day, a crush.
- **💝 Heart with ribbon.** A gift of love, used for presents, Mother's Day and thank-yous.
- **💟 Heart decoration.** A white heart on a purple or pink tile. Purely ornamental, and rarely used in conversation.

As a group, the decorated pink hearts read as enthusiastic and affectionate but less heavy than red. Several of them in a row is a normal way for friends to gush.

## Orange and yellow: the friendship hearts

The [yellow heart](/emojis/yellow-heart/) is the friendship heart. It is sunny and cheerful, and carries no romantic expectation. It is a good fit for family group chats, a friend's good news, or a thank-you to someone you like but are not close enough to send red.

The [orange heart](/emojis/orange-heart/) sits between yellow and red. It shows care and encouragement, and because it is a step short of red, it has picked up a joking reputation online as the heart you send when you like someone as a friend. Most people who use it mean nothing so pointed. They like the color, or they are matching an autumn photo or a team.

One app-specific meaning is worth knowing. On Snapchat, a yellow heart next to a friend's name is not something anyone typed. It is a friend emoji the app assigns when you are each other's number one best friend. It turns into a red heart after two weeks and into two pink hearts after two months. The full system is explained in [Snapchat friend emojis](/guides/snapchat-friend-emojis-explained/).

## Green, blue, light blue and purple

**Green.** The [green heart](/emojis/green-heart/) is tied to the natural world: plants, hiking, healthy food, environmental causes, St. Patrick's Day. It can also hint at envy, borrowing the "green with jealousy" idiom, but that use is occasional and usually a joke.

**Blue.** The [blue heart](/emojis/blue-heart/) signals trust, loyalty and calm support. It is the least romantic of the saturated colors, which is why brands use it freely and why friends, especially male friends, often choose it over red. It is also common for water, sky and autism awareness posts.

**Light blue.** The light blue heart, added alongside pink and grey, is softer still. It shows up with winter scenes, baby announcements, and pastel feeds.

**Purple.** The [purple heart](/emojis/purple-heart/) has the widest range. In general use it suggests admiration and a bit of glamour. In fandom it belongs to BTS and their fans. In the United States, the Purple Heart is a military decoration awarded to service members wounded or killed in action, and the emoji is sometimes used to honor veterans. Which meaning applies is nearly always obvious from the conversation.

## Black, white, grey and brown

The neutral hearts are picked for mood more than for a specific feeling.

**Black.** The [black heart](/emojis/black-heart/) is affection in a dry, dark-humored voice. People who find red hearts sugary send black ones to say the same thing. It also fits a gothic or monochrome aesthetic, and it is used for grief. In an argument it can mean "my heart is cold toward you," but that reading needs supporting evidence in the words.

**White.** The [white heart](/emojis/white-heart/) is quiet and sincere. It is the most common heart in condolence messages because it expresses care without the passion of red. It is also popular in minimalist captions and wedding posts.

**Grey.** The [grey heart](/emojis/grey-heart/) is the most muted option. Some people use it for a neutral aesthetic, some for feeling flat or uncertain, and some because it suits a rainy-day photo. It has no established strong meaning yet.

**Brown.** The [brown heart](/emojis/brown-heart/) conveys warmth and earthiness: coffee, chocolate, autumn. It is also widely used to express love and solidarity within Black and Brown communities, and many people use it simply as the heart that matches their own skin tone.

## Hearts that show a state, not a color

Some heart emojis describe what is happening to the heart.

- **💔 [Broken heart](/emojis/broken-heart/).** Heartbreak, grief, disappointment. Also used lightly: "they discontinued my favorite snack 💔."
- **❤️‍🔥 [Heart on fire](/emojis/heart-on-fire/).** Burning passion or desire. Stronger and more physical than a plain red heart.
- **❤️‍🩹 [Mending heart](/emojis/mending-heart/).** A bandaged heart: healing, recovery, sympathy for someone going through something hard.
- **❣️ Heart exclamation.** A heart used as punctuation, for emphatic agreement or affection.
- **🫶 [Heart hands](/emojis/heart-hands/).** Two hands forming a heart. Gratitude, support and "love this," popular between friends and from creators to audiences.
- **💌 Love letter.** A sealed note with a heart; sending love, or literally a card or invitation.
- **🫀 Anatomical heart.** A realistic organ. Used for health topics, or as an ironic "my actual heart."

Heart on fire and mending heart were both introduced in Emoji 13.1. Technically each is a sequence that joins a red heart with a second emoji (fire, or an adhesive bandage), which is why an outdated device may show the two pieces side by side instead of one image.

## Who are you sending it to?

The same heart lands differently depending on the relationship. These are the conventions most people follow.

1. **A partner.** Red is the baseline. Heart on fire adds desire, and the pink family adds playfulness. Switching from red to a cooler color after a disagreement will be noticed.
2. **A crush or new match.** Start below red. A pink heart, a [smiling face with hearts](/emojis/smiling-face-with-hearts/), or a heart reaction on a message shows interest without declaring love. The guide to [flirty emojis](/guides/flirty-emojis-guide/) covers pacing.
3. **Close friends.** Anything goes, including red. Friends commonly send 💕, 🫶, yellow and each other's favorite colors.
4. **Family.** Red, yellow and white are all standard. Parents tend to send red hearts to everyone they love, with no hidden meaning.
5. **Coworkers and clients.** Prefer a heart reaction over a typed heart, and blue, yellow or green over red. In formal workplaces, skip hearts and use a thank-you or 🙏. See [emojis at work](/guides/emojis-at-work-etiquette/).
6. **Someone grieving.** White, black or a mending heart. Keep it to one, alongside real words.

## When a group gives a color its own meaning

General meanings give way whenever a community has claimed a color.

- **Fandoms.** Purple for BTS is the best-known example, and many other music fandoms have an official color that fans use as a heart in comments.
- **Sports.** Fans post hearts in team colors, often in pairs: 💙🤍, ❤️🖤, 💚💛. Here the hearts mean loyalty to the club and nothing about the color's usual tone.
- **Flags and identity.** Hearts lined up in sequence can spell out a flag. Six hearts in rainbow order echo the [rainbow flag](/emojis/rainbow-flag/), and light blue, pink and white echo the transgender flag. National colors work the same way.
- **Causes.** Awareness campaigns adopt colors, so a single heart in a bio or caption may point to a cause, such as green for environmental action.
- **Aesthetics.** Many people choose a heart to match the photo they are posting or the color scheme of their profile. A brown heart under a latte picture is about the latte.

If a heart color seems odd for the conversation, check for one of these before reading emotion into it. The broader guide to [emoji meanings in texting](/guides/emoji-meanings-in-texting/) explains how to weigh context against the emoji itself.

## Choosing the right heart: a short checklist

- Mean "I love you"? Send ❤️.
- Like them, but it is early? Send 🩷 or a heart reaction.
- Friend, and you want it to stay clearly friendly? Send 💛, 🧡 or 💙.
- Sympathy or condolences? Send 🤍 or ❤️‍🩹 with a real sentence.
- Dry humor or a dark aesthetic? Send 🖤.
- Matching a team, flag, cause or photo? Use that color and nobody will read romance into it.
- Unsure about a work contact? Use a reaction, or leave the heart out.

When you are on the receiving end, apply the same logic in reverse, and remember that many people pick their heart by favorite color. A habit is not a message. A change of habit sometimes is.
