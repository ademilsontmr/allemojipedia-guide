No, there is no seahorse emoji. Unicode, the standard that defines every emoji your phone can type, has never included one, and the latest release, Emoji 18.0 from 2026, did not add one either. If you remember sending a seahorse, you most likely sent a tropical fish, a dragon, a sticker or an image, and you are in very large company.

The rest of this guide explains where the memory comes from, why the question took off online in 2025, which emojis people use instead, and what it would take to get a real seahorse added.

## How to check for yourself

You do not have to take anyone's word for it. There are three quick ways to confirm that no seahorse exists.

1. **Search your emoji picker.** Type "seahorse" or "sea horse" in the search field of the iPhone, Android (Gboard or Samsung Keyboard), Mac or Windows emoji picker. You will get fish, horses or nothing, never a seahorse.
2. **Check the official list.** The Unicode Consortium publishes every emoji in its [full emoji list](https://unicode.org/emoji/charts/full-emoji-list.html). Search the page for "seahorse" and nothing comes up.
3. **Browse the animal category.** Our [animals and nature](/categories/animals-and-nature/) category includes every marine animal currently in the standard, from the 2010-era fish to the [orca added in Emoji 17.0](/guides/new-emojis-2026/). Our full emoji list, which includes Emoji 18.0, has no seahorse entry.

The Emoji 18.0 additions are a cracking face, leftward and rightward thumb signs, a monarch butterfly, a pickle, a lighthouse, a meteor, an eraser and a net with a handle. The net is the only one with a nautical feel, and it is not a seahorse.

## Why so many people remember a seahorse emoji

The seahorse is one of the best-known examples of what people online call a Mandela effect: a false memory shared by a large number of people. The name comes from the widespread belief that Nelson Mandela died in prison in the 1980s, when in fact he was released and later became president of South Africa. Other popular examples include the Monopoly man's monocle and the spelling of the Berenstain Bears.

A shared false memory is less mysterious than it sounds. Memory does not store perfect snapshots. It stores the gist and rebuilds the details when you recall them, filling gaps with what seems likely. Several things make a seahorse emoji seem very likely.

### The ocean section feels incomplete without one

The emoji set already contains a long list of sea life: [dolphin](/emojis/dolphin/), [shark](/emojis/shark/), [octopus](/emojis/octopus/), [crab](/emojis/crab/), [turtle](/emojis/turtle/), whales, a squid, a lobster, a shrimp, a jellyfish, coral and more. A seahorse is one of the first animals a child draws for an underwater scene. When you scroll past a row of sea creatures, your brain reasonably assumes the seahorse is in there somewhere, and later "remembers" it being there.

### Look-alike emojis

Several real emojis share one or two features with a seahorse, and at a glance, or in memory, they blend together:

- **Tropical fish 🐠** is bright, striped and clearly marine, the kind of reef animal a seahorse lives alongside.
- **Dragon 🐉** has the curled body, snout and ridged back of a seahorse. A seahorse even looks a bit like a tiny sea dragon, and a real animal called the leafy sea dragon is a close relative.
- **Horse face 🐴** supplies the head shape that gives the animal its name.
- **[Unicorn](/emojis/unicorn/) 🦄** is another horse-headed fantasy animal that sits in the same playful, pastel space.
- **Fish 🐟 and blowfish 🐡** fill out the same corner of the picker.

### Stickers, GIFs and clip art

Phones are full of seahorse images that are not emojis. Keyboard apps offer GIF and sticker search alongside the emoji tab, and typing "seahorse" there returns plenty of results. Messaging apps sell or bundle sticker packs with ocean themes. Word processors, presentation software and children's websites have used cartoon seahorse clip art for decades. Each of these sits inches away from real emojis on the screen, so it is easy to file a seahorse sticker under "emoji" in memory.

Google's [Emoji Kitchen](/guides/emoji-kitchen-guide/) adds another source of confusion. It blends two existing emojis into a new sticker image, so mashups involving fish, horses or dragons can produce creatures that look sea-horse-like. Those mashups are images sent as stickers. They are not characters in the Unicode standard and cannot be typed into a text field.

### Other people are just as sure

When you search the question and find thousands of posts from people who also remember a seahorse emoji, the shared certainty feels like evidence. It is not. A false memory that many people share is still false, and the posts themselves keep the belief going.

## The 2025 chatbot episode

In 2025 the seahorse question found a new audience when people started asking AI chatbots whether a seahorse emoji exists. Users shared screenshots of several popular assistants answering yes, then trying to produce the emoji and printing something else, such as a fish, a horse, a dragon or a unicorn. Some responses noticed the mistake, apologized, tried again and repeated the cycle several times before settling on an answer, and some never admitted that the emoji does not exist.

The episode spread because it looked like a machine having the same Mandela effect as people. There is a reasonable explanation that does not require anything spooky. Language models learn from large amounts of human writing, and the internet contains a lot of confident human statements about remembering a seahorse emoji. A model that has absorbed that belief will tend to repeat it, but when it tries to output the character, there is no seahorse character to output, so it reaches for the nearest real ones. Behavior varied between products and changed as companies updated their models, so results you get today may differ from the screenshots that went around.

The practical lesson is simple. For questions about whether a specific emoji exists, an emoji picker search or the Unicode list is a more reliable source than a chatbot, and so is a reference site that is generated from the Unicode data itself.

## What to send instead of a seahorse

Since you cannot type a seahorse, the goal is to get the idea across. Pick a substitute based on what the seahorse means in your message.

### Single-emoji stand-ins

- 🐠 **Tropical fish** for a cute, colorful ocean animal. This is the most common choice and the one most people will read as "sea life."
- 🐉 **[Dragon](/emojis/dragon/)** when you care about the curled shape. Readers who know the leafy sea dragon will get the joke.
- 🦄 **Unicorn** for a whimsical, magical horse when the sea part does not matter.

### Copy-ready combos

Combining two emojis is often clearer than any single one, because the reader assembles the meaning. For more ideas on building combos that read well, see our guide to [emoji combinations](/guides/emoji-combos-ideas/).

| Combo | What it says | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| 🐴🌊 | Sea horse, literally | The clearest stand-in in casual chat |
| 🐠🐴 | Fish-horse | Playful, a little absurd |
| 🐉🌊 | Little sea dragon | Closest to the real animal's shape |
| 🐴🫧 | Horse underwater | Bubbles add the water without a wave |
| 🐠🪸🐚 | Reef scene | Aquarium, snorkeling or beach posts |
| 🐴🌊❓ | "Where's the seahorse emoji?" | Joking about the Mandela effect |
| 🧜‍♀️🐴🌊 | Mermaid riding a sea horse | Fantasy or kids' party themes |

The [water wave](/emojis/water-wave/) 🌊 does a lot of work in these combos, because it turns almost any land animal into a sea version. A few [sparkles](/emojis/sparkles/) ✨ after the combo make it read as cute rather than literal.

### Images and stickers

If you need an actual picture of a seahorse, use the GIF or sticker tab in your keyboard, an Emoji Kitchen sticker if one fits, or a photo. These display as images, so they work in most chat apps but not in places that accept only text, such as usernames, many form fields, SMS on some phones, and email subject lines.

### Seahorse in a username or bio

Text-only fields cannot hold a sticker. In a profile name or bio, 🐴🌊 or 🐠 is the practical option. Avoid pasting a seahorse picture from a "fancy text" website: those sites sometimes offer symbols that are not emojis at all, and they may display as an empty box on other people's phones. Our guide to [why emojis show as boxes](/guides/why-emojis-show-as-boxes/) explains what happens when a device meets a character it cannot draw.

## How a seahorse emoji could be added

No seahorse has been approved by Unicode as of Emoji 18.0. The only way one would ever appear on phones is through Unicode's proposal process, which is open to anyone and is described in detail in our guide to [how new emojis get approved](/guides/how-new-emojis-get-approved/). In short:

1. **Submit a proposal** during Unicode's annual submission window, using the required format, with a sample image you have the rights to.
2. **Show evidence of use.** Unicode wants data that people search for and write about the concept often, compared with emojis that already exist. Search interest in "seahorse emoji" itself would be an interesting data point, though much of it comes from the false-memory question rather than from people who want to send one.
3. **Explain why existing emojis are not enough.** Reviewers ask whether the concept can already be expressed with current emojis or a combination. A proposer would need to argue that 🐠 and 🐴🌊 do not cover it.
4. **Wait for review and approval.** Accepted proposals become candidates and are formally approved in a Unicode release, usually in September.
5. **Wait for platforms.** Apple, Google, Samsung and Microsoft then draw their own designs and ship them in software updates over the following months.

From submission to your keyboard, the process usually takes about two years. Petitions and social media campaigns do not count as evidence under Unicode's guidelines, so viral interest on its own will not add the emoji. The Unicode Consortium's [emoji pages](https://home.unicode.org/emoji/) explain the current rules and submission dates.

If a seahorse were approved, it would sit alongside the other sea animals in the [animals and nature](/categories/animals-and-nature/) category, and older phones would show it as a box until they were updated.

## Why the myth will probably stick around

Even with the facts settled, the seahorse emoji question keeps coming back, for a few reasons:

- **New people discover it every year.** Each wave of curious searchers finds the same posts and screenshots, and some of them share their own memory of a seahorse.
- **The images keep existing.** Seahorse stickers, GIFs and clip art are still one tap away from the emoji tab.
- **The emoji set keeps growing.** New animals such as the jellyfish in 2022 and the orca in 2025 make it plausible that a seahorse was added at some point, so the claim never sounds absurd.
- **It is a fun argument.** "Is there a seahorse emoji?" is an easy, low-stakes debate that people enjoy having, with a clear answer at the end.

If someone insists they have seen one, the friendliest response is a combo: 🐴🌊 [🤷](/emojis/person-shrugging/). It makes the point and gives them something to send.

## Quick answer checklist

Use this the next time the question comes up in a group chat:

- **Does a seahorse emoji exist?** No, not in any version of Unicode through Emoji 18.0 (2026).
- **Was it ever removed?** No. Unicode does not remove emojis, and a seahorse was never added.
- **Why do I remember one?** Look-alike emojis, seahorse stickers and clip art, and a widely shared false memory.
- **Why did chatbots get it wrong?** They repeat a belief that appears often in human writing, then cannot output a character that does not exist.
- **What should I send instead?** 🐠 on its own, or 🐴🌊 for a clear sea horse.
- **Can it be added?** Only through a successful Unicode proposal, which takes about two years.

When in doubt about any emoji, search your phone's emoji picker first. If it is not there, and your phone is up to date, it almost certainly does not exist yet.
