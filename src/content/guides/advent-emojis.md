There is no Advent wreath emoji or Advent candle emoji, so people build the wreath themselves: one 🕯️ for each Sunday, a bit of green such as 🌲 or 🌿, and colored hearts for the candles, 💜 for violet and 🩷 for the rose candle of the third Sunday. In 2026, Advent begins on Sunday, November 29, so the first line to post is something like 🌿🕯️💜 "First Sunday of Advent."

This guide covers what Advent is, how to build the wreath in emoji, what the four candles are commonly said to mean, the O Antiphons of the last week, and ready-to-post captions for each Sunday. The broader set of faith symbols is in [Christian emojis and their meanings](/guides/christian-emojis-meanings/), and the manger scene is in [nativity emojis](/guides/nativity-emojis/).

## What Advent is, and the 2026 dates

Advent is the season of preparation before Christmas in Western Christianity, observed by Catholics, Anglicans, Lutherans, Methodists, and many other churches. The name comes from the Latin *adventus*, "coming." The season looks two ways at once: back to the birth of Jesus at Bethlehem and forward to his return at the end of time. For Catholics and several other churches, the first Sunday of Advent also begins a new liturgical year.

Advent starts on the fourth Sunday before Christmas, so its length changes each year from 22 to 28 days. In 2026 it is 26 days long. If you want the full calendar and how the start date is worked out, see this explanation of [when Advent 2026 begins](https://guidecatholic.com/blog/advent-2026-start-date-when-does-advent-begin/).

| Week | Sunday in 2026 | Candles lit | Emoji line |
|---|---|---|---|
| First Sunday of Advent | November 29 | 1 violet | 🕯️💜 |
| Second Sunday of Advent | December 6 | 2 violet | 🕯️🕯️💜💜 |
| Third Sunday (Gaudete) | December 13 | 2 violet, 1 rose | 🕯️🕯️🕯️💜💜🩷 |
| Fourth Sunday of Advent | December 20 | all 4 | 🕯️🕯️🕯️🕯️💜💜🩷💜 |
| Christmas | Friday, December 25 | 4, plus a white center candle in some homes | 🕯️🤍🌟 |

Because Christmas falls on a Friday this year, the fourth week of Advent is unusually short: Sunday, December 20, through Thursday, December 24. Christmas Eve Masses and services begin on the evening of the 24th, which is when the season turns.

Eastern Orthodox and Eastern Catholic churches keep a longer pre-Christmas period called the Nativity Fast, which starts on November 15 for churches on the Gregorian calendar. Its customs differ from the Western wreath, so this guide focuses on the Western season.

## The Advent wreath in emoji

The Advent wreath is a circle of evergreen branches holding four candles, with one more candle lit each Sunday. It is commonly traced to Johann Hinrich Wichern, a Lutheran pastor in Hamburg, who in 1839 used a cartwheel of candles to count down the days to Christmas with the children in his care. The four-candle evergreen version spread through Germany and then to Catholic and Protestant homes worldwide. The evergreen is usually read as a sign of life that does not die, and the circle as a sign of eternity.

Unicode has no wreath, so the emoji version is assembled from parts:

- **The candles:** the [candle](/emojis/candle/) 🕯️, repeated once per week. This is the one emoji everyone recognizes as Advent when it is counted.
- **The greenery:** 🌲 evergreen tree, 🌿 herb, or 🍃 leaves. The [Christmas tree](/emojis/christmas-tree/) 🎄 works visually but many people save it for Christmas itself.
- **The candle colors:** the [purple heart](/emojis/purple-heart/) 💜 for violet, the [pink heart](/emojis/pink-heart/) 🩷 for rose, and the [white heart](/emojis/white-heart/) 🤍 for the Christ candle.
- **The circle:** ⭕ (hollow red circle) can frame the line, though most people skip it because it adds red, which is not an Advent color.

The candle emoji itself is drawn white or cream on most platforms, so a row of 🕯️ cannot show which candle is violet and which is rose. That is why the hearts sit next to the candles. Two common layouts:

1. **Count-up line:** candles for the weeks so far, then the color of this week's candle. Third Sunday: 🕯️🕯️🕯️🩷.
2. **Full wreath with progress:** all four colors shown, with candles only over the lit ones. Second Sunday: 🌿 💜🕯️ 💜🕯️ 🩷 💜 🌿.

The count-up line is easier to read on a phone and survives being copied between apps. Keep it to one line; emoji wrapping across two lines breaks the picture.

If your family or parish blesses the wreath on the first Sunday, these [Advent wreath prayers and blessing](https://guidecatholic.com/blog/advent-wreath-prayers-blessing-catholic-guide/) give the words for each week, and the emoji line can go at the end of the post with the prayer in text.

## The colors of Advent: violet, rose, and blue

Color carries much of the meaning in Advent emoji, so it helps to know where each comes from.

**Violet.** The color of the season in the Catholic Church and many Protestant churches. Vestments and altar cloths are violet, and three of the four wreath candles usually are too. In emoji: 💜, 🟣, or 🟪. The heart is the friendliest; the circle and square look more like a color swatch, which suits a parish calendar graphic.

**Rose.** On the third Sunday, Gaudete Sunday, Catholic priests may wear rose vestments, and the wreath's third candle is rose or pink. The pink heart 🩷 is the standard stand-in. Unicode has no pink circle or square, so 🩷 is the only plain pink color block. It arrived in Emoji 15.0 (2022); if a reader's phone shows a box, the guide on [why emojis show as boxes](/guides/why-emojis-show-as-boxes/) explains the fix.

**Blue.** Some Lutheran, Anglican, Episcopal, and Methodist churches use blue for Advent, often described as the color of hope or of the night sky before dawn. Their wreaths may have four blue candles. In emoji: the [blue heart](/emojis/blue-heart/) 💙, 🔵, or 🟦. Blue is also associated with Mary, which is why 💙 shows up in posts for the Immaculate Conception on December 8, a Catholic feast that falls in Advent.

**White.** Many wreaths have a white Christ candle in the center, lit on Christmas Eve or Christmas Day. 🤍 marks it in emoji, and white is the liturgical color of the Christmas season that follows.

For what each heart color signals outside church, see [heart emoji colors and their meanings](/guides/heart-emoji-colors-meaning/). Outside a religious context, 💜 can read as affection or a fandom color, which is another reason to put words like "Advent" beside it.

## The four candles: hope, peace, joy, love

Many churches, devotional booklets, and family guides give each candle a theme. The most widespread set is hope, peace, joy, and love, but traditions vary, so treat the table below as common practice, not a universal rule.

| Sunday | Common theme | Other names you may see | Emoji to pair |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Hope | Prophecy candle, Prophets' candle | 🕯️💜🌅 or 🕯️💜🌱 |
| Second | Peace | Bethlehem candle, sometimes Love | 🕯️🕯️💜🕊️ |
| Third (Gaudete) | Joy | Shepherds' candle | 🕯️🕯️🕯️🩷✨ |
| Fourth | Love | Angels' candle, sometimes Peace | 🕯️🕯️🕯️🕯️💜❤️ |

Some notes on these pairings:

- **Hope:** the [sunrise](/emojis/sunrise/) 🌅 suggests light breaking in, and the [seedling](/emojis/seedling/) 🌱 suggests something small that will grow. Both fit the first week's readings, which in many churches speak of staying awake and watching.
- **Peace:** the [dove](/emojis/dove/) 🕊️ is the familiar peace symbol and also a Christian symbol of the Holy Spirit.
- **Joy:** sparkles ✨ or 🎶 keep the third week bright without jumping to party emojis.
- **Love:** a red heart ❤️ or 🤍 works; some families use 🫶 heart hands for a warmer tone.

The Catholic liturgy itself does not assign official themes to the four candles. Joy on the third Sunday is the firmest link, because it comes from the Mass: the entrance antiphon for that day begins "Gaudete in Domino semper," "Rejoice in the Lord always," from Paul's letter to the Philippians. That opening word gives Gaudete Sunday its name.

## The O Antiphons: December 17 to 23

In the last days of Advent, from December 17 to December 23, the Church sings a series of seven short prayers called the O Antiphons. Each one is sung with the Magnificat at Evening Prayer (Vespers) and calls on Christ by a title drawn from the Old Testament. They are also used as the Gospel acclamation at Catholic Mass on those days, and the hymn "O Come, O Come, Emmanuel" is a paraphrase of them.

Read backward, the first letters of the Latin titles spell *ERO CRAS*, which is usually translated "Tomorrow I will be there." Each title has a natural emoji:

| Date | Antiphon | Title | Emoji |
|---|---|---|---|
| December 17 | O Sapientia | O Wisdom | 📖 or 💡 |
| December 18 | O Adonai | O Lord, who appeared in the burning bush | 🔥 |
| December 19 | O Radix Jesse | O Root of Jesse | 🌱 |
| December 20 | O Clavis David | O Key of David | 🔑 |
| December 21 | O Oriens | O Dayspring, O Rising Dawn | 🌅 |
| December 22 | O Rex Gentium | O King of the Nations | 👑 |
| December 23 | O Emmanuel | O Emmanuel, God with us | 👶🤍 |

The [key](/emojis/key/) 🔑 for the Key of David and the [crown](/emojis/crown/) 👑 for the King of the Nations are the most immediately readable. The [open book](/emojis/open-book/) 📖 works for Wisdom, or the light bulb 💡 if you want something lighter. In 2026, December 20 is both the Fourth Sunday of Advent and the day of O Clavis David, so a parish post that day can combine them: "Fourth Sunday of Advent 🕯️🕯️🕯️🕯️ O Key of David, come 🔑"

A daily O Antiphon series makes a good week of posts for a parish account: the title, the emoji, and one line of the antiphon in your missal's translation.

## Weekday Advent posts and family ideas

Not every post needs to be a Sunday. A few patterns that work through the week:

1. **The countdown.** The [hourglass](/emojis/hourglass-not-done/) ⏳ or 📅 with the number of days to Christmas. "18 days ⏳🕯️" reads at a glance.
2. **The Advent calendar.** Families who open a door a day can post 📅🍫 or 📅🍪 with the day number. Advent calendars often start on December 1, not on the first Sunday, so do not be surprised when the two do not line up.
3. **Marian feasts.** December 8, the Immaculate Conception, and December 12, Our Lady of Guadalupe, both fall in Advent. 💙🙏 or 🌹 with the feast name fits either; for Guadalupe many Mexican and Mexican-American Catholics also use roses 🌹, after the account of the roses Juan Diego gathered in his tilma.
4. **The road to Bethlehem.** Some families move Mary and Joseph across the room toward the empty stable through Advent. In emoji: 🫏🤰🧔 then a few more steps of 🛣️ or 🏜️ each week, with 🛖 waiting at the end. The [donkey](/emojis/donkey/) 🫏 is from 2022, so it can appear as a box on older phones.
5. **Prayer requests.** [Folded hands](/emojis/folded-hands/) 🙏 with 🕯️ is the standard way to say "praying for you" in any season, and fits Advent naturally.

Short combos to keep handy:

- 🕯️💜🙏 quiet Advent prayer
- 🌿🕯️🕯️🕯️🕯️🌿 the full wreath
- ⏳🌟 waiting for the star
- 🌅🌱 hope, new beginnings
- 📖🕯️ evening reading by candlelight

For more on stringing emojis into a readable sequence, the guide to [emoji combos](/guides/emoji-combos-ideas/) has general rules that apply here too.

## Dos and don'ts for Advent posts

Advent is a short season with a distinct mood, and the commonest mistake is treating it as early Christmas. The secular holiday season starts after [Thanksgiving](/guides/thanksgiving-emojis/) and [Black Friday](/guides/black-friday-emojis/), and that is fine for shopping posts, but church and family faith posts usually keep the waiting. For a full overview of the season's customs and readings, this [complete Catholic guide to Advent](https://guidecatholic.com/blog/complete-catholic-advent-guide/) is a good companion.

**Do:**

- Count the candles correctly for the week. One candle on the first Sunday, two on the second, and so on.
- Put the rose 🩷 on the third Sunday, not the fourth.
- Write the words. "First Sunday of Advent 🕯️" is clear; 🕯️💜 alone could mean a cozy evening or a remembrance.
- Match your church's colors. If your congregation uses blue, use 💙.
- Keep lines short. A screen reader announces each emoji by name, so four candles become "candle, candle, candle, candle." That is acceptable; twenty symbols are not.

**Don't:**

- Post 👶 in the manger before Christmas Eve if you are writing for a parish. The empty manger is part of the point.
- Say "Merry Christmas" on the First Sunday of Advent in a religious post. "Blessed Advent" or "Happy Advent" fits.
- Use 🎉 or 🥳 for Gaudete. It is joy, but quiet joy; ✨ and 🎶 suit it better.
- Treat Advent like Lent. The season has a penitential note, but its mood is expectant, so the [Latin cross](/emojis/latin-cross/) ✝️ is less typical than the candle and the star.
- Mix 🎅 into wreath lines. Santa and the Advent wreath are both December, but many Christians prefer to keep them apart.
